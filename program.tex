\section*{Scope and endpoint}

The seminar develops derived manifolds from the elementary problem of
nontransverse intersections to modern representability results for moduli
spaces of solutions to nonlinear elliptic equations. It is intended to be
accessible to first-year PhD students from several areas. We will therefore
organize the material around recurring examples, universal properties, and a
small number of explicit takeaways.

Each talk lasts 90 minutes. Substantial working manuscript material exists for
all fourteen dates, but the chapter boundaries remain revisable during the
program-wide consolidation pass. The newly assigned bordism interlude has now
been written as Chapter~6 of the working manuscript.

The principal endpoint is Steffens's relative elliptic representability
theorem. In a condensed form, it says that if $S$ is a smooth stack,
$M\to S$ is a proper $S$-family of manifolds, and $\mathcal E$ is an
$S$-family of elliptic differential moduli problems over $M$, then
\[
  \operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)\longrightarrow S
\]
is represented by quasi-smooth derived \Cinfty-schemes locally of finite
presentation.

Our goal is to understand why this statement has exactly this form and how its
proof combines three engines:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Derived geometry supplies pullbacks, tangent complexes, and local
    derived zero loci.
  \item Fredholm analysis supplies finite-dimensional reductions and
    obstruction spaces.
  \item Descent and locality of representability turn the local reductions
    into an intrinsic global moduli object.
\end{enumerate}
We will not attempt complete proofs of every technical ingredient. By the end,
participants should be able to trace the proof architecture, identify the
deformation and obstruction spaces at a solution, and explain why
representability remains logically separate from compactification,
orientations, and virtual fundamental classes.

\section*{Program}

\Talk{1}{13 October}{Why derived manifolds?}
{See the geometric and analytic problems before developing the formalism.}
{Prove the transverse fiber product theorem and compare a transverse
intersection of plane curves with the tangent line--parabola intersection.
Explain what the underlying zero set forgets about its defining equation. Use
a family of two-term complexes to show why kernels alone do not form a stable
geometric object. End with the local finite-dimensional reduction of an
elliptic equation as a quoted preview and formulate the nonuniqueness and
coherence problem for the resulting zero-locus charts.}
{Spivak, \emph{Derived smooth manifolds}, Section~1; Steffens,
\emph{Derived differential geometry}, Chapter~1, especially
Section~1.0.1; Pardon, \emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm
analysis}, Sections~1--2.}

\Talk{2}{20 October}{Smooth geometry through \Cinfty-rings}
{Learn the algebraic structure which remembers every smooth operation.}
{Define a \Cinfty-ring as a finite-product-preserving functor from Euclidean
spaces and smooth maps to sets. Prove that $\Cinfty(\mathbb R^n)$ is free on
$n$ generators. Study quotients and the different equations which can define
the same underlying point. Use Borel's theorem to illustrate the difference
between smooth and polynomial algebra. Prove the closed-subset case of the
full-faithfulness theorem needed for manifolds.}
{Moerdijk--Reyes, \emph{Models for smooth infinitesimal analysis},
Chapter~I, Section~1, especially Proposition~1.1, Theorem~1.3, and
Proposition~1.5.}

\Talk{3}{27 October}{Manifolds as \Cinfty-rings}
{Recover the part of differential geometry needed for the universal
construction of derived manifolds.}
{Prove the regular-equation lemma and use it to show that $\Cinfty(M)$ is
finitely presented for every manifold $M$. Compute products algebraically.
Prove locally that transverse inverse images become pushouts of
\Cinfty-rings, explain the descent step, and conclude that transverse fiber
products become pushouts. Return to the line--parabola example to mark the
boundary of this theorem.}
{Moerdijk--Reyes, Chapter~I, Section~2, especially Lemma~2.1,
Theorem~2.3, Propositions~2.5--2.6, and Theorem~2.8.}

\Talk{4}{3 November}{From ordinary to derived smooth objects}
{Understand why the universal enlargement of manifolds by arbitrary
pullbacks is an $\infty$-category.}
{First identify the opposite category of finitely presented ordinary
\Cinfty-rings as the finite-limit envelope appropriate to ordinary
categories. Show that it contains ordinary singular zero loci but collapses a
repeated relation in a self-intersection. Use a self-pullback of a point in
animae to show how pullbacks retain equality data. Introduce animated
\Cinfty-rings and state the animated finite-limit-envelope theorem. Apply the
Carchedi--Steffens comparison theorem to obtain the universal property and
algebraic presentation of derived manifolds.}
{Carchedi--Steffens, \emph{On the universal property of derived manifolds},
Definition~2.1, Theorem~3.22, Remark~3.26, Lemma~5.1,
Corollary~5.2, Theorem~5.3, and Corollary~5.4.}

\Talk{5}{10 November}{Calculating derived intersections}
{Calculate the pullbacks constructed in Talk~4 and see exactly what their
derived structure remembers.}
{Define a derived zero locus as a pullback and calculate its animated
\Cinfty-ring using a Koszul presentation. Introduce the smooth Dold--Kan
bridge only as a computational tool. Compare the equations $x$, $x^2$, and the
zero map, carefully distinguishing the underlying set, classical truncation,
and positive homotopy groups. Extend the calculation to the zero locus of a
vector-bundle section and ask how its class compares with the ordinary
manifold obtained from a transverse perturbation.}
{Steffens, \emph{Derived differential geometry},
Examples~4.1.5.10--4.1.5.11 and Construction~5.1.0.1; Spivak,
\emph{Derived smooth manifolds}, Sections~1 and~7.}

\Talk{6}{17 November}{Derived bordism and fundamental classes}
{Turn compact quasi-smooth derived intersections into cobordism classes, and identify precisely
what this does and does not provide for moduli problems.}
{Begin with a compact derived zero locus $X=Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)$ of a
vector-bundle section. Perturb $s$ transversely and explain why a homotopy from
$s$ to the perturbation defines a derived cobordism from $X$ to an ordinary
manifold. Define derived cobordism using proper collared families over
$\mathbb R$. Quote the embedding and global zero-locus theorems for compact
quasi-smooth derived manifolds, and use them to extend the perturbation argument to the
comparison isomorphism
\[
  \Omega(T) \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } \Omega^{\mathrm{der}}(T).
\]
Deduce the intersection formula
\[
  [A]\smile[B]=[A\mathbin{\times^{\mathrm{der}}_M}B]
\]
without a transversality hypothesis, and use the derived self-intersection of
the zero section of a vector bundle as the main example, recovering its Euler
class. Briefly explain the stable normal-bundle formulation of orientations
and how a map from~$MO$ extends compatible manifold fundamental classes to
compact quasi-smooth derived manifolds. End by observing that bordism retains less than the
full derived object and motivate the stable linearization studied next.}
{Spivak, \emph{Derived smooth manifolds}, Definition~2.1(7),
Proposition~3.3, Definitions~3.5--3.6, Theorem~3.12, and
Corollary~3.13, together with the discussion of fundamental classes in
Section~1; Carchedi--Steffens, Theorem~5.10, for comparison with the modern
framework; Joyce,
\emph{D-manifolds and d-orbifolds}, Section~15, for comparison with the
orbifold and virtual-class setting.}

\Talk{7}{24 November}{Stable linearization and tangent complexes}
{Extract the deformation and obstruction information which derived bordism
does not see.}
{Motivate stable modules by explaining why kernels and cokernels belong to one
linear object. Give a brief heuristic comparison between $\mathbb S$-modules
and $\mathbb Z$-modules, then define stable modules over an animated
\Cinfty-ring through its underlying commutative ring spectrum. Introduce
square-zero extensions and characterize the cotangent complex by derivations.
Use base change and transitivity to prove the tangent-complex formula
\[
  [T_xU\longrightarrow E_x]
\]
for the derived zero locus of a section $s\colon U\to E$. Interpret its
cohomology as deformation and obstruction spaces, compute the virtual
dimension, and introduce affine quasi-smoothness.}
{Steffens, \emph{Derived differential geometry}, Remark~5.1.0.4,
Proposition~5.1.1.11, and Corollary~5.1.1.13; Spivak,
\emph{Derived smooth manifolds}, Section~7, especially
Corollaries~7.3--7.6.}

\Talk{8}{1 December}{Local derived geometry and Kuranishi charts}
{Pass from affine calculations to local geometric objects and understand why
their finite-dimensional presentations are nonunique.}
{Introduce only the localization and spectrum language needed to define
derived \Cinfty-schemes locally of finite presentation. Discuss open
restriction, gluing, truncation, and quasi-smoothness as local notions. Then
interpret a Kuranishi chart $(U,E,s)$ as a local presentation of a
quasi-smooth derived object. Study shrinking and stabilization, using
\[
  x\longmapsto x^2,
  \qquad
  (x,y)\longmapsto(x^2,y)
\]
as the principal comparison. Explain what changes of presentation must
preserve and end with the resulting coherence problem.}
{Steffens, \emph{Derived differential geometry}, Chapter~1 and
Section~4.1.3; Borisov, \emph{Derived manifolds and Kuranishi models},
Introduction and Section~3.3; Joyce, \emph{Kuranishi spaces as a 2-category},
Sections~1 and~4.}

\Talk{9}{8 December}{Stacks and local representability}
{See how intrinsic moduli functors replace a manually chosen coherent
Kuranishi atlas.}
{Write down coordinate changes on double overlaps, homotopies on triple
overlaps, and the next compatibility on quadruple overlaps. Explain what the
\v{C}ech nerve of an already given derived object records, without asserting a
general strictification theorem for arbitrary Kuranishi data. Then introduce a
moduli functor of families, a representing object with its universal family,
and the locality of representability. Use the derived zero locus of a section
as the main example: maps into it are maps into the ambient manifold together
with a chosen equality between the section and zero.}
{Steffens, \emph{Representability of elliptic moduli problems},
Section~1.0.2, Proposition~2.1.48, and Corollary~2.1.49; Pardon,
\emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm analysis},
Introduction; Steffens, \emph{Derived differential geometry}, Chapter~1, for
the coherence problem.}

\Talk{10}{15 December}{Section stacks and derived solution stacks}
{Construct the intrinsic moduli functor whose representability will later be
proved.}
{For a family $W\to M\to S$, define the stack of sections after arbitrary
base change. Explain why a finite-order differential operator is naturally a
map involving relative jets, while treating the technical jet and
Weil-restriction constructions as quoted machinery. Define an elliptic
differential moduli problem and its solution stack as a pullback of section
stacks. Identify the tangent complex at a solution with the complex induced by
the linearized differential operator. Use
\[
  P(u)=\Delta u+u^2
\]
on a closed connected Riemannian manifold as the running example, and isolate
the analytic input still missing.}
{Steffens, \emph{Representability of elliptic moduli problems},
Definition~3.2.1, Proposition~3.2.22, Definition~3.3.7,
Proposition~3.3.9, and Definition~3.4.2; Pardon,
\emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm analysis}, Section~4.}

\Talk{11}{12 January}{Fredholm equations and finite-dimensional reduction}
{Turn one infinite-dimensional elliptic equation into a finite-dimensional
derived zero-locus model.}
{Explain why Sobolev completion turns the elliptic linearization into a
Fredholm operator. Choose a finite-dimensional obstruction space mapping
isomorphically to its cokernel, apply the Banach implicit function theorem to
the augmented problem, and use elliptic regularity to recover smooth
solutions. Carry out the reduction for $\Delta u+u^2=0$ near $u=0$: split into
constant and mean-zero functions, obtain the obstruction map $a\mapsto a^2$
exactly, and compare its tangent complex with
$[\mathbb R\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb R]$. End by identifying the gap between a
chosen Kuranishi model and representability of the intrinsic solution stack.}
{Steffens, \emph{Representability of elliptic moduli problems},
Section~1.0.1, Lemmas~3.4.5--3.4.6, and the analytic part of the proof of
Theorem~3.4.3; Pardon, \emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm
analysis}, Section~2 and the first step of the proof sketch of Theorem~5.1.}

\Talk{12}{19 January}{Elliptic representability I: the smooth-to-derived bridge}
{Explain why Sobolev analysis proves an intrinsic submersiveness statement in
derived smooth geometry.}
{State Steffens's relative elliptic representability theorem as the
destination. Localize on the smooth-stack base, pass to vector-bundle charts
for the relevant section stacks, and reduce to one nonlinear PDE over a fixed
compact manifold. Construct a functorial Sobolev tower and compatible
obstruction augmentation. Test the augmented smooth operator after pullback
from an ordinary manifold, then use nonlinear and linear elliptic regularity
to prove that the resulting tower is eventually locally constant. Conclude
that the augmented operator is submersive after passage to derived geometry.
Stop before forming its finite-dimensional derived zero locus.}
{Steffens, \emph{Representability of elliptic moduli problems in derived
\Cinfty-geometry}, Proposition~2.2.18, Proposition~3.2.22,
Lemma~3.4.4, Theorem~3.4.3, and Lemmas~3.4.5--3.4.6.}

\Talk{13}{26 January}{Elliptic representability II: derived charts and globalization}
{Turn the derived-submersive augmented equation into local
finite-dimensional charts and complete the representability theorem.}
{Form the ordinary finite-dimensional zero fiber of the augmented operator,
project it to the obstruction space, and recover the original problem as a
finite-dimensional derived zero locus. Identify this object with an open
restriction of the intrinsic solution stack by pasting pullback squares,
compare its tangent complex with the elliptic deformation-obstruction complex,
and prove quasi-smoothness. Vary the solution and apply the effective
open-atlas criterion to complete Steffens's theorem. End with a concise
comparison with Pardon's alternate route through ordinary regularity, proper
section stacks on derived tests, and finite-dimensional parameter thickening.
Treat the published argument as a proof sketch rather than as a second full
proof.}
{Steffens, \emph{Representability of elliptic moduli problems in derived
\Cinfty-geometry}, Proposition~2.1.48, Corollary~2.1.49,
Theorem~3.4.3, and Remark~3.4.7; Pardon, \emph{Representability in non-linear
elliptic Fredholm analysis}, Sections~4--5, especially Theorem~5.1,
Proposition~5.2, and Lemma~5.3.}

\Talk{14}{2 February}{Pseudo-holomorphic curves as a derived moduli problem}
{See the complete representability architecture operate on its principal
geometric application.}
{Describe families of maps from smooth compact Riemann surfaces to an almost
complex manifold and formulate the Cauchy--Riemann equation as a finite-order
differential moduli problem. Identify its linearization as a Cauchy--Riemann
type operator and prove ellipticity by calculating the principal symbol. Write
the relative deformation-obstruction complex and interpret its cohomology.
Retain families and automorphisms through the smooth-stack base, then apply
Talks~12--13 to obtain relative representability. End the seminar by separating
this smooth-domain result from compactification by nodal curves, orientations,
and the construction of virtual classes, recalling the precise contribution
of the bordism interlude.}
{Wendl, \emph{Lectures on holomorphic curves in symplectic and contact
geometry}, Sections~2.1, 2.3--2.4, and 3.3--3.4; Steffens,
\emph{Representability of elliptic moduli problems}, Section~4; Pardon,
\emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm analysis}, Section~4.}
