\section*{Goals}

The main goal of this seminar is to introduce the modern theory of derived manifolds, an elegant technical solution to various difficulties present in the classical theory of manifolds surrounding non-tranverse intersections. In the second half of the seminar we will present one application of derived manifolds: they are the correct home for moduli spaces of solutions to nonlinear elliptic partial differential equations. Such moduli spaces are of fundamental importance in much of modern geometric topology, such as symplectic topology, Floer theory, complex geometry and the theory of 4-manifolds. In each case, studying these moduli spaces leads to extremely interesting invariants. Specifically, our second goal will be to present an overview of a recent theorem of Pelle Steffens, which explains how to view such moduli spaces as derived manifolds: they end up being defined as solutions to natural moduli problems associated to the elliptic differential equation. We will not attempt to discuss complete proofs of every technical ingredient going into this theorem. Instead, we hope that by the end, participants will be ready to dig into the source material themselves.

The seminar is intended to be accessible to first-year PhD students joining the SFB. We have attempted to keep the seminar as self-contained and self-motivated as possible. In particular we do not suppose intimate knowledge of the theory of $\infty$-categories. Instead we hope to motivate it as a natural and indispensible language with which to discuss derived manifolds.  Nevertheless, the difficulty of talks does ramp up as we get to the later weeks of the program.

Each talk should last 90 minutes. The program below is a suggestion, but can be adapted as the semester progresses according to the needs and interests of the participants.

\section*{Program}

\Talk{1}{13 October}{Why derived manifolds?}
{Motivate the theory of derived manifolds}
{Prove that pullbacks of transverse intersection exist in the category of manifolds. Discuss the issues with understanding non-transverse intersection. As a running example, compare the zero-set of a line with the zero-set of a parabola. Using more examples, explain how remembering the differential as a two-term complex solves some, but not all, problems. This shows that the correct home for such intersections is necessarily ``derived''. With more examples, such as the zero-sets of $x^2$ vs $x^3$, explain how this is still not enough to fully capture the geometry of non-tranverse intersections. Give an informal discussion of the universal property of derived manifolds, as a maximal way to encode such non-transerve intersections.
	
End with giving an introduction to moduli spaces of elliptic equations. Discuss the local finite-dimensional reduction of an
elliptic equation, which allows one to present such moduli spaces locally as non-transverse intersections. Formulate the nonuniqueness and coherence problem for the resulting local presentations, again motivating derived manifolds.}
{\textcite[Section~1]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds};
\textcite[Chapter~1, especially Section~1.0.1]{Steffens_Derived_Differential_Geometry};
\textcite[Sections~1--2]{Pardon_Representability_Elliptic_Fredholm}.}

\Talk{2}{20 October}{Introduction to \Cinfty-rings}
{Familiarize ourselves with the algebraic structure used to generalize manifolds.}
{Define \Cinfty-rings and introduce their basic categorical properties. Prove that $\Cinfty(\mathbb R^n)$ is free on
$n$ generators. Prove Hadamard's lemma and discuss quotients of \Cinfty-rings. Discuss Borel's theorem. Define $\Cinfty(X)$ for $X\subset \mathbb R^n$ an arbitrary closed subset. Prove that the resulting functor from closed subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\Cinfty$-rings is fully faithful.}
{\textcite[Chapter~I, Section~1]{Moerdijk_Reyes_Smooth_Infinitesimal_Analysis}.}

\Talk{3}{27 October}{\Cinfty-rings as generalized manifolds}
{Understand how \Cinfty-rings function as generalized manifolds.}
{Introduce coproducts of $\Cinfty$-rings. Prove Lemma 2.1 in Moerdijk--Reyes. Deduce that $\Cinfty(M)$ is finitely presented for every manifold $M$. Prove that $\Cinfty(M\times N) \simeq \Cinfty(M) \otimes \Cinfty(N)$. Prove that transverse pullbacks in manifolds induce pushouts of $\Cinfty$-rings.}
{\textcite[Chapter~I, Section~2]{Moerdijk_Reyes_Smooth_Infinitesimal_Analysis}.}

\Talk{4}{3 November}{From generalized manifolds to derived manifolds}
{Understand why the universal enlargement of manifolds by arbitrary pullbacks should be an $\infty$-category.}
{Formulate the 1-categorical universal property of finitely presented $\Cinfty$-rings. Use the results of the last talk to sketch a proof. Briefly introduce $\infty$-categories and homotopy pullbacks. Motivate the passage to $\infty$-categories for the theory of derived manifolds via abstract considerations and examples such as self-intersections. Discuss the process of ``animation''. Define derived manifolds via the $\infty$-categorical analogue of the previous universal property. State the theorem of Carchedi--Steffens identifying them with finitely presented derived $\Cinfty$-rings. Discuss as much of the proof as time permits.}
{\textcite{Carchedi_Steffens_Universal_Derived_Manifolds}.}

\Talk{5}{10 November}{Calculating derived intersections}
{Learn to calculate derived zero loci via explicit dg-algebra models}
{Introduce \Cinfty-dg-algebras, state the \Cinfty-Dold--Kan correspondence, and explain how it identifies derived zero loci with homotopy pushouts of \Cinfty-dg-algebras. Construct the Koszul model for the derived zero locus of a smooth map $f\colon\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^r$ by resolving the evaluation map $\Cinfty(\mathbb R^r)\to\mathbb R$. Explain the extension to families. Give examples, such as Spivak's Example~2.7 and the zero loci of the equations $x$, $x^2$, and $0$, distinguishing the underlying zero set, classical truncation, and higher homotopy groups of the animated ring of functions.}
{\textcite[Definition~3.3.1, Theorem~3.3.12, and Examples~3.3.16--3.3.17]{Steffens_Derived_Cinfty_Geometry_I};
\textcite[Example~2.7]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.}

\Talk{6}{17 November}{Tangent and cotangent complexes}
{Learn about (co)tangent complexes of derived manifolds.}
{Define $\Cinfty$-derivations, the module of smooth K\"ahler differentials, and its relation to the cotangent bundle of a manifold. Explain the need for a cotangent \emph{complex} in the derived setting. Introduce stable $\infty$-categories, with the derived $\infty$-category of real vector spaces as the main example. Discuss shifts, fiber and cofiber sequences, and their relation to short exact sequences in abelian categories. Introduce derived square-zero extensions and \Cinfty-derivations, and define absolute and relative cotangent complexes. State base change and the transitivity cofiber sequence for cotangent complexes, and use that to calculate the tangent complex of the derived zero locus of a section $s\colon U\to E$ at a zero $x$:
\[
  [T_xU\xrightarrow{d_xs}E_x].
\]
Explain how to interpret its cohomology in degrees $0$ and $1$ as deformation and obstruction spaces.}
{\textcite[Section~5]{Steffens_Derived_Differential_Geometry};
\textcite{Pascarella_Cotangent_Complex_Derived_Differential_Geometry};
\textcite[Section~7]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.}

\Talk{7}{24 November}{Quasi-smooth derived geometry and Kuranishi charts}
{Understand quasi-smooth derived \Cinfty-schemes and their local descriptions via Kuranishi charts.}
{Introduce localizations of derived $\Cinfty$-rings, stating flatness as a black-box, and define the spectrum of a derived $\Cinfty$-ring. Define derived \Cinfty-schemes locally of finite presentation, and identify the affine objects with derived manifolds. Define quasi-smooth derived \Cinfty-schemes. Introduce Kuranishi charts $(U,E,s)$ and their associated derived manifolds, and discuss the normal-form theorem: every quasi-smooth derived \Cinfty-scheme of finite presentation is locally presented by a Kuranishi chart. Illustrate the non-uniqueness of the presentation by discussing stabilization of charts.}
{\textcite[Chapter~1 and Sections~4.1.3 and~5.1.1]{Steffens_Derived_Differential_Geometry}.}

\Talk{8}{1 December}{Derived bordism and fundamental classes}
{Define the theory of derived bordism and understand its benefits over classical bordism.}
{We follow Spivak. Explain how quasi-smooth derived manifolds are good for doing intersection theory. Define derived cobordism over $X$. State proposition~3.3, recalling its proof for derived \Cinfty-rings. State proposition~3.4. Prove that the comparison map
	\[
	\Omega(X) \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ } \Omega^{\mathrm{der}}(X).
	\] 
	is an isomorphism by transverse pertubation. Deduce the intersection formula
	\[
	[A]\smile[B]=[A\mathbin{\times^{\mathrm{der}}_M}B]
	\]
	without transversality hypothesis. Discuss the derived self-intersection of the zero section of a vector bundle as the main example, recovering its Euler class.}
{\textcite[Sections~1--3]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.}

\Talk{9}{8 December}{Stacks and local representability}
{Understand how stacks describe moduli problems.}
{Define open coverings of derived manifolds and the resulting notion of ($\infty$-)stacks on the category of derived manifolds via higher categorical descent. Briefly explain the comparison with smooth stacks. Define when such a derived \Cinfty-stack is representable by a derived \Cinfty-scheme, giving the derived zero locus of a section as a running example. Define effective epimorphisms and atlases. State the local criterion for representability criterion, and explain how an open atlas yields a global representing derived \Cinfty-scheme. Introduce relative representability and its locality on the base. }
{\textcite[Section~2.1]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.}

\Talk{10}{15 December}{Section stacks and derived solution stacks}
{Construct the moduli stack of solutions to a differential operator}
{Motivate differential operators and their solutions (e.g.\ harmonic functions, critical points of nonlinear energies, pseudo-holomorphic curves), and explain how to see these as solutions for $P(u) = b$ for a map $P\colon \Gamma_N(X \to N) \to \Gamma_N(Y \to N)$ and a section $b$. Introduce jet stacks to define finite-order differential operators and their induced maps on sections. Explain the relative version for smooth families $M\to S$, and briefly discuss how descent allows $S$ to be a stack. Define differential moduli problems and their solution stacks. Explain linearization and \emph{elliptic} differential moduli problems. As a running example, use
\[
  P(u)=\Delta u+u^2
\]
on a closed connected Riemannian manifold.}
{\textcite[Definition~3.2.1, Definition~3.3.7,
	Proposition~3.3.9, and
	Definitions~3.4.1--3.4.2]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli};
	\textcite[Section~4]{Pardon_Representability_Elliptic_Fredholm}.}

\Talk{11}{12 January}{Elliptic representability I: local reductions}
{Reduce the representability problem to section stacks of vector bundles}
{State the seminar's main theorem: Steffens's relative elliptic representability theorem \cite[Theorem~3.4.3]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}. For most of the talk, fix $S = \mathrm{pt}$ and consider a moduli problem of the form $P(u) = b$ for a finite-order elliptic differential operator $P$ and a section $b$. Use relative exponential maps and Lemma 3.4.4 to identify its solution stack locally around a solution $u_0$ with the solution stack of a moduli problem of the form $\widetilde{P}(u) = 0$ for $\widetilde{P}\colon Q \to \operatorname{Map}_N(N,E)$, where $E,F \to N$ are vector bundles and $Q \subseteq \operatorname{Map}_N(N,F)$ is an open substack. Explain how varying the chosen solution gives an effective open cover, so that local representability implies representability. Next, explain how the same coordinate construction reduces a moduli problem $P_1(u_1) = P_2(u_2)$ to this form. Finally, explain what changes when the base $S$ is a general manifold or a smooth stack.}
{\textcite[Theorem~3.4.3, Proposition~3.2.19, Lemmas~3.2.20--3.2.21,
	Proposition~3.3.9, and Lemma~3.4.4]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli};
	\textcite[Introduction and the beginning of Section~5]{Pardon_Representability_Elliptic_Fredholm}.}

\Talk{12}{19 January}{Elliptic representability II: the local Kuranishi criterion}
{Explain the formal thickening argument that produces Kuranishi charts.}
{Start with $S = \mathrm{pt}$ and continue from the case $\widetilde{P}(u) = 0$ from last talk. Explain formal thickening of operators: for a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ and a linear map $\iota\colon V \to \operatorname{Map}_N(N,E)$, consider the augmented operator
\[
	\overline{P}\colon Q \times V \to \operatorname{Map}_N(N,E), \qquad \overline{P}(u,v) := \widetilde{P}(u) + \iota(v),
\]
and show that the solution stack $\operatorname{Sol}(\widetilde{P})$ is the derived zero locus of the map of stacks $\operatorname{Sol}(\overline{P}) \to V$. Deduce that if $Z = \operatorname{Sol}(\overline{P})$ is a finite-dimensional manifold, then $\operatorname{Sol}(\widetilde{P}) \simeq Z \times^{\mathrm{der}}_V 0$ provides a Kuranishi chart. Explain the analogous local statement around a solution of $\widetilde{P}$.

Introduce submersive maps of smooth stacks and of derived \Cinfty-stacks, and show that the inclusion of smooth stacks into derived stacks preserves pullbacks along submersive maps. Show how to regard the Fréchet vector space $\Gamma(N;E)$ of sections of $E$ as a smooth stack, and identify its derived \Cinfty-stack with $\operatorname{Map}_N(N,E)$. Deduce that $\operatorname{Sol}(\overline{P}\vert_U)$ is a finite-dimensional manifold whenever $V$ and $\iota$ are chosen such that the augmented operator $\overline{P}\vert_U \colon U \subseteq Q \times V \to \Gamma(N;E)$ is a submersive map of smooth stacks. Finally, explain the relative version over $S$ and how the resulting local Kuranishi presentations imply relative representability.}
{\textcite[Propositions~2.2.17--2.2.18 and Corollary~2.2.19;
	Propositions~3.1.7 and~3.1.9 and Lemma~3.2.11;
	the final pullback argument following the proof of claim~$(*)$ in Theorem~3.4.3;
	Proposition~2.1.48 and Corollary~2.1.49]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.}

\Talk{13}{26 January}{Elliptic representability III: the analytic input}
{Use nonlinear elliptic Fredholm analysis to make the augmented operator into a submersive map of smooth stacks}
{Start with $S = \mathrm{pt}$. Introduce the Sobolev spaces of sections $H^{\ell}(E;N)$ and explain how a differential operator of order $k$ extends to compatible maps
\[
	\widetilde{P}_\ell\colon Q_{k+\ell}\longrightarrow H^\ell(E;N)
\]
for sufficiently large $\ell$. Define Fredholm operators, and state as an analytic black-box that the Sobolev extensions of a linear elliptic operator on a compact manifold are Fredholm. Given a solution $t$ of $\widetilde{P}$, choose $V = \operatorname{coker}(T_t\widetilde{P}) = \Gamma(N;E)/\operatorname{im}(T_t\widetilde{P})$ and a splitting $\iota\colon V \to \Gamma(N;E)$ of the quotient map, so that the derivative of the augmented operator $\overline{P}$ is surjective at $(t,0)$. Restrict to compatible neighbourhoods on which the augmented derivatives are surjective.

For an ordinary manifold $Z$ and a smooth map $h\colon Z \to \Gamma(N;E)$, apply the Banach implicit function theorem to the equation $\widetilde{P}_{\ell}(u) + \iota(a) = h(z)$ to obtain finite-dimensional manifolds $W_{\ell}$ of Sobolev solutions whose maps to $Z$ are submersions. Using linear and nonlinear elliptic regularity, show that the tower $\dots \to W_{\ell+1} \to W_{\ell}$ is eventually constant and consists of smooth solutions, so that the limit $W$ agrees with the pullback $Z \times_{\Gamma(N;E)} U$ of smooth stacks, where $U\subseteq Q\times V$ is the corresponding neighbourhood of smooth sections. Conclude that the augmented operator $\overline{P}\vert_U \colon U \to \Gamma(N;E)$ is submersive in the sense required by the previous talk. Explain the relative version over $S$, and conclude the proof of the representability theorem. Illustrate the argument with $P(u)=\Delta u+u^2$ near $u=0$.}
{\textcite[Lemmas~3.4.5--3.4.6, the analytic part of the proof of
	Theorem~3.4.3]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}}

\Talk{14}{2 February}{Pseudo-holomorphic curves as a derived moduli problem}
{Apply the representability theorem to a key geometric application.}
{Describe families of maps from smooth compact Riemann surfaces to an almost complex manifold and formulate the Cauchy--Riemann equation as a finite-order differential moduli problem. Identify its linearization as a Cauchy--Riemann type operator and discuss/prove its ellipticity. Write the relative deformation-obstruction complex and interpret its cohomology. Apply Steffen's elliptic representability to obtain a derived moduli stack of pseudo-holomorphic curves. Given time, briefly discuss the task of representing the compactifications thereof and its applications to enumerative questions.}
{\textcite[Sections~2.1, 2.3--2.4, and
3.3--3.4]{Wendl_Lectures_Holomorphic_Curves};
\textcite[Section~4]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.}
