# Joint handoff contract for Talks 9--11

## Purpose

Talks 9--11 form one block. Their collective task is to move from the local
Kuranishi presentations of Talk 8 to one finite-dimensional analytic model
of an intrinsic differential moduli problem. The three talks should not each
repeat the motivation, the derived zero-locus formalism, or the distinction
between a moduli functor and a representing object.

## The three responsibilities

### Talk 9: stacks and local representability

**Input from Talk 8.** Quasi-smooth derived objects admit local Kuranishi
presentations, presentations are nonunique, and pairwise changes do not by
themselves supply higher coherence.

**Question.** How can an intrinsic functor of families carry all coherence,
and when do representable local charts glue to a derived scheme?

**Output.** The audience may subsequently use:

1. A prestack as an anima-valued functor on affine derived test objects.
2. Stack descent for open covers of the parameter object.
3. Representability and the universal family.
4. Representable and locally representable morphisms.
5. Open local representability and its relative consequence.
6. The principle that local representatives of one stack inherit their
   comparison maps and all higher coherences from that stack.

**Not its task.** It does not define section stacks, relative jets, solution
stacks, or Fredholm reduction.

### Talk 10: section stacks and derived solution stacks

**Input from Talk 9.** Intrinsic moduli problems are stacks, representability
is a theorem rather than a definition, and a derived pullback represents an
equation with its equality data.

**Question.** How does a finite-order differential equation define an
intrinsic derived moduli stack in families, before representability is known?

**Output.** The audience may subsequently use:

1. The section stack and its universal property.
2. Base change and descent for section stacks.
3. The geometric meaning of relative jets and jet prolongation.
4. The solution stack as a derived pullback.
5. The relative tangent complex as the complex of the linearized differential
   operator.
6. Ellipticity as the condition which makes that linearized complex Fredholm
   after analytic completion.

**Not its task.** It does not construct a finite-dimensional reduction or
prove that the solution stack is represented.

### Talk 11: Fredholm equations and finite-dimensional reduction

**Input from Talk 10.** The intrinsic solution stack exists and its tangent
complex at a solution is the infinite-dimensional linearized elliptic
complex.

**Question.** How does Fredholmness produce an actual finite-dimensional
Kuranishi model near one solution?

**Output.** The audience may subsequently use:

1. The Sobolev extension and elliptic Fredholm theorem as quoted analytic
   inputs.
2. The obstruction-space construction of a finite-dimensional Kuranishi
   chart.
3. A quasi-isomorphism from its tangent complex to the Fredholm complex.
4. Nonlinear elliptic regularity as the return from Sobolev to smooth
   solutions.
5. The exact reduction of $\Delta u+u^2$ to $a\mapsto a^2$.
6. The precise remaining gap: a Kuranishi model for the ordinary solution
   germ has not yet been identified with an open part of the intrinsic
   derived solution stack.

**Not its task.** It does not prove elliptic representability, compatibility
in families, or independence of analytic choices as a statement about the
intrinsic stack.

## Repetition policy

1. Talk 9 may use the derived zero-locus functor as a short representability
   example, but must not recalculate its Koszul algebra or tangent complex.
2. Talk 10 may recall the definition of a stack in one sentence, but must not
   repeat Cech descent or the open-atlas theorem.
3. Talk 11 may display the solution-stack tangent complex, but must not
   reconstruct section stacks, jets, or the solution pullback.
4. The equation $\Delta u+u^2=0$ begins in Talk 10 and continues into Talk 11.
   Talk 10 proves that its ordinary solution set is one point and computes
   the linearized complex. Talk 11 computes its finite-dimensional reduction.
5. Talk 11 gives the obstruction-space reduction as the only general
   reduction. General Lyapunov--Schmidt coordinates remain supplementary and
   are used live only in the concrete Laplacian calculation.

## Timing contract

Each live route should contain at most 75 minutes of prepared material. The
targets are:

1. Talk 9: 70 minutes.
2. Talk 10: 73 minutes.
3. Talk 11: 74 minutes.

This leaves 15--20 minutes in each meeting for questions, slower passages,
and board transitions.
