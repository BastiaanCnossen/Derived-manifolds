# Deliberation 02: smooth geometry through $C^\infty$-rings

## Status and provisional verdict

This dossier concerns the material provisionally assigned to Talk 2 in
`program.tex`. The title and chapter boundary remain provisional. Each seminar
talk lasts 90 minutes.

The detailed delivery plan is now recorded in `planning/02-outline.md`, and a
complete working manuscript has been written in `chapters/02.tex`. The
mathematical boundary remains provisional until the chapter has been tested as
a live talk.

The proposed unit is viable as a full talk, but its present description contains
four substantial ingredients: the definition of a $C^\infty$-ring, the free-ring
theorem, Borel's theorem, and full faithfulness for locally closed subsets. These
ingredients form a coherent unit only if they are given different weights.

The recommended spine is:

> A $C^\infty$-ring is a set on which every smooth map acts as an algebraic
> operation. The free examples are rings of smooth functions on Euclidean
> spaces, and their quotients encode smooth equations. This algebra already
> distinguishes equations with the same ordinary zero set.

The free-ring theorem and the construction of quotients should form the
elementary engine. The quotient $C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x^2)$ should be the
principal worked example. Borel's theorem should remain in the manuscript as a
range test, but it is supplementary for the default live route. The
closed-subset case of the full-faithfulness result should be proved as the
geometric payoff and as the load-bearing input passed to Talk 3. The extension
to arbitrary locally closed subsets should be stated, with its proof placed in
supplementary manuscript material.

With these choices, the talk has about 70--75 minutes of prepared material. A
complete proof of Borel's theorem or of the auxiliary characteristic-function
lemma for arbitrary open subsets would overload the unit. The closed-subset
argument has priority because it supplies the manifold consequence used later.

## Role in the seminar

Talk 1 ended with the request for an algebraic object which retains equations,
not merely their solution sets. Talk 2 should give the first answer to that
request. It does not yet construct a derived object. Instead, it explains the
ordinary smooth algebra on which the later derived theory is built.

The geometric question is:

> What algebraic structure records a smooth equation while remembering that
> arbitrary smooth functions, not only polynomials, may be substituted into
> it?

The talk succeeds if participants understand why an ordinary commutative
$\mathbb R$-algebra is not the natural first object, can use the universal
property of $C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$, and can read a quotient
$C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/I$ as a system of smooth equations.

Later units may assume the definition of a $C^\infty$-ring, the free-ring
universal property, the existence of quotient $C^\infty$-rings, and the notions
of finite generation and finite presentation. They may also reuse the
$x$ versus $x^2$ calculation. They should not assume that all finitely generated
$C^\infty$-rings come from manifolds, or that ordinary $C^\infty$-rings solve
the homotopy-coherence problem.

## Audience contract

1. Assume basic commutative algebra, smooth maps between Euclidean spaces, and
   elementary category theory involving finite products and functors.
2. Do not assume familiarity with Lawvere theories. Introduce the operations
   first, then package them as a product-preserving functor.
3. Explain explicitly why every $C^\infty$-ring has an underlying commutative
   $\mathbb R$-algebra, and why the converse is false.
4. Prove the free-ring universal property and justify quotients using
   Hadamard's lemma.
5. Keep Borel's theorem as supplementary manuscript material and an optional
   reserve-period range test.
6. Prove full faithfulness for closed subsets of Euclidean spaces directly from
   the free-ring theorem and smooth extension. State the locally closed version
   and identify the additional characteristic-function lemma it requires.
7. Use no $\infty$-categorical formalism. The derived enhancement is a later
   problem, not part of the definition given here.

## The connective opening

The opening should return directly to the two equations from Talk 1,

\[
  x=0
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  x^2=0.
\]

They have the same ordinary zero set. Their quotient $C^\infty$-rings are

\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x^2),
\]

and these are not isomorphic. Thus the quotient construction already retains
some information which the underlying set forgets. The talk should then ask
what structure is needed to make sense of these quotients as smooth, rather
than merely polynomial, algebras.

This opening is preferable to beginning with the category of Euclidean spaces.
It supplies a concrete problem before the formalism and creates a direct
dependency on Talk 1.

## Two presentations of the definition

The most accessible presentation starts with smooth functional calculus. A
$C^\infty$-ring consists of a set $A$ and, for every smooth map
$f\colon\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$, an operation

\[
  \Phi_f\colon A^n\longrightarrow A,
\]

compatible with projections and composition of smooth maps. The operations
associated to addition, multiplication, scalar multiplication, zero, and one
give $A$ an underlying commutative $\mathbb R$-algebra.

After this has been made concrete, introduce the categorical formulation. Let
$\mathrm{Euc}$ have objects $\mathbb R^n$ and smooth maps as morphisms. A
$C^\infty$-ring is equivalently a finite-product-preserving functor

\[
  A\colon \mathrm{Euc}\longrightarrow \mathrm{Set}.
\]

The underlying set is $A(\mathbb R)$, while product preservation identifies
$A(\mathbb R^n)$ with $A(\mathbb R)^n$. A smooth map
$f\colon\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ therefore produces the operation $\Phi_f$.

The category-theoretic formulation should take only a few minutes. Its purpose
is to display $C^\infty$-rings as models of an algebraic theory and to prepare
for later derived and anima-valued variants. It should not become a general
discussion of Lawvere theories.

## The elementary engine: free $C^\infty$-rings

For a manifold $M$, smooth functional calculus makes $C^\infty(M)$ a
$C^\infty$-ring. In particular, the coordinate functions
$x_1,\ldots,x_n$ lie in $C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$.

The principal result is the universal property

\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{C^\infty\mathrm{Ring}}
  \bigl(C^\infty(\mathbb R^n),A\bigr)
  \cong A^n.
\]

The map sends a morphism to the images of the coordinate functions. Conversely,
a tuple $(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ defines a morphism by

\[
  f\longmapsto \Phi_f(a_1,\ldots,a_n).
\]

This proof is short, but it should be carried out in full. It tells the audience
what a generator means in smooth algebra and makes every later presentation
$C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/I$ intelligible.

The theorem also makes the difference from ordinary polynomial algebra
precise. The free $C^\infty$-ring on one generator contains an operation
$f(x)$ for every smooth $f$, not only expressions built from addition and
multiplication.

## Structural lemma: quotients

If $I$ is an ideal in the underlying commutative $\mathbb R$-algebra of a
$C^\infty$-ring $A$, then $A/I$ inherits a $C^\infty$-ring structure. This is
not completely formal from the operations definition, since one must show that
each smooth operation is independent of the chosen representatives.

The relevant form of Hadamard's lemma says that, for a smooth
$f\colon\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$, there are smooth functions $g_i$ such that

\[
  f(y)-f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i-x_i)g_i(x,y).
\]

Consequently, replacing every input by an element congruent modulo $I$ changes
the output by an element of $I$. This proof deserves board time. It is the
first place where a specifically smooth fact ensures that a familiar algebraic
construction remains available.

A $C^\infty$-ring is finitely generated precisely when it admits a presentation

\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/I
\]

for some ideal $I$. It is finitely presented when $I$ can be chosen finitely
generated. These two notions must be kept distinct because
$C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ is not Noetherian.

## Principal worked example: one point with a first-order thickening

Hadamard's lemma gives

\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x)\cong\mathbb R.
\]

Taylor's formula with smooth remainder gives

\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x^2)
  \cong \mathbb R[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\]

Indeed, modulo $x^2$ every smooth function is determined by its value and first
derivative at zero. The smooth functional calculus on the dual numbers is

\[
  f(a+b\varepsilon)=f(a)+b f'(a)\varepsilon.
\]

Both $C^\infty$-rings have one $\mathbb R$-point, so they determine the same
ordinary solution set. They are nevertheless different $C^\infty$-rings. This
is the exact algebraic continuation of the $x$ versus $x^2$ comparison in Talk
1.

The example needs one caveat. An ordinary quotient $C^\infty$-ring remembers
the ideal of equations, including nilpotent thickening data, but it does not yet
remember derived self-intersections or higher homotopies between equations.
The distinction between ordinary and derived smooth geometry should be named,
but not developed, here.

## Borel's theorem and its proper role

Let $\mathfrak m_0^\infty\subset C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ be the ideal of
functions all of whose derivatives vanish at the origin. Borel's theorem says
that every formal power series is the Taylor series of a smooth function. Thus
the Taylor map induces an isomorphism

\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/\mathfrak m_0^\infty
  \cong \mathbb R[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]].
\]

This result is useful for three reasons.

1. It gives a vivid example of the flexibility of smooth functions as compared
   with analytic functions.
2. It shows that a quotient of a free $C^\infty$-ring on finitely many generators
   can have a very large underlying commutative algebra.
3. It introduces infinite jets and flat functions, notions which naturally
   reappear around formal and derived geometry.

Borel's theorem is explanatory enrichment, not a dependency for later talks.
A construction using bump functions with rapidly shrinking supports may be
sketched. A complete convergence proof would displace the main narrative and
should be supplementary manuscript material, if included at all.

## Geometric payoff: full faithfulness

Moerdijk--Reyes, Proposition I.1.5, gives a full-faithfulness result for locally
closed subsets of Euclidean spaces equipped with their rings of smooth
functions. Informally, a morphism of their function $C^\infty$-rings is induced
by a unique smooth map in the opposite direction.

The source defines a smooth function on a subset $X\subset\mathbb R^n$ to be a
function which extends smoothly to an open neighbourhood of $X$. A smooth map
between two such subsets is defined coordinatewise in the same way.

For closed $X\subset\mathbb R^n$, smooth extension gives

\[
  C^\infty(X)\cong C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/\mathfrak m_X,
\]

where $\mathfrak m_X$ is the ideal of functions vanishing on $X$. The free-ring
theorem describes a homomorphism

\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/I
  \longrightarrow C^\infty(\mathbb R^m)/J
\]

by a smooth map $\phi\colon\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$ satisfying
$\phi^*(I)\subset J$, with two such maps identified when their coordinate
functions agree modulo $J$. Taking $I=\mathfrak m_X$ and
$J=\mathfrak m_Y$, the condition says exactly that $\phi(Y)\subset X$, and the
equivalence relation says that two extensions determine the same map
$Y\to X$. This proves the full-faithfulness statement for closed subsets. It
does not require a separate classification of $\mathbb R$-points.

The passage from closed to locally closed subsets is the genuinely additional
part. Moerdijk--Reyes first prove that every open
$U\subset\mathbb R^n$ has a smooth characteristic function in their sense,
namely a smooth $f$ with

\[
  U=f^{-1}(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}).
\]

Their proof writes $U$ as a countable union of open cubes and chooses
coefficients small enough that a sum of bump functions and all its derivatives
converge. The graph

\[
  \{(x,y)\mid y f(x)=1\}\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}
\]

is then closed and diffeomorphic to $U$. Intersecting with the closed part of a
locally closed subset reduces Proposition I.1.5 to the closed case.

The proposition is therefore board-ready only if this auxiliary construction
is included or quoted. Its short final argument is board-ready, but the full
locally closed reduction costs another analytic convergence argument closely
resembling the proof pattern of Borel's theorem. Since later talks do not use
locally closed subsets in this generality, the live route should prove the
closed-subset case, state Proposition I.1.5, and move its characteristic-function
lemma to supplementary material.

This result is the correct handoff to Talk 3. Every manifold in the seminar's
convention admits a closed embedding in some Euclidean space. The closed-subset
case therefore shows that the contravariant functor
$M\mapsto C^\infty(M)$ is fully faithful. Talk 3 then proves finite presentation
and studies how products and transverse pullbacks are translated into colimits
of $C^\infty$-rings. Talk 4 and the later universal-property discussion continue
to rely on this embedding.

## Engine and application

The distinction should remain visible in both the manuscript and the live
talk.

1. **Engine:** Smooth functional calculus, the free-ring universal property,
   and the quotient construction via Hadamard's lemma.
2. **Application:** The rings associated to $x=0$ and $x^2=0$ distinguish two
   equations with the same solution set.
3. **Range test:** Borel's theorem shows how far finitely generated smooth
   algebra can depart from polynomial or analytic intuition. It is not used in
   any later argument.
4. **Geometric payoff:** Full faithfulness for closed subsets explains why
   passing to functions is not merely a loss of geometry. Its manifold
   consequence is used throughout the later seminar. The locally closed
   extension is supplementary.

3. **20--34 minutes:** Prove that $C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ is free on the

manifold consequence is a later input.

## Final takeaways

1. A $C^\infty$-ring has an operation for every smooth map between Euclidean
   spaces, subject to the identities forced by composition and projections.
2. The ring $C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ is free on its coordinate functions, so
   quotients of such rings are algebraic presentations of smooth equations.
3. Quotients exist because Hadamard's lemma makes every smooth operation
   compatible with ordinary ideals.
4. The quotients by $(x)$ and $(x^2)$ have the same ordinary point set but
   different smooth algebra, while Borel's theorem displays the much larger
   world of infinite smooth jets.
5. Function $C^\infty$-rings recover smooth maps in the relevant geometric
   settings. The next question is how manifolds and their transverse pullbacks
   are recognized algebraically.

If five takeaways prove too many for the live talk, combine the second and third.

## Resolved proof decisions

1. Borel's theorem is not a dependency for later material. This remains true
   for the unit on section stacks and jets, which uses finite-order jets rather
   than the realization of arbitrary formal Taylor series. Its complete proof
   belongs, if anywhere, in supplementary manuscript material.
2. The full-faithfulness conclusion for manifolds is used in Talks 3--4 and in
   the later universal-property discussion. Its proof belongs to the live
   route in Talk 2.
3. Later talks require only the closed-subset case, since manifolds admit closed
   Euclidean embeddings. This case follows directly from smooth extension and
   the free-ring theorem and should be proved.
4. The extension to arbitrary locally closed subsets requires the additional
   characteristic-function lemma, proved by a rapidly convergent sum of bump
   functions. Since that generality is not used later, the lemma and the full
   extension are supplementary manuscript material.

## Resolved expository choices

1. The quotient $C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x^2)$ is first computed by Taylor's
   formula and only then called the dual numbers.
2. Both finite generation and finite presentation are defined. Their detailed
   comparison and the proof that function rings of manifolds are finitely
   presented remain in Talk 3.
3. The operations definition precedes the product-preserving functor
   formulation, so that the categorical packaging names structure the audience
   has already seen concretely.
4. The live route proves full faithfulness for closed subsets by ambient
   coordinates and bump-function separation. Borel's theorem and the locally
   closed extension are explicitly secondary.
