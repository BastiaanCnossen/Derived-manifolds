# Deliberation 04: from smooth algebra to derived manifolds

## Quick orientation

### Verdict

Talk 4 should compare the ordinary and animated finite-limit envelopes of the
algebraic theory of smooth functions. Its central question is:

> What changes when the universal finite-limit completion of smooth algebra is
> formed in infinity-categories rather than only in ordinary categories?

This is the direct continuation of Talk 3. The audience has already proved
that
\[
  C^\infty(-)\colon \mathrm{Mfd}^{\mathrm{op}}
  \longrightarrow C^\infty\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}^{\mathrm{fp}}
\]
is fully faithful and sends transverse pullbacks to pushouts. Talk 4 first
explains that the opposite category of finitely presented ordinary
$C^\infty$-rings is the finite-limit envelope appropriate to ordinary
categories. It then shows, through a self-intersection, why this
$1$-categorical envelope cannot retain higher relation data. Animae enter at
that precise boundary. Repeating the same envelope construction with
anima-valued $C^\infty$-rings produces the infinity-category of derived
manifolds.

The talk does not discuss spectra, structure sheaves, tangent complexes, or a
particular model of infinity-categories. Earlier proposals which mixed these
topics into Talk 4 have been removed. Derived zero-locus calculations and
tangent complexes are developed separately in Talks 5 and 7.

The notation $\mathrm{DMfd}$ in this unit refers to the Carchedi--Steffens
universal finite-limit envelope. It should not yet be treated as synonymous by
definition with the locally affine derived $C^\infty$-schemes used in the
elliptic representability theorem. Talk 6 supplies the spectrum,
structure-sheaf, and gluing language needed to compare the two descriptions.

### Aim

By the end of the talk, participants should understand derived manifolds as a
universal construction rather than as an unexplained new category:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{ordinary $C^\infty$-rings}\cr
\text{valued in sets}
\end{array}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\begin{array}{c}
\text{ordinary finite-limit envelope}\cr
(C^\infty\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}^{\mathrm{fp}})^{\mathrm{op}},
\end{array}
\]
whereas
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{animated $C^\infty$-rings}\cr
\text{valued in animae}
\end{array}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\begin{array}{c}
\text{derived finite-limit envelope}\cr
\mathrm{DMfd}.
\end{array}
\]

The decisive conceptual point is that the ordinary construction is not a bad
approximation. It is universal among ordinary categories. Derived manifolds
arise when the same problem is asked in infinity-categories, where equality
between morphisms is structure and must be retained coherently.

## Inputs from Talks 1--3

The audience may be assumed to know the following.

1. A $C^\infty$-ring is a finite-product-preserving functor from Euclidean
   spaces to sets.
2. The ring $C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ is free on $n$ generators.
3. Finitely presented $C^\infty$-rings are obtained from finitely generated
   free rings by finitely many generators and relations.
4. Every manifold has a finitely presented ring of smooth functions.
5. The functor $C^\infty(-)$ is contravariantly fully faithful.
6. Products of manifolds become coproducts of function rings.
7. Transverse pullbacks become pushouts of function rings.
8. The tangent line--parabola intersection produces
   $C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x^2)$, which is not the function ring of a manifold.

The audience is not assumed to know animae, infinity-categories, animated
$C^\infty$-rings, or derived manifolds.

## Outputs needed later

Later talks may use the following statements.

1. For a category $C$ with finite products, a $C^\infty$-ring object in $C$ is
   a finite-product-preserving functor $\mathrm{Euc}\to C$.
2. The category
   \[
     \mathrm{Aff}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{C^\infty}
     =
     (C^\infty\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}^{\mathrm{fp}})^{\mathrm{op}}
   \]
   is the ordinary finite-limit envelope of the $C^\infty$ algebraic theory.
3. This ordinary envelope computes algebraic zero loci, but an ordinary
   self-intersection satisfies
   \[
     (A/I)\amalg_A(A/I)\cong A/I.
   \]
4. An anima is a collection in which equality is structure rather than a
   property. An infinity-category has hom animae rather than hom sets.
5. Pullbacks in an infinity-category are characterized by a universal
   property in hom animae. A generalized point includes a specified equality
   between the two composites and all its coherence.
6. An animated $C^\infty$-ring is a finite-product-preserving functor
   $\mathrm{Euc}\to\mathrm{An}$.
7. The opposite of the infinity-category of homotopically finitely presented
   animated $C^\infty$-rings is the finite-limit and idempotent-complete
   envelope of the $C^\infty$ algebraic theory.
8. The infinity-category $\mathrm{DMfd}$ is universal among idempotent-complete
   infinity-categories with finite limits receiving manifolds in a way that
   preserves transverse pullbacks and the terminal object.
9. Equivalently, for every such $C$,
   \[
     \operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{lex}}(\mathrm{DMfd},C)
     \simeq
     C^\infty\text{-}\mathrm{Ring}(C).
   \]
10. Consequently,
    \[
      \mathrm{DMfd}
      \simeq
      \operatorname{Alg}_{C^\infty}(\mathrm{An})_{\mathrm{fp}}^{\mathrm{op}}.
    \]
11. The later phrase ``derived $C^\infty$-scheme'' belongs to a locally affine
    geometric formalism. Its comparison with the universal affine category is
    an output of Talk 6, not an input to this talk.

Later talks may not yet assume a Koszul calculation, a tangent-complex formula,
quasi-smoothness, or a theory of locally affine derived $C^\infty$-schemes.

## Engine and application

### Engine

For an algebraic theory $T$, the opposite of the infinity-category of compact
$T$-algebras in animae is its finite-limit and idempotent-complete envelope.
For every idempotent-complete infinity-category $C$ with finite limits,
restriction along the free algebras gives
\[
  \operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{lex}}
  \bigl(\operatorname{Alg}_T(\mathrm{An})_{\mathrm{fp}}^{\mathrm{op}},C\bigr)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Alg}_T(C).
\]

The live talk should explain the proof architecture:

1. Finitely generated free algebras are compact.
2. Compact algebras are retracts of finite colimits of finitely generated free
   algebras.
3. Taking opposites turns finite colimits into finite limits.
4. A finite-product-preserving functor on the theory therefore extends
   uniquely to a left exact functor on the envelope.

The complete right Kan extension proof is supplementary manuscript material.

### Geometric application

Apply the engine to the algebraic theory of smooth functions. Carchedi and
Steffens prove that restriction from manifolds to Euclidean spaces induces
\[
  \operatorname{Fun}^{\pitchfork}(\mathrm{Mfd},C)
  \simeq
  \operatorname{Fun}^{\times}(\mathrm{Euc},C).
\]
Here the left side consists of functors preserving transverse pullbacks and
the terminal object. Combining this with the engine identifies the animated
finite-limit envelope with the universal infinity-category of derived
manifolds.

## Principal examples

### Ordinary zero loci

For $f\colon\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^k$, the ordinary algebraic zero locus is
represented by the pushout
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^k) \rar \dar
    \drar[pushout]
    & C^\infty(\mathbb R^n) \dar \\
  \mathbb R \rar & C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)/(f_1,\ldots,f_k).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The cases $f=x$ and $f=x^2$ show that the ordinary envelope already contains
both transverse and singular zero loci.

### Ordinary self-intersection

For $B=A/I$,
\[
  B\amalg_A B\cong B.
\]
The ordinary construction remembers the quotient but not that the same
relation was imposed twice. This example should precede any use of animae.

### A self-pullback in animae

For a point $x\colon *\to X$ of an anima,
\[
  *\times_X *\simeq X(x,x).
\]
Thus a self-pullback can retain the anima of self-equalities of the point. For
$X=\Pi_\infty(S^1)$, this is the loop anima of the circle. This is the
smallest example showing how a pullback changes when equality is structure.

The analogy with derived self-intersections should be labelled as motivation.
The actual algebraic calculation belongs to the next talk.

## Expository decisions

1. Introduce animae only after the ordinary self-intersection has produced a
   precise failure.
2. Use the principle that an equality should include a specified witness.
3. Do not introduce a model of infinity-categories.
4. State both universal properties of derived manifolds and explain why their
   equivalence is geometrically meaningful.
5. Prove the ordinary finite-presentation calculation live.
6. Explain the proof architecture of the animated envelope theorem, but do not
   reproduce its complete proof.
7. Keep the distinction between ordinary finite presentation and homotopical
   finite presentation visible in the manuscript.
8. End by asking what the newly adjoined pullbacks look like, thereby handing
   the seminar to derived zero loci and tangent complexes.

## Technical warnings

1. Do not claim that merely having all pullbacks makes a category derived.
   The ordinary finite-limit envelope is the counterexample.
2. Do not claim that any infinity-category automatically has tangent
   complexes. That requires additional infinitesimal structure.
3. Do not identify every ordinary finitely presented $C^\infty$-ring with a
   discrete compact animated $C^\infty$-ring. An ordinary finitely presented
   algebra need not be homotopically finitely presented.
4. Do not claim that the self-pullback example in animae computes the derived
   self-intersection of a manifold. It explains the categorical mechanism,
   while the algebraic computation comes later.
5. Do not replace the universal properties by the algebraic presentation.
   The presentation is a theorem identifying the universal category, not the
   conceptual definition forced on the audience.
6. Do not say that Talk 3 alone proves the equivalence between functors on
   manifolds and $C^\infty$-ring objects. The extension theorem of
   Carchedi--Steffens is an additional input.

## Deliberate exclusions

1. Spectra, stalks, structure sheaves, and locally affine $C^\infty$-schemes.
2. Presentation-dependent approaches to infinity-categories and animated
   $C^\infty$-rings.
3. Koszul algebras and explicit derived zero-locus calculations.
4. Tangent and cotangent complexes.
5. Quasi-smoothness and virtual dimension.
6. Kuranishi models and coherent descent.
7. Virtual fundamental classes and bordism.

## Provisional verdict

The talk has a coherent 90-minute narrative. Its engine is the finite-limit
envelope of an algebraic theory. Its geometric application is the
Carchedi--Steffens characterization of derived manifolds. Its decisive example
is the contrast between self-pullbacks in sets and in animae. The proposed
live route occupies approximately 75 minutes, leaving 15 minutes for questions
and slower board work.
