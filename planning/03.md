# Deliberation 03: manifolds as $C^\infty$-rings

## Quick orientation

This dossier concerns the material provisionally assigned to Talk 3 in
`program.tex`. The title and chapter boundary remain provisional. Each seminar
meeting lasts 90 minutes, and the prepared core should occupy at most 70--75
minutes.

There is no numbering mismatch with the local copy of Moerdijk--Reyes. The
program correctly cites Chapter I, Section 2, Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.3,
Propositions 2.5--2.6, and Theorem 2.8. The cited list does omit three genuine
dependencies: Corollary 2.2 presents the function ring of an open subset,
Lemma 2.4 computes products of open subsets, and Lemma 2.7 globalizes the
local proof of Proposition 2.6. These should be included in the source route,
even if not all three are proved during the live talk.

The proposed unit is mathematically coherent, but the current program
description conceals the amount of globalization in Proposition 2.6. A
complete live proof of every result in Section 2 would overload the talk. The
right division is:

1. Prove the regular-equation lemma, Lemma 2.1, as the main geometric engine.
2. Prove finite presentation of function rings and compute products at the
   level needed later.
3. Prove the transverse-pullback theorem locally and explain its global descent
   step precisely.
4. Include a complete proof of the descent lemma and the global theorem in the
   written manuscript, while allowing the descent calculation itself to be
   supplementary to the live route.

This respects the earlier dependency decision: the seminar will later use the
transverse-pullback theorem, so the manuscript should prove it. Accessibility
does not require every partition-of-unity globalization to be performed at the
board.

The talk sits at a structural hinge. Talk 2 showed that manifolds and smooth
maps can be recovered from their function $C^\infty$-rings. Talk 3 must show
that this embedding also remembers the geometric constructions which are
allowed classically. The crucial claim is:

> Contravariance turns transverse pullbacks of manifolds into pushouts of
> $C^\infty$-rings.

This is the exact classical input to the later universal property of derived
manifolds. It also isolates the failure which motivates the next two talks:
for a nontransverse pullback, the algebraic pushout generally ceases to be the
function ring of a manifold.

### Inputs from Talks 1--2

1. The transverse fiber product theorem and the tangent-space criterion.
2. The operations definition of a $C^\infty$-ring.
3. The free-ring theorem for $C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$.
4. The construction of quotient $C^\infty$-rings from ordinary ideals.
5. Hadamard's lemma.
6. Full faithfulness of $M\mapsto C^\infty(M)$.
7. The existence of a smooth function whose cozero locus is a prescribed open
   subset, at least as a quoted result from the supplement to Talk 2.

### Outputs needed later

1. Every manifold has a finitely presented function $C^\infty$-ring.
2. Products of manifolds become coproducts of $C^\infty$-rings.
3. Transverse inverse images and, consequently, arbitrary transverse fiber
   products become pushouts.
4. The category of manifolds is therefore contravariantly embedded in
   finitely presented $C^\infty$-rings in a way that preserves the classical
   pullbacks singled out by transversality.
5. When transversality fails, the algebraic pushout still exists but need not
   lie in the image of manifolds. This is the handoff to ordinary
   $C^\infty$-schemes and then to derived geometry.

## The slogans

### Slogan 1: regular equations generate their whole vanishing ideal

If $g=(g_1,\ldots,g_q)\colon M\to\mathbb R^q$ is a submersion along
$Z=g^{-1}(0)$, then every smooth function vanishing on $Z$ is a global smooth
linear combination of the $g_i$.

Concretely,
\[
  \mathfrak m_Z=(g_1,\ldots,g_q)
  \qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
  C^\infty(Z)\cong C^\infty(M)/(g_1,\ldots,g_q).
\]

Six facts to remember:

1. The inclusion $(g_1,\ldots,g_q)\subseteq\mathfrak m_Z$ is formal.
2. Near a point of $Z$, the submersion theorem makes the $g_i$ into coordinate
   functions.
3. Hadamard's lemma then proves local ideal membership.
4. Away from $Z$, one of the $g_i$ is invertible locally.
5. A partition of unity turns the local decompositions into a global one.
6. The regularity hypothesis is essential. For $g(t)=t^2$, the vanishing ideal
   of $Z=\{0\}$ is $(t)$, not $(t^2)$.

### Slogan 2: smooth finite presentation is geometric, not polynomial

Every manifold is described by finitely many generators and finitely many
relations in the algebraic theory containing all smooth operations.

What this means concretely:

1. If $U\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is open and $U=\{a\neq0\}$, then
   \[
     C^\infty(U)
     \cong
     C^\infty(\mathbb R^{n+1})/(y a(x)-1).
   \]
   An arbitrarily complicated open set is therefore presented by one extra
   generator and one relation.
2. A manifold admits a closed Euclidean embedding and a tubular-neighborhood
   retraction.
3. Consequently, $C^\infty(M)$ is a retract of $C^\infty(U)$ for an open
   Euclidean subset $U$.
4. Finitely presented objects are closed under retracts.
5. The result applies to noncompact manifolds. This is surprising only if one
   mistakenly interprets smooth finite presentation as polynomial finite
   presentation.
6. This finiteness statement is the classical predecessor of the
   homotopically finitely presented derived $C^\infty$-rings appearing later.

### Slogan 3: transverse geometry is underived geometry

For transverse maps $f\colon M\to N$ and $g\colon P\to N$, the square
\[
  \begin{array}{ccc}
    C^\infty(N)&\longrightarrow&C^\infty(M)\\
    \big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\\
    C^\infty(P)&\longrightarrow&C^\infty(M\times_NP)
  \end{array}
\]
is a pushout of $C^\infty$-rings.

Six facts to remember:

1. Products are the special case $N=*$. Thus
   $C^\infty(M\times P)$ is the coproduct of $C^\infty(M)$ and
   $C^\infty(P)$ in $C^\infty$-rings.
2. For an inverse image $f^{-1}(Z)$, transversality says that local defining
   functions for $Z$ remain independent after pullback along $f$.
3. The regular-equation lemma identifies both geometric function rings with
   the corresponding quotients.
4. The local square is then a pushout for the elementary reason that pushout
   along a quotient imposes the pulled-back relations.
5. The global step is descent for finitely presented $C^\infty$-rings, proved
   with a partition of unity.
6. A general transverse fiber product is an inverse image of the diagonal
   under $f\times g$.

## The main theorems

### The engine

The engine is Moerdijk--Reyes, Lemma 2.1:

> If $g_1,\ldots,g_q\in C^\infty(M)$ have linearly independent differentials
> along their common zero locus $Z$, then
> $\mathfrak m_Z=(g_1,\ldots,g_q)$.

The theorem contains exactly the local-to-global mechanism used throughout
the talk:

1. Differential topology identifies a regular zero locus locally with a
   coordinate plane.
2. Hadamard's lemma turns vanishing along that coordinate plane into ideal
   membership.
3. A partition of unity globalizes the local expressions.

This is more important for the audience to understand than the formal
manipulation of pushout diagrams. It explains mathematically why
transversality is the hypothesis under which ordinary quotient algebra agrees
with ordinary smooth geometry.

### The first application: finite presentation

Moerdijk--Reyes, Theorem 2.3 states that $C^\infty(M)$ is finitely presented
for every manifold $M$ with a countable basis. Its proof has three steps:

1. Use the smooth cozero function for an open $U\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ to
   realize $U$ as the regular hypersurface $y a(x)=1$.
2. Apply the engine to obtain a one-relation presentation for $C^\infty(U)$.
3. Realize $C^\infty(M)$ as a retract of such an open-set ring by a closed
   embedding and tubular-neighborhood retraction.

The external differential-topological input is the proper Whitney embedding
theorem together with the tubular-neighborhood theorem. The categorical input
is that retracts of finitely presented objects are finitely presented.

### The principal application: transverse pullbacks

Moerdijk--Reyes, Proposition 2.6 and Theorem 2.8 state that transverse inverse
images, and hence transverse fiber products, become pushouts. The local proof
is elementary once the engine is available. The global proof uses the
additional descent statement in Lemma 2.7.

This should be the one principal theorem named in the final summary. Finite
presentation and products are indispensable supporting results, but the
transverse-pullback theorem is the conceptual endpoint.

### The running application: axes versus tangency

The two pictures from Talk 1 give the best opening and closing calculation.
Let $A=C^\infty(\mathbb R^2)$.

1. For the two coordinate axes, the two function rings are $A/(x)$ and
   $A/(y)$. Their pushout over $A$ is
   \[
     A/(x,y)\cong\mathbb R,
   \]
   which is the function ring of their transverse intersection point.
2. For the $x$-axis and the parabola $y=x^2$, the two function rings are
   $A/(y)$ and $A/(y-x^2)$. Their pushout is
   \[
     A/(y,y-x^2)
     \cong C^\infty(\mathbb R)/(x^2),
   \]
   the dual-number example from Talk 2.

The set-theoretic pullback is a point in both cases. In the transverse case its
function ring is the algebraic pushout. In the tangent case the algebraic
pushout contains a nilpotent and is not the function ring of a manifold. This
calculation makes the boundary of the classical theorem visible before any
abstract proof begins.

## Closure properties and structural lemmas

### 1. Local ideal membership is global

For fixed $g_1,\ldots,g_q$, membership in the ideal they generate can be
checked on an open cover. The proof is the partition-of-unity calculation
\[
  h=\sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha h
   =\sum_{i=1}^q
     \left(\sum_\alpha\rho_\alpha a_{\alpha i}\right)g_i.
\]
This is the globalization step inside Lemma 2.1 and should be written
explicitly once.

### 2. Open subsets have one-relation presentations

If $U=\{a\neq0\}$, the map
\[
  U\longrightarrow
  \{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^{n+1}\mid y a(x)=1\},
  \qquad
  x\longmapsto(x,1/a(x))
\]
is a diffeomorphism. The defining function is a submersion along its zero
locus because its derivative with respect to $y$ is $a(x)\neq0$. Lemma 2.1
therefore supplies the quotient presentation.

### 3. Finite presentation is closed under retracts

The clean proof uses the categorical characterization: an algebra for a
finitary theory is finitely presented precisely when its covariant Hom functor
preserves filtered colimits. A retract of such a Hom functor again preserves
filtered colimits. The written chapter should state the characterization and
give this two-line proof. The live talk may present closure under retracts as a
standard categorical fact after explaining why it applies to $C^\infty$-rings.

### 4. Coproducts combine generators and relations

Write $A\otimes_\infty B$ provisionally for the coproduct of $C^\infty$-rings.
Then
\[
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)\otimes_\infty
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^m)
  \cong
  C^\infty(\mathbb R^{n+m}),
\]
and
\[
  (A/I)\otimes_\infty(B/J)
  \cong
  (A\otimes_\infty B)/(I,J).
\]
These formulas are immediate from universal properties and are enough to
compute products of open subsets. A retraction argument then gives
\[
  C^\infty(M)\otimes_\infty C^\infty(P)
  \cong C^\infty(M\times P).
\]

The notation $\otimes_\infty$ must be explained as a categorical coproduct,
not the ordinary tensor product of the underlying commutative
$\mathbb R$-algebras.

### 5. Finitely presented quotients satisfy descent on their zero loci

Moerdijk--Reyes, Lemma 2.7 says, in effect, that if
\[
  B=C^\infty(\mathbb R^k)/(f_1,\ldots,f_p)
\]
and $\{W_\alpha\}$ covers the common zero locus of the $f_i$, then $B$ is the
limit of the corresponding Cech diagram of the restricted quotient rings.
Compatible local representatives are glued with a partition of unity;
uniqueness follows from the local-to-global ideal-membership calculation.

The statement looks technical in the source because it is written as a full
inverse limit over all finite intersections. Its conceptual content is simple:
functions modulo finitely many equations satisfy descent on the locus cut out
by those equations.

This lemma is load-bearing for the global proof of the transverse-pullback
theorem. It should be proved in the manuscript. For the live talk, the speaker
should state it, explain the partition-of-unity mechanism, and prove at most
the two-open-set case unless the preceding material has run ahead of schedule.

## Strategy of the proof

The full dependency chain should be kept visible:

\[
  \text{Hadamard + submersion theorem + partitions of unity}
  \Longrightarrow
  \text{regular-equation lemma}
\]
\[
  \Longrightarrow
  \begin{cases}
    \text{Open sets and manifolds are finitely presented},\\
    \text{Local transverse inverse images are quotient pushouts}.
  \end{cases}
\]
\[
  \text{Coproduct calculation + retracts}
  \Longrightarrow
  \text{Products become coproducts}
\]
\[
  \text{Descent lemma}
  \Longrightarrow
  \text{Global transverse inverse images become pushouts}
\]
\[
  \text{Products + inverse images of the diagonal}
  \Longrightarrow
  \text{All transverse fiber products become pushouts}.
\]

### Step 1: Prove the regular-equation lemma

For a function $h$ vanishing on $Z(g_1,\ldots,g_q)$, check ideal membership
locally. At a point outside the zero locus, some $g_i$ is invertible. At a
point on the zero locus, use submersion coordinates and Hadamard's lemma. Then
globalize with a partition of unity.

### Step 2: Deduce finite presentation

Present an open subset by the single regular equation $y a(x)-1=0$. Embed a
general manifold as a closed Euclidean submanifold, retract a tubular
neighborhood onto it, and pass finite presentation through the resulting
retract of function rings.

### Step 3: Compute products

First combine the one-relation presentations of two open subsets. Then use
tubular-neighborhood retractions for two embedded manifolds and the universal
property of the coproduct to descend the open-set result to the manifolds.

### Step 4: Prove the transverse inverse-image theorem locally

Near a point of a codimension-$q$ submanifold $Z\subseteq N$, choose independent
defining functions $g_1,\ldots,g_q$. If $f\colon M\to N$ is transverse to $Z$,
then $g_1\circ f,\ldots,g_q\circ f$ are independent along $f^{-1}(Z)$. The
regular-equation lemma identifies the restriction maps with the quotients
\[
  C^\infty(U)\longrightarrow C^\infty(U)/(g_1,\ldots,g_q)
\]
and
\[
  C^\infty(f^{-1}U)
  \longrightarrow
  C^\infty(f^{-1}U)/(g_1\circ f,\ldots,g_q\circ f).
\]
The resulting square is visibly a pushout.

### Step 5: Globalize

Use the Cech descent lemma for finitely presented test rings. Local pushout
factorizations are unique and compatible, hence glue to the required global
factorization. This is the technically least transparent part of the source,
so the written proof should separate the following claims:

1. Maps from the four global function rings induce maps on suitable local
   restrictions.
2. The local pushout property produces compatible local maps.
3. Lemma 2.7 glues those local maps uniquely.

### Step 6: Pass from inverse images to fiber products

For transverse maps $f\colon M\to N$ and $g\colon P\to N$, the product map
\[
  f\times g\colon M\times P\longrightarrow N\times N
\]
is transverse to the diagonal. Its inverse image is $M\times_NP$. Apply the
product theorem and the transverse inverse-image theorem.

## Expository audit

### The compelling narrative

The chapter should not read as a sequence of five results from a textbook.
Every result should answer one question:

> When does the algebraic pushout of smooth function rings still describe an
> ordinary manifold?

The answer is transversality. Finite presentation and products are the
structural inputs needed to state and globalize that answer.

### Likely points of confusion

1. Variance: a pullback square of manifolds becomes a pushout square of
   function rings. Draw the two squares side by side at least once.
2. Coproduct notation: $\otimes_\infty$ is not the tensor product of underlying
   commutative algebras.
3. Finite presentation: the finite operations are generators and relations in
   the smooth algebraic theory, not polynomial generators of the underlying
   algebra.
4. Regular equations: independence is required only along the common zero
   locus.
5. Local versus global: the quotient description is immediate in submersion
   coordinates; partitions of unity are what make it a statement about global
   function rings.
6. Products versus transverse inverse images: products are proved first because
   the diagonal reduction for a general fiber product uses them.
7. Nontransverse pushouts: the dual-number pushout is a valid
   $C^\infty$-ring, but it is not the function ring of an ordinary manifold.
   It is not yet a derived $C^\infty$-ring either.
8. Descent: Lemma 2.7 is descent for functions modulo equations, not the stacky
   or higher-categorical descent used later. It is nevertheless a useful
   classical precursor.

### Board strategy

1. Keep the two intersection pushouts visible until the final comparison.
2. Reserve one board panel for the implication
   \[
     \text{Independent differentials}
     \Longrightarrow
     \mathfrak m_Z=(g_1,\ldots,g_q).
   \]
3. Draw a pullback square of manifolds and its reversed pushout square of rings
   in matching positions.
4. Treat the tubular-neighborhood retraction as a geometric picture before
   writing the induced retract of rings.
5. State the descent lemma conceptually before showing any Cech notation.
6. End on the tangent parabola calculation, not on the technical descent
   argument.

## Deliberate exclusions

1. Do not introduce local $C^\infty$-rings, spectra, or structure sheaves. They
   belong to Talk 6, after the universal construction and affine derived
   calculations.
2. Do not define derived $C^\infty$-rings or homotopy pushouts.
3. Do not discuss a general model of infinity-categories.
4. Do not classify finitely presented $C^\infty$-rings or imply that every one
   comes from a manifold.
5. Do not develop germ-determined ideals beyond the local-to-global principle
   actually used.
6. Do not prove a general descent theorem for $C^\infty$-schemes.
7. Do not suggest that nilpotents detect every failure of transversality.
8. Do not discuss Weil algebras or jet rings, despite their appearance in the
   next section of Moerdijk--Reyes.

## Resolved choices for the detailed outline

1. Pose both intersection pushouts in the opening, compute the transverse axes
   immediately, and complete the dual-number calculation at the end.
2. Prove the categorical finite-presentation criterion in the manuscript, but
   emphasize only closure under retracts in the live route.
3. Keep the two-open-set proof of the Cech descent lemma in the reserve period.
4. Use $A\otimes_\infty B$ for absolute coproducts, following the source. Draw
   relative pushouts as squares rather than introducing a relative tensor
   notation.

## Provisional verdict

Talk 3 is viable as a 90-minute unit and should not presently be merged or
split. Its strongest narrative is not merely ``manifolds are finitely
presented.'' It is:

> Regular equations are algebraically exact, so transverse pullbacks of
> manifolds are computed by pushouts of smooth function rings. Nontransverse
> pushouts leave the world of manifolds.

The regular-equation lemma is the engine, the transverse-pullback theorem is
the principal application, finite presentation is the structural finiteness
result, and the axes-versus-parabola calculation is the geometric thread. The
one significant compression is the global descent lemma: it belongs in full in
the manuscript, but its complete proof should not be part of the default live
route.

`planning/03-outline.md`.
