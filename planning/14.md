# Deliberation 14: Pseudo-holomorphic curves as a derived moduli problem

## Quick orientation

The final talk should be the application and synthesis of the seminar, not a
fourteenth foundational installment. Its protagonist is the nonlinear
Cauchy--Riemann equation

\[
  \bar\partial_Ju=0
\]

for maps from smooth compact Riemann surfaces to an almost complex manifold.
The audience should see the entire architecture of the preceding talks act on
one familiar geometric equation:

\[
  \text{finite-order equation}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{elliptic linearization}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{Fredholm deformation complex}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{quasi-smooth derived moduli stack}.
\]

Talks 12 and 13 have already supplied the hard representability theorem.
Talk 14 should therefore spend its proof time on the Cauchy--Riemann symbol
calculation and on checking that the geometric moduli problem satisfies the
theorem's hypotheses. It should not repeat the Sobolev augmentation,
finite-dimensional reduction, or descent proofs.

The talk has one application theorem:

> The stack of pseudo-holomorphic maps from varying smooth compact Riemann
> surfaces to a fixed almost complex manifold is relatively represented over
> the stack of domains by quasi-smooth derived smooth schemes locally of finite
> presentation.

The source formulation is stated for a symplectic target with a tame almost
complex structure. The local representability argument uses only the almost
complex structure. The symplectic form becomes essential for the global
compactness questions which are deliberately excluded from the theorem.

## The role of the talk

1. It gives the seminar's general theory a nontrivial geometric payoff.
2. It identifies a standard analytic deformation-obstruction complex as the
   intrinsic relative tangent complex of a derived moduli stack.
3. It shows why varying domains and reparametrizations require a stack rather
   than a set or an ordinary moduli space.
4. It closes the logical ledger by distinguishing local representability from
   compactification, orientation, and virtual-class constructions.
5. It reconnects with the derived-bordism interlude: a compact oriented derived
   manifold can carry a fundamental bordism class, but Talk 14 does not prove
   the compactness or orientation hypotheses required to use that result.

## Content contract

The live talk should establish only the following four outputs.

1. **Geometric equation.** The equation is the vanishing of the
   complex-antilinear part of the first derivative.
2. **Principal calculation.** The linearized Cauchy--Riemann operator has
   invertible principal symbol at every nonzero covector.
3. **Derived interpretation.** Its Fredholm complex is the relative tangent
   complex, with kernel the deformation space and cokernel the obstruction
   space.
4. **Representability payoff.** The local zero-section version of the theorem
   from Talks 12--13 applies in families over the smooth stack of domains.

Everything else is subordinate to these outputs.

## Decisions about the live and supplementary boundary

1. The talk assumes the elementary language of almost complex structures, but
   derives the complex-linear and complex-antilinear decomposition needed for
   the equation.
2. The first-jet formulation is explained conceptually. The talk does not
   reconstruct the general theory of relative jets from Talk 9.
3. The linearization formula is derived, and the principal symbol is proved to
   be an isomorphism. This is the main live proof.
4. Fredholmness and the Riemann--Roch index formula are quoted. The index
   formula is displayed once but not proved.
5. The fixed-domain deformation-obstruction complex is live.
6. Constant maps receive a three-minute live checkpoint giving their kernel
   and cokernel. The Dolbeault-cohomology proof and genus-by-genus discussion
   remain supplementary.
7. The stack of smooth compact Riemann surfaces, its universal family, and the
   role of automorphisms are live. Kodaira--Spencer theory is quoted.
8. The printed symmetric five-tuple is not developed live. A four-minute
   source note explains why the proof instead uses local zero-section charts.
   The full tangent cancellation and mixed-order audit remain supplementary.
9. The relative representability theorem and its proof by a short hypothesis
   checklist are live.
10. Absolute tangent complexes, marked points, varying targets, and equivariant
    variants remain supplementary.
11. Nodal compactification, bubbling analysis, orientations, and virtual
    classes are not developed. Their absence is stated as a mathematical
    boundary of the result.
12. The former logarithmic final chapter remains supplementary outlook
    material. It is not part of the live route.

## Source-status decision

Steffens's Construction 4.0.1 displays the pseudo-holomorphic moduli problem
as a symmetric five-tuple with the full first-jet bundle as common output and
then invokes the general elliptic representability theorem. Taken literally,
this presentation has two problems.

1. The full jet target retains complementary complex-linear jet directions in
   obstruction degree.
2. Replacing the target by the complex-antilinear jet bundle gives the correct
   tangent complex, but the symmetric matching operator has a zeroth-order
   value-matching component and a first-order Cauchy--Riemann component. It is
   therefore not elliptic in the fixed-order sense of the cited definition.

The application itself remains valid by the local proof architecture already
developed in Talks 12--13. In a local chart on the section stack, the equation
is the zero locus of a nonlinear operator whose vertical linearization is
precisely the elliptic operator \(D_u\). The final talk should state this
correction plainly, apply the local zero-section argument, and leave the full
algebraic audit to the manuscript supplement.

This is a source correction, not a claim that pseudo-holomorphic
representability fails.

## Narrative arc

The talk should move through five questions.

1. What is the geometric equation?
2. Why is its infinitesimal operator elliptic?
3. What derived tangent object does that operator determine?
4. How do domains, families, and automorphisms enter?
5. What does the representability theorem give, and what does it still not
   give?

The five eventual manuscript sections should answer exactly these questions.
The timed implementation is recorded in `planning/14-outline.md`.

## Source card

1. Pelle Steffens, *Representability of Elliptic Moduli Problems in Derived
   \(C^\infty\)-Geometry*, especially Definition 3.4.2, Theorem 3.4.3,
   Construction 4.0.1, and Remarks 4.0.2--4.0.3. Use the local copy in the
   derived-manifolds source folder and the current arXiv version 2404.07931.
2. Chris Wendl, *Lectures on Holomorphic Curves in Symplectic and Contact
   Geometry*, especially Sections 2.1, 2.3--2.5, 3.3, and 3.4. Use this for
   real Cauchy--Riemann type operators, the linearization, regularity,
   Fredholmness, and the index formula.
3. The preceding seminar chapters are the primary source for the derived
   zero-locus tangent complex, the smooth stack of domains, and the local
   elliptic representability engine. Avoid importing a second foundational
   formalism in the final talk.

## Remaining checks for the writing phase

1. Fix one convention for the sign in the principal-symbol formula and use it
   consistently with the convention for \(\bar\partial_J\).
2. State clearly whether the displayed Riemann--Roch index is relative to a
   fixed domain.
3. Separate the almost complex version proved by the local argument from the
   symplectic tame version appearing in Steffens's application.
4. Phrase the smoothness of the domain stack as a quoted result and do not
   suggest that its proof occurs in the talk.
5. Keep the relative Artin conclusion distinct from the stronger properness,
   orientation, and virtual-class statements which are not established.
