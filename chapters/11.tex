\chapter{Elliptic representability I: local reductions}
\label[chapter]{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models}

We now begin the proof that an elliptic equation has a finite-dimensional
derived moduli object. The preceding chapter constructed its solution
stack; we must now find open neighbourhoods represented by derived
zero loci. The proof occupies three chapters, beginning here with a
coordinate reduction of the nonlinear equation.

Near a chosen solution, relative exponential coordinates replace the
unknowns and outputs by sections of fixed vector bundles. These are
coordinates on the full nonlinear problem, not an approximation by its
linearization. We show that the resulting zero locus is an open
substack of the original solution stack, then extend the construction
to families. The problem remains infinite-dimensional at this stage;
the next two chapters carry out its finite-dimensional reduction.

\section{The representability theorem and proof strategy}

We state the relative theorem first, since parameters must be retained
throughout the proof. The terms in its statement are those introduced
in the preceding two chapters.

\begin{theorem}[Relative elliptic representability]
\label[theorem]{thm:Relative_Elliptic_Representability}
Let $S$ be a smooth stack, let $M\to S$ be a proper family of
manifolds, and let $\mathcal E$ be an $S$-family of elliptic differential
moduli problems on $M$. Then
\[
 \Sol(\mathcal E)\longrightarrow S
\]
is representable by quasi-smooth derived $\Cinfty$-schemes locally
of finite presentation.
\end{theorem}

This is \cite[Theorem~3.4.3]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Properness makes the fibre manifolds compact. The submersion hypotheses
in a differential moduli problem give local coordinates on its section
stacks. Ellipticity will provide the finite-dimensional analytic
reduction in \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Derived_Charts}.

Relative representability means that every base change to a derived
test object over $S$ is represented by a derived scheme.
When $S=*$, it says that the whole solution stack is a quasi-smooth
derived scheme. A nontrivial parameter stack can retain automorphisms
in the total moduli stack.

The proof has three steps, with a different output at each stage.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{.16\linewidth}p{.76\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Step & Output \\
\midrule
Coordinates & An exact local nonlinear equation between sections of
fixed vector bundles (this chapter). \\[4pt]
Augmentation & A criterion identifying the solution stack with
$Z\times_V^{\mathrm{der}}\{0\}$, where $Z$ is an ordinary
finite-dimensional manifold
(\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}). \\[4pt]
Analysis & A proof of that criterion for arbitrary smooth families
of right-hand sides
(\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Derived_Charts}). \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each local identification concerns the solution functor on derived
parameters. In the last step, smoothness of a single ordinary zero set
would prove too little: the analytic family criterion is what permits
comparison with the derived pullback. The final chapter of the proof
assembles these local identifications into relative representability.

\section{Coordinates on section stacks}

We first work over a point. The coordinate neighbourhoods come from
local additions on the source and target submersions. Compactness of
the domain then turns them into open neighbourhoods of entire sections.

Let $N$ be a closed manifold, let $X,Y\to N$ be submersions, and let
\[
 p\colon J_N^k(X)\longrightarrow Y
\]
define an operator $P\colon\Sec_N(X)\to\Sec_N(Y)$.
Choose a section $b$ of $Y$ and a solution $u_0$ of $P(u)=b$.
The source and target need not be vector bundles.

The bundles of infinitesimal variations at the chosen sections are
\[
 F=u_0^*T_{X/N},\qquad E=b^*T_{Y/N}.
\]
Both are finite-rank vector bundles on $N$.
The reduction will produce an open substack
$Q\subseteq\Sec_N(F)$ containing its zero section and an operator
\[
 \widetilde P\colon Q\longrightarrow\Sec_N(E),
 \qquad \widetilde P(0)=0,
\]
whose derived zero locus is an open neighbourhood of $u_0$ in
$\Sol(P;b)$. It is still an infinite-dimensional section problem.
The gain is that unknowns and outputs now admit vector addition.

For a submersion $q\colon X\to N$, its vertical tangent bundle is
$T_{X/N}=\ker dq$. Choose a Riemannian metric along the fibres.
Its fibrewise exponential map is defined near the zero section:
\[
 \operatorname{Exp}\colon V\subseteq T_{X/N}\longrightarrow X.
\]
For a point $x\in X$, the restriction to $V_x$ takes values in the
same fibre $X_{q(x)}$ and has derivative the identity at zero.

More precisely, the map
\[
 (\pi,\operatorname{Exp})\colon V\longrightarrow X\times_N X
\]
can be restricted to a neighbourhood on which it is a diffeomorphism
onto a neighbourhood of the relative diagonal.
Such a map is called a \emph{local addition}.
The existence of local additions is a relative form of the tubular
neighbourhood construction.

Pulling back along $u_0$ gives
\[
 e_X\colon V_F\subseteq F\longrightarrow X
\]
over $N$, carrying the zero section to $u_0$ and identifying $V_F$
with an open neighbourhood of its image.
Similarly,
\[
 e_Y\colon V_E\subseteq E\longrightarrow Y
\]
is an open neighbourhood of $b$.
These are the relative exponential coordinates used in the proof.

For the special case $X=N\times Z$, a section is a map $u_0\colon N\to Z$.
The first coordinate map has the concrete formula
\[
 v(x)\longmapsto\exp_{u_0(x)}v(x),
 \qquad v\in\Gamma(N;u_0^*TZ)
\]
sufficiently small. It converts a nearby map into a vector field along
$u_0$, without choosing a coordinate chart of $Z$ containing the whole
image of $u_0$.

An open embedding $V_F\to X$ induces a map of section stacks
$\Sec_N(V_F)\to\Sec_N(X)$.
For compact $N$ this is an open inclusion
\cite[Proposition~3.2.19]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
On ordinary sections, the condition is that the image of a section
lie in $V_F$.

Compactness explains the openness. If a family $u_t$ lies in $V_F$
at $t_0$, each point of $N$ has a neighbourhood on which that remains
true for nearby $t$. A finite subcover gives one parameter
neighbourhood working on all of $N$.
The source proposition establishes this assertion for section stacks,
including derived parameters.

Finite-order operators require control of jets as well as values.
The coordinate changes induce maps on $k$-jets. Choose an open
neighbourhood $W\subseteq J_N^k(F)$ of the jet of the zero section
on which the transformed output lies in $V_E$.
Define
\[
 Q=\{v\in\Sec_N(F):j^kv\text{ factors through }W\}.
\]
Formally this is the pullback of the open inclusion
$\Sec_N(W)\to\Sec_N(J_N^k(F))$ along jet prolongation.
Thus $Q$ is open. Its ordinary sections form an open set in the
Whitney $C^k$ topology.

\section{The equation in local coordinates}

The coordinate maps give a new finite-order operator. To use it in
the representability proof, we check both that ellipticity is preserved
and that its derived zero locus is the required open substack.
The same construction then treats a matching problem.

On $Q$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Locally_Reduced_Operator}
 \widetilde P(v)=e_Y^{-1}\bigl(P(e_X(v))\bigr).
\end{equation}
The notation means composition on sections. It is defined because
$Q$ was chosen so that the output lies in the target chart.
At $v=0$ its value is zero.

This is again a finite-order operator. The change $e_X$ depends only
on the value of the section, so its $k$-jet depends on derivatives
of $v$ through order $k$. Postcomposing with $e_Y^{-1}$ also uses
no additional derivatives. We therefore obtain a smooth jet map
$\widetilde p\colon W\to E$ inducing \Cref{eq:Locally_Reduced_Operator}.

Ellipticity is preserved. At a section, the source and target coordinate
changes induce vector-bundle isomorphisms on variations.
The highest-order symbol of the transformed operator is the original
symbol composed with these isomorphisms. Derivatives of their coefficients
contribute only lower-order terms. Hence invertibility of the principal
symbol is unchanged.

The derived solution stacks also agree on this neighbourhood.

\begin{lemma}[Open restriction of a pullback]
\label[lemma]{lem:Open_Restriction_Cospan}
Suppose a map between two cospans of derived stacks has open inclusions
on all three objects. The induced map between their pullbacks
is an open inclusion.
\end{lemma}

This is the open-inclusion case of
\cite[Lemma~3.4.4]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
It follows by expressing the induced map as pullbacks and composites
of open inclusions. The target inclusion is a monomorphism, so a
matching homotopy between points in the restricted target lifts uniquely.

Apply the lemma to the source and target coordinate neighbourhoods
and to the chosen right-hand side.
First compute the pullback with target $\Sec_N(V_E)$.
Its inclusion into $\Sec_N(E)$ is open, and both the transformed
operator and zero section factor through it, so computing the
pullback in the larger target gives the same result.

\begin{proposition}[Local vector-bundle reduction]
\label[proposition]{prop:Local_Vector_Bundle_Reduction}
There is an open inclusion
\[
 \Sol(\widetilde P;0)\longrightarrow\Sol(P;b)
\]
whose image contains $u_0$. It identifies the first stack with an
open neighbourhood of that solution.
\end{proposition}

This statement concerns the full functors on derived parameters.
An equality of ordinary zero sets would not be sufficient for
the representability proof. The open-cospan lemma is what promotes
the coordinate construction to the asserted derived neighbourhood.

Now start with $P_1(u_1)=P_2(u_2)$ and a chosen solution
$(u_{1,0},u_{2,0})$ with common output $w_0$.
Choose relative exponential charts around all three sections.
They give fixed bundles
\[
 F_1=u_{1,0}^*T_{Y_1/N},\quad
 F_2=u_{2,0}^*T_{Y_2/N},\quad
 E=w_0^*T_{X/N}.
\]
Shrink jet neighbourhoods so both transformed outputs lie in the
same target chart.

The two transformed operators can now be subtracted:
\[
 \widetilde P(v_1,v_2)
 =\widetilde P_1(v_1)-\widetilde P_2(v_2).
\]
Its source is an open substack of $\Sec_N(F_1\oplus F_2)$.
Its zero locus gives an open part of the original matching problem.
The reason is the identity
\[
 a=b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a-b=0
\]
in a vector bundle, interpreted as an identity of pullback diagrams.
More categorically, the diagonal of a vector bundle is the pullback
of its zero section along the difference map.
Pasting these pullbacks proves the derived assertion.

The principal symbol is precisely that of the linearized difference
in the definition of an elliptic differential moduli problem.
This is the point at which the matching equation becomes the single
vector-bundle equation used in the remaining proof.

\section{Open covers and parameter families}

The local construction must cover all solutions and remain available
when the equation varies. We first check the covering property over
a point, then describe the additional trivializations needed over a base.

Carry out the construction at every real solution $t$.
It gives open substacks $\Sol(\mathcal E)_t$ and a map
\[
 \coprod_t\Sol(\mathcal E)_t\longrightarrow\Sol(\mathcal E).
\]
This is an effective epimorphism. Indeed, pull it back along a test
family $T\to\Sol(\mathcal E)$. The resulting open subsets of $T$
contain every real point, since the chart centred at its image
contains that solution. They therefore cover $T$.

The open local representability theorem of \Cref{chap:Coherence_Descent_Local_Representability} now reduces
representability to the individual charts.
A local quasi-smooth representing object for each
$\Sol(\mathcal E)_t$ will yield a global quasi-smooth derived scheme.
The original solution stack supplies the overlaps and their identifications.

For a proper submersion $M\to S$ of ordinary manifolds, Ehresmann's
theorem gives local trivializations
\[
 M|_{S_0}\cong S_0\times N
\]
with $N$ compact. The fibre $N$ is fixed in this local discussion,
while the coefficients of the equation vary with $s\in S_0$.

Choose a solution over $s_0$. The family version of the relative
exponential construction gives vector-bundle neighbourhoods over
$S_0\times N$ arising from fixed bundles on $N$, after shrinking
$S_0$. This is
\cite[Lemma~3.2.20]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
The bundles on $N$ need not be trivial. It is their variation with
the parameter that has been locally trivialized.

For a matching problem, these choices reduce the equation to
\[
 \widetilde P\colon S_0\times Q
 \longrightarrow S_0\times\Sec_N(E),
 \qquad(s,v)\longmapsto(s,\widetilde P_s(v)),
\]
with $Q\subseteq\Sec_N(F)$ open and $F,E$ fixed bundles on $N$.
The chosen solution is $(s_0,0)$ and
$\widetilde P_{s_0}(0)=0$.
The zero section need not solve the equation for every other $s$:
the coordinate construction extends the ambient charts, not the chosen
solution as a family of solutions.

The section-stack product identifications used here are
\cite[Lemma~3.2.21]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Compatibility of jets with base change ensures that the transformed
operator remains a relative finite-order operator.
The same open-cospan argument gives an open neighbourhood in the
relative solution stack.

For a smooth stack base, these coordinate reductions are made after
pullback to manifold families. Their use in the global proof will be
explained after the analytic criterion has been established in
\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Derived_Charts}.

\section{Further reading}

Maps to a circle give an explicit version of the exponential-coordinate
construction. The exercises check how these coordinates affect
derivatives and why the section neighbourhoods are open.

\begin{example}[Maps to a circle]
Let $N$ be compact and consider maps $u\colon N\to S^1$.
Fix $u_0$. For a sufficiently small real function $v$,
\[
 u_v(x)=u_0(x)\exp(i v(x))
\]
defines a nearby map. Choosing $|v(x)|<\pi$ gives a unique local
logarithm of $u/u_0$. Thus a neighbourhood of $u_0$ in its mapping
stack is an open part of the section stack of a real line bundle
(in this example the bundle is trivial).

For an operator of order two on these maps, substitute $u_v$ and
differentiate. First and second derivatives of $u_v$ involve only
$v,dv,d^2v$, with coefficients determined by $u_0$.
The transformed operator still has order at most two.
The same reasoning works for a general target manifold using the
exponential map and the bundle $u_0^*TZ$.

This example also shows the locality of the construction. A single
logarithm does not parametrize every map $N\to S^1$.
The representability argument needs one neighbourhood at a time,
and the effective open cover provides the global passage.
\end{example}

\begin{exercise}
In the coordinate formula above, calculate $du_v$ and show directly
that its leading term in a variation of $v$ is multiplication by
a pointwise invertible map followed by $dv$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
Let $p\colon W\subseteq J_N^k(F)\to E$ be a partially defined
operator. Describe the open set of sections on which it is defined,
and explain where compactness of $N$ enters the openness assertion.
\end{exercise}

\cite[Theorem~3.4.3, pp.~66--67]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli},
using Proposition 3.2.19, Lemmas 3.2.20--3.2.21, Proposition 3.3.9,
and Lemma 3.4.4.
