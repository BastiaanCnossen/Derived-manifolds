\chapter{Elliptic representability II: the local Kuranishi criterion}
\label[chapter]{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}

After the local reduction, the equation has the form
\[
 \widetilde P\colon Q\subseteq\Sec_N(F)\longrightarrow\Sec_N(E),
 \qquad \widetilde P(u)=0,
\]
for vector bundles on a closed manifold $N$.
We will add finitely many parameters so that the augmented equation
has an ordinary manifold of solutions. Setting the added parameters
back to zero will then give a finite-dimensional derived zero locus.

The pullback identity is formal, but identifying the augmented derived
zero locus with an ordinary manifold requires more than a description
of its real solutions. We formulate the needed condition as
submersiveness in smooth stacks: every smooth family of right-hand
sides must have a finite-dimensional solution manifold submersive over
its parameter manifold.

We first establish the augmentation identity, then explain how smooth
families of sections connect the analytic setting to section stacks.
The comparison theorem for submersions gives a local Kuranishi
criterion, including its relative form. The next chapter will choose
the auxiliary variables and prove the family condition by elliptic analysis.

\section{Adding auxiliary variables}

Adding a finite-dimensional space of parameters turns the original
problem into a fibre of an augmented problem. We first establish this
identity globally and illustrate it with $x^2$, then restrict it to the
neighbourhoods needed in the analytic argument.

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional vector space and choose a linear map
\[
 \iota\colon V\longrightarrow\Gamma(N;E).
\]
It gives a map $V\to\Sec_N(E)$: a family of vectors is sent to the
corresponding linear combination of fixed smooth sections.
Define
\[
 \overline P\colon Q\times V\longrightarrow\Sec_N(E),
 \qquad\overline P(u,a)=\widetilde P(u)+\iota(a).
\]
Its solution stack has a projection
$\pi\colon\Sol(\overline P)\to V$.

\begin{proposition}[The augmentation identity]
\label[proposition]{prop:Augmentation_Pullback_Identity}
The following square is a pullback in derived stacks:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
 \Sol(\widetilde P) \rar \dar \drar[pullback]
   & \Sol(\overline P) \dar["\pi"] \\
 \{0\} \rar & V.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pullback of $Q\times V\to V$ along the origin is $Q$.
On this pullback, $\overline P$ restricts to $\widetilde P$ because
$\iota(0)=0$. Pulling back the equation $\overline P=0$ along the
same map therefore imposes $\widetilde P=0$.
Associativity of pullbacks gives the displayed isomorphism.
\end{proof}

This identity holds for every choice of augmentation, regardless of
whether $\Sol(\overline P)$ is represented by a manifold.

If it is represented by an ordinary finite-dimensional manifold $Z$,
the projection becomes a smooth map $\kappa\colon Z\to V$, and
\[
 \Sol(\widetilde P)\cong Z\times_V^{\mathrm{der}}\{0\}.
\]
The right side is the Kuranishi presentation $(Z,Z\times V,\kappa)$.
The remaining equation $\kappa=0$ need not be transverse.
It retains the singularities of the original problem.

For example, augment $f(x)=x^2$ to
\[
 \overline f(x,a)=x^2+a\colon\R^2\longrightarrow\R.
\]
Its derivative $(v,b)\mapsto2xv+b$ is surjective everywhere. Its derived
zero locus is therefore the ordinary parabola $Z=\{a=-x^2\}$.
Using $x$ as a coordinate on $Z$, the projection to the auxiliary line
is $\kappa(x)=-x^2$. Its derived zero fibre is the multiple point with
function ring $\R[\delta]/(\delta^2)$ calculated earlier.
The singularity is retained by the projection $\kappa$ from the smooth
augmented zero manifold.

In practice we work on an open neighbourhood $U\subseteq Q\times V$
of a point $(t,0)$. Set
\[
 H=\{u\in Q:(u,0)\in U\}.
\]
This is an open substack of $Q$.
Let $\mathcal Z_U=\Sol(\overline P|_U)$, with its projection to $V$.
The same argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Local_Augmentation_Identity}
 \Sol(\widetilde P|_H)\cong\mathcal Z_U\times_V^{\mathrm{der}}\{0\}.
\end{equation}
If $\mathcal Z_U$ is represented by a finite-dimensional manifold
$Z_U$, this is a Kuranishi chart around $t$.
Using $H$ records exactly which part of the original equation lies
inside the chosen augmented neighbourhood.

\section{From smooth section spaces to section stacks}
\label[section]{sec:Section_Stack_Charts_Twelve}

The local operators can be studied analytically on Fr\'echet spaces
of sections. To use their analytic properties in derived geometry,
we need to identify the associated functors of smooth families and
then their extensions to derived parameters.

For a finite-rank bundle $E$ over compact $N$, the space $\Gamma(N;E)$
has seminorms measuring all derivatives:
\[
 p_r(u)=\max_{0\leq j\leq r}\sup_{x\in N}|\nabla^j u(x)|.
\]
They give a complete metrizable locally convex topology, hence a
Fr\'echet space. Choices of connection and metrics give the same
topology. There is generally no single norm controlling all derivatives.

Fr\'echet spaces are examples of \emph{convenient vector spaces}.
One characterization of convenience is that every smooth curve admits
a smooth antiderivative. Smooth maps in the convenient calculus can
be tested on smooth curves. For Fr\'echet spaces this agrees with
the usual smooth calculus based on jointly continuous iterated
directional derivatives.

A convenient manifold is locally modelled on suitable open subsets
of convenient vector spaces, with smooth transition maps.
Only Fr\'echet section spaces, their open subsets, and Banach Sobolev
manifolds will occur below.

A convenient manifold $B$ determines a smooth stack
\[
 j_{\mathrm{Con}}(B)(T)=C^\infty(T,B),
 \qquad T\in\mathrm{Mfd}.
\]
This is the restricted Yoneda functor. It is fully faithful and preserves
all limits that exist in convenient manifolds
\cite[Proposition~3.1.7]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
These limits are preserved in \emph{smooth stacks}.
After further inclusion into derived stacks, arbitrary pullbacks
need not be preserved.

The exponential law says that a smooth map
$T\to\Gamma(N;E)$ is exactly a jointly smooth section on $T\times N$.
Consequently,
\[
 j_{\mathrm{Con}}\Gamma(N;E)\cong\Sec_N(E)_{\mathrm{Sm}}.
\]
This identifies the analytic notion of a smooth family of sections
with the smooth mapping stack
\cite[Proposition~3.1.9]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

We also need the identification on derived parameters:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Section_Stack_Convenient_Comparison}
 j_!j_{\mathrm{Con}}\Gamma(N;E)\cong\Sec_N(E).
\end{equation}
It uses the additional theorem that this derived section stack is smooth
\cite[Lemma~3.2.11]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
The ordinary exponential law alone would not imply that assertion.

The same comparison applies to the open sets of sections produced in
\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models}. Thus $Q$, $U$, and their operators can be discussed both
as convenient manifolds and as smooth stacks, with the derived
incarnations specified by \Cref{eq:Section_Stack_Convenient_Comparison}.

\section{Submersions and the comparison of pullbacks}

The inclusion of smooth stacks into derived stacks does not preserve
every pullback. Submersiveness gives a sufficient condition. Its
definition is phrased in terms of families so that the analytic
argument can verify it using ordinary parameter manifolds.

For ordinary manifolds, a smooth map is a submersion if its differential
is surjective everywhere. The implicit function theorem identifies it
locally with a projection. Its pullback along any smooth map from
another manifold is again a manifold, and the resulting projection
is a submersion.

\begin{definition}[Submersiveness in smooth stacks]
\label[definition]{def:Submersive_Smooth_Stack_Map}
A morphism $\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ of smooth stacks is submersive if,
for every ordinary manifold $T$ and map $T\to\mathcal B$, the pullback
\[
 T\times_{\mathcal B}^{\mathrm{Sm}}\mathcal A
\]
is represented by an ordinary finite-dimensional manifold whose map
to $T$ is a submersion.
\end{definition}

The superscript indicates that the pullback is computed in smooth
stacks. It will be useful when comparing categories.
A submersion need not be surjective; the definition permits an empty
fibre. The finite-dimensionality requirement is substantial when
$\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ arise from infinite-dimensional manifolds.

There is an analogous definition for derived stacks.
After base change to a derived affine test $T$, a submersive morphism
must be represented by a derived submersion over $T$, locally a
projection with a finite-dimensional smooth manifold factor.
The following comparison makes it possible to test this property using
ordinary parameters.

\begin{theorem}[Comparison along submersions]
\label[theorem]{thm:Smooth_Derived_Submersion_Comparison}
A morphism of smooth stacks is submersive in smooth stacks if and
only if it is submersive after inclusion into derived smooth stacks.
If $\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ is such a morphism and
$\mathcal C\to\mathcal B$ is a map of smooth stacks, then
\[
 j_!\bigl(\mathcal A\times_{\mathcal B}^{\mathrm{Sm}}\mathcal C\bigr)
 \cong
 j_!\mathcal A\times_{j_!\mathcal B}^{\mathrm{der}}j_!\mathcal C.
\]
\end{theorem}

These are the relevant consequences of
\cite[Propositions~2.2.17--2.2.18 and Corollary~2.2.19]
{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Locally a submersion is a projection, and inclusion of smooth stacks
preserves products. Open descent then permits these local comparisons
to be glued. The cited propositions carry this reasoning out for
stack bases and verify the test-object criterion.

In particular, the derived fibre of a submersive map over an ordinary
point is the ordinary smooth fibre. This is the mechanism that will
make the augmented zero locus finite-dimensional and ordinary.
The final fibre over $0\in V$ is a different pullback, for which
submersiveness of $\kappa$ is not assumed.

\section{The local Kuranishi criterion}

The section-stack identification and the comparison along submersions
now combine with the augmentation identity. We state the criterion
over a point, spell out the analytic condition it requires, and then
retain a parameter base in the same construction.

Consider the convenient smooth map
\[
 G=\overline P|_U\colon U\longrightarrow\Gamma(N;E).
\]
Via the preceding identifications it induces the augmented operator
on section stacks.

\begin{theorem}[Local Kuranishi criterion]
\label[theorem]{thm:Local_Kuranishi_Criterion}
Suppose that the morphism
\[
 j_{\mathrm{Con}}G\colon j_{\mathrm{Con}}U
 \longrightarrow j_{\mathrm{Con}}\Gamma(N;E)
\]
is submersive as a map of smooth stacks.
Then its derived zero fibre is represented by an ordinary
finite-dimensional manifold $Z_U$.
Projection onto $V$ defines a Kuranishi map
$\kappa\colon Z_U\to V$, and
\[
 \Sol(\widetilde P|_H)\cong
 Z_U\times_V^{\mathrm{der}}\{0\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The smooth-stack pullback along $0\colon *\to\Gamma(N;E)$ is represented
by an ordinary finite-dimensional manifold by the definition of
submersiveness. The comparison theorem identifies its image in
derived stacks with the derived zero fibre of $G$.
Now apply \Cref{eq:Local_Augmentation_Identity}.
\end{proof}

This isolates the sufficient condition to be proved by analysis.
Explicitly, for every ordinary manifold $T$ and smooth family
$h\colon T\to\Gamma(N;E)$, the smooth-stack pullback
\[
 T\times_{\Gamma(N;E)}^{\mathrm{Sm}}U
\]
must be a finite-dimensional manifold submersive over $T$.
Its equation is
\[
 \widetilde P(u)+\iota(a)=h(t).
\]
Checking only $h=0$ establishes one fibre. The universal condition
on $T,h$ is what proves submersiveness and permits the comparison
with derived pullbacks.

It is also stronger than surjectivity of the derivative as a map of
Fr\'echet spaces. General Fr\'echet calculus has no implicit function
theorem with that hypothesis alone. The next chapter uses Sobolev
completions and elliptic regularity to prove the stated family condition.

Let $S$ now be an ordinary manifold, with local operator
$\widetilde P_s$ as in \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models}.
Take an open neighbourhood
$U\subseteq S\times Q\times V$ and form the map over $S$
\[
 G(s,u,a)=\bigl(s,\widetilde P_s(u)+\iota(a)\bigr)
 \colon U\longrightarrow S\times\Gamma(N;E).
\]
Suppose it is submersive as a map of smooth stacks.
Its pullback along the zero section
$S\to S\times\Gamma(N;E)$ is an ordinary manifold
$Z_U$ submersive over $S$.
The auxiliary-variable projection gives a map over $S$
\[
 \kappa\colon Z_U\longrightarrow S\times V.
\]
The local original solution stack is
\[
 Z_U\times_{S\times V}^{\mathrm{der}}S.
\]

This is relatively quasi-smooth over $S$.
Indeed, $Z_U\to S$ is a submersion, so its relative cotangent bundle
is in degree zero. Imposing the $V$-valued equation gives a
two-term relative cotangent complex in degrees $1,0$.
It is therefore a relative Kuranishi presentation.

The resulting zero locus identifies an open substack of the original
solution stack. This is the local output needed for globalization.
It remains conditional on submersiveness of the augmented operator;
the next chapter proves that condition and then assembles the open atlas.

The pullback argument is the final part of the proof of
\cite[Theorem~3.4.3, following claim~$(*)$]
{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
At this point the remaining task is to choose $V,\iota,U$ for which
the submersiveness hypothesis holds.

\section{Further reading}

Two comparisons clarify the criterion: the weaker notion of stacky
submersiveness explains why finite-dimensional fibres must be required,
and a linear example isolates the role of the auxiliary variables.

Steffens also uses the weaker term \emph{stacky submersive}.
Its local models, after stacky \'etale changes, are projections
away from an arbitrary smooth stack, rather than necessarily
a finite-dimensional manifold
\cite[Definition~2.2.16]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
His pullback-preservation theorem holds in that greater generality.
For the Kuranishi criterion, however, we need the representable
submersive condition, since the augmented fibre must be an ordinary
finite-dimensional manifold.

The distinction is visible in the projection
$j_{\mathrm{Con}}\Gamma(N;E)\to*$.
It is stacky submersive, as a projection away from a smooth stack,
but an infinite-dimensional section space is not a finite-dimensional
manifold. Stacky submersiveness alone cannot give the required
Kuranishi chart.

\begin{example}[The finite-dimensional linear analogue]
The augmentation construction has a finite-dimensional analogue.
For $f\colon A\to B$ between vector spaces, choose $\iota\colon V\to B$
surjecting onto $\operatorname{coker}f$. Then
\[
 f+\iota\colon A\oplus V\longrightarrow B
\]
is surjective. Its kernel $Z$ is a vector space, and the derived
zero locus of $Z\to V$ recovers the derived zero fibre of $f$.
The elliptic argument is a nonlinear version of this construction,
with analysis needed to justify the ordinary manifold $Z$.
\end{example}

\begin{exercise}
For a linear map $f\colon A\to B$ and the surjective augmentation above,
prove that the tangent complex $[Z\to V]$ is quasi-isomorphic to
$[A\to B]$ in degrees $0,-1$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
Explain why the pullback of $x^2\colon\R\to\R$ along zero in smooth
stacks is different from its pullback in derived stacks.
Locate exactly which hypothesis of the comparison theorem fails.
\end{exercise}
