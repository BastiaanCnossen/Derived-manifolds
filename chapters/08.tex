\chapter{Derived bordism and fundamental classes}
\label[chapter]{chap:Derived_Bordism_And_Fundamental_Classes}

A quasi-smooth derived manifold is locally the zero locus of a section of a
vector bundle. Its tangent complex records both the infinitesimal solutions
and the failure of transversality. A transverse perturbation usually changes
the derived manifold, and can change even its underlying topological space.
It nevertheless preserves a useful invariant: the bordism class.

We begin with a homotopy of sections, whose derived zero locus gives a
cobordism between the endpoint zero loci. The global normal form of
\Cref{thm:Global_Normal_Form_Affine} extends this construction to all
compact quasi-smooth derived manifolds, and yields the comparison with
classical bordism. Derived intersections then represent intersection
products without a transversality hypothesis, and the self-intersection of
a zero section represents an Euler class. Finally, we explain how bordism
classes give homology classes and where orientations enter.

Throughout this chapter we restrict to quasi-smooth objects of
$\mathrm{DMfd}$. Their underlying spaces are Hausdorff and second
countable, and compactness refers to the underlying topological space.
References to Spivak use the numbering of his published
\emph{Duke Mathematical Journal} article. He proves his results for any category
which is ``good for doing intersection theory on manifolds''
\cite[Definition~2.1]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}, and verifies these
axioms for his own model in \cite[Theorem~9.6]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.
Within these separation conventions, the two settings describe the same
compact derived manifolds. A compact derived manifold in Spivak's model
is a global derived zero locus
\cite[Proposition~3.3 and Definition~2.1(7)]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds},
and every quasi-smooth object of $\mathrm{DMfd}$ is one by
\Cref{thm:Global_Normal_Form_Affine}. Such a zero locus is the pullback
of two sections over an ordinary manifold, computed in the ambient category
of locally ringed spaces \cite[Theorem~8.15]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.
Spivak's model is a full subcategory there, and $\mathrm{DMfd}$ identifies
with the full subcategory generated by $\mathrm{Mfd}$ under finite limits and retracts
\cite[Section~5.2, especially Theorem~5.10]
{Carchedi_Steffens_Universal_Derived_Manifolds}, so the mapping animae
agree as well. We prove the results below directly in our setting. Spivak's
model is used only once, for a gluing step in
\Cref{prop:Derived_Cobordism_Equivalence_Relation}.

\section{From perturbations to derived cobordisms}
\label[section]{sec:From_Perturbations_To_Derived_Cobordisms}

A family joining a section to a transverse perturbation is the basic
example of a derived cobordism. We construct this family first, so that
properness and the collar conditions in the definition have a concrete
geometric purpose.

Let $M$ be a manifold, let $q\colon E\to M$ be a rank-$r$ vector bundle,
and let $s\colon M\to E$ be a section. Recall that its derived zero locus is
defined by the pullback square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s) \rar \dar \drar[pullback]
    & M \dar["0"] \\
  M \rar["s"'] & E .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Its underlying set is the ordinary zero set of $s$, but its structure sheaf
retains the equations defining that set.

Choose another section $s'\colon M\to E$, and suppose that there is a
compact subset $K\subseteq M$ such that $s$ and $s'$ agree on $M\smallsetminus K$
and have no zeros there. If $M$ is compact, we may take $K=M$, and the
condition is empty. Choose a smooth function
\[
  \beta\colon\R\longrightarrow[0,1]
\]
which is identically zero on a neighborhood of $(-\infty,0]$ and
identically one on a neighborhood of $[1,\infty)$. Fiberwise affine
combination defines a section
\[
  H\colon\R\times M\longrightarrow\operatorname{pr}_M^*E,
  \qquad
  H(u,m)=(1-\beta(u))s(m)+\beta(u)s'(m).
\]
Its derived zero locus is taken over $\R\times M$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  W \rar \dar \drar[pullback]
    & \R\times M \dar["0"] \\
  \R\times M \rar["H"'] & \operatorname{pr}_M^*E .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Projection to the first factor induces a map
\[
  p\colon W=Z^{\mathrm{der}}(H)\longrightarrow\R.
\]
Both endpoint zero loci occur as fibers of $W$.

\begin{proposition}[A homotopy of sections gives a cobordism]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Homotopy_Of_Sections_Derived_Cobordism}
  With notation as above, $W$ is a quasi-smooth derived manifold of virtual
  dimension $\dim M+1-r$. The map $p\colon W\to\R$ is proper and is a
  product near its fibers over $0$ and $1$. Moreover,
  \[
    W_{p=0}\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s),
    \qquad
    W_{p=1}\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s').
  \]
  If $s'$ is transverse to the zero section, then
  $W_{p=1}\simeq Z(s')$ is the ordinary zero manifold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The defining square presents $W$ as a pullback of ordinary manifolds
  in $\mathrm{DMfd}$. Locally its cotangent complex has the two-term
  bundle presentation of \Cref{thm:Tangent_Complex_Derived_Zero_Locus},
  with ranks $r$ and $\dim M+1$ in degrees $1$ and $0$. This gives
  quasi-smoothness and the stated virtual dimension.

  Pulling the defining square of $W$ back along $\{0\}\to\R$ replaces $H$
  by $s$, so
  \[
    W_{p=0}\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s).
  \]
  The same argument at $1$ gives $W_{p=1}\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s')$. If
  $s'$ is transverse to the zero section, its derived zero locus is the
  ordinary zero manifold $Z(s')$.

  Choose $\varepsilon>0$ so that $\beta$ is zero on
  $(-\infty,\varepsilon)$ and one on $(1-\varepsilon,\infty)$. Over these
  two intervals the section $H$ is pulled back from $s$, respectively $s'$,
  along the projection to $M$. Pasting pullback squares gives
  \[
    W|_{(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
    \simeq
    Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
  \]
  and
  \[
    W|_{(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)}
    \simeq
    Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s')\times(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon).
  \]

  It remains to check properness. Let $L\subset\R$ be compact. The
  underlying space of $p^{-1}(L)$ is the ordinary zero set of $H$ inside
  $L\times M$. It is closed because the zero section of the vector bundle
  is closed. Outside $L\times K$ we have $H(u,m)=s(m)\neq0$, since $s$ and
  $s'$ agree and have no zeros on $M\smallsetminus K$. Hence this zero set is a
  closed subset of the compact set $L\times K$, and so it is compact. Thus
  $p$ is proper.
\end{proof}

The proof separates the two roles of the hypotheses. The constancy of the
homotopy near $0$ and $1$ supplies product neighborhoods of the endpoint
fibers. The compact set $K$ prevents zeros from escaping to infinity while
the section is perturbed.

The preceding construction contains every clause in the general definition.

\begin{definition}[Collared map]
  \label[definition]{def:Collared_Map_Derived_Manifolds}
  Let $p\colon W\to\R$ be a map of derived manifolds and let $i\in\R$. We say
  that $p$ is \emph{$i$-collared} if there is an $\varepsilon>0$ and an
  isomorphism over $(i-\varepsilon,i+\varepsilon)$
  \[
    W|_{|p-i|<\varepsilon}
    \simeq
    W_{p=i}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon).
  \]
  On the right, the interval is translated so that its origin maps to $i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Derived cobordism]
  \label[definition]{def:Derived_Cobordism}
  Let $X_0$ and $X_1$ be compact quasi-smooth derived manifolds. A
  \emph{derived cobordism} from $X_0$ to $X_1$ consists of a quasi-smooth
  derived manifold $W$ and a proper map
  \[
    p\colon W\longrightarrow\R
  \]
  such that:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The map $p$ is $0$-collared and $1$-collared.
    \item There are isomorphisms
      \[
        W_{p=0}\simeq X_0,
        \qquad
        W_{p=1}\simeq X_1.
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
  If the objects carry maps to a manifold $T$, a derived cobordism
  \emph{over $T$} also carries a continuous map from the underlying space of
  $W$ to $T$ which restricts to the endpoint maps.
\end{definition}

This is Spivak's boundaryless formulation of cobordism
\cite[Definitions~3.5--3.6]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}. Spivak
allows $T$ to be any CW complex; we only need manifolds, which allows a
tubular-neighborhood argument below. The portion over $[0,1]$ is compact,
and its collars allow it to be joined to another cobordism along an
endpoint.

\begin{corollary}[Homotopic sections]
  \label[corollary]{cor:Homotopic_Sections_Derived_Cobordant}
  Let $M$ be compact and let $s_0,s_1$ be sections of the same vector bundle
  over $M$. Then their derived zero loci are derived cobordant. If $s_1$ is
  transverse, then $Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s_0)$ is derived cobordant to the
  ordinary manifold $Z(s_1)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Apply \Cref{prop:Homotopy_Of_Sections_Derived_Cobordism} with $K=M$.
\end{proof}

\section{Classical and derived bordism}
\label[section]{sec:Classical_And_Derived_Bordism}

The homotopy construction applies directly to a single bundle section.
To compare bordism theories, we need global equations for an arbitrary
compact quasi-smooth derived manifold. After stating the comparison,
we explain this geometric input and use perturbations to prove both
surjectivity and injectivity.

Let $T$ be a manifold. Write $\Omega_d(T)$ for the ordinary unoriented
bordism group of continuous maps from compact $d$-manifolds without boundary
to $T$, with compact bordisms over $T$. Equivalently,
$\Omega_*(T)$ is the homology theory represented by $MO$. Define
$\Omega_d^{\mathrm{der}}(T)$ in the same way using compact quasi-smooth
derived manifolds of virtual dimension $d$ and derived cobordisms over $T$.
Disjoint union gives the group operation.

The inclusion of ordinary manifolds into derived manifolds induces a map
\[
  i_*
  \colon
  \Omega_*(T)\longrightarrow\Omega_*^{\mathrm{der}}(T).
\]
At first sight the target might be larger. Derived manifolds provide many new
representatives, including singular zero loci and nontransverse
intersections. The comparison theorem says that these representatives do not
produce new bordism classes.

\begin{theorem}[Spivak's bordism comparison]
  \label[theorem]{thm:Spivak_Derived_Bordism_Comparison}
  For every manifold $T$, inclusion induces an isomorphism
  \[
    i_*
    \colon
    \Omega_*(T)
    \iso
    \Omega_*^{\mathrm{der}}(T).
  \]
\end{theorem}

The absolute and relative assertions are, respectively,
\citet[Theorem~3.12 and Corollary~3.13]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.

The proof reduces the general case to the construction from the first
section. The geometric input is the global normal form.

\begin{corollary}[Global zero-locus presentation]
  \label[corollary]{cor:Compact_Derived_Manifold_Global_Zero_Locus}
  Let $X$ be a compact quasi-smooth derived manifold. Then there are an open
  subset $U\subseteq\R^N$, a finite-rank vector bundle $E\to U$ and a
  section $s\colon U\to E$ with
  \[
    X\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s).
  \]
  The underlying space of $X$ is then the zero set of $s$, which is a
  compact subset of $U$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The presentation is \Cref{thm:Global_Normal_Form_Affine}, which does not
  require compactness. The underlying space of $Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)$ is the
  zero set of $s$. It is compact because $X$ is.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Spivak's route to the presentation]
  \label[remark]{rem:Spivak_Embedding_Normal_Bundle}
  In Spivak's axiomatic setting, the same presentation is obtained in two
  steps. A compact derived manifold embeds in some $\R^N$
  \cite[Proposition~3.3]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}: one takes
  finitely many local zero-locus charts and extends their coordinate
  functions with cutoffs. The cutoffs and coordinates separate points, and
  near each point one chart still supplies its original coordinates, so
  the resulting map is locally a derived embedding. The normal-bundle axiom
  \cite[Definition~2.1(7)]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds} then collects
  the local equations into a section of one vector bundle on a neighborhood
  of the image. In our affine setting, the embedding comes for free from
  generators of $\pi_0$ of the function ring, and the normal form of
  \Cref{chap:Local_Derived_Geometry_Kuranishi_Charts} replaces the
  normal-bundle axiom.
\end{remark}

We will use a relative form of transversality twice. Suppose $s$ is already
transverse to the zero section outside the interior of a compact set
$K\subset U$. Its nontransverse zeros form a compact subset of
$\operatorname{int}K$. Cover them by finitely many open sets on each of
which a bump function is identically one. Choose each bump supported in
a bundle chart inside $\operatorname{int}K$. Multiply the local frame by
the bump, extend by zero, and add arbitrary linear combinations of these
sections to $s$. On the union of the chosen open sets, the parameter
derivatives span each fiber. Parametric transversality therefore gives
regular parameters arbitrarily close to zero
\cite[Chapter~3, \S2, Theorem~2.7]{Hirsch_Differential_Topology}.
On the compact remainder of $K$, including the transition regions outside
this union, $s$ is already transverse. Transversality there is open in
the $C^1$ topology, so choosing a sufficiently small regular parameter
preserves it. Outside $K$ the section is fixed. We obtain a transverse
section $s'$ with perturbation supported in $\operatorname{int}K$.

In particular, when $X=Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)$ is compact, we may take $K$
to be a compact neighborhood of its entire zero set. The sections $s,s'$
then satisfy the hypotheses of \Cref{prop:Homotopy_Of_Sections_Derived_Cobordism}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:Spivak_Derived_Bordism_Comparison}]
  We first prove surjectivity in the absolute case. Let $X$ be a compact
  quasi-smooth derived manifold. Choose a global zero-locus presentation
  \[
    X\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)
  \]
  as in \Cref{cor:Compact_Derived_Manifold_Global_Zero_Locus}, and a
  transverse section $s'$ which agrees with $s$ outside a compact
  neighborhood $K$ of the zero locus, as above.
  \Cref{prop:Homotopy_Of_Sections_Derived_Cobordism} gives a derived
  cobordism
  \[
    X\sim_{\mathrm{bord}}Z(s').
  \]
  The endpoint $Z(s')$ is an ordinary compact manifold. Hence every derived
  bordism class is in the image of $i_*$.

  For injectivity, suppose that ordinary compact manifolds $M_0$ and $M_1$
  are derived cobordant, and let
  \[
    p\colon W\longrightarrow\R
  \]
  be a derived cobordism between them. We first present $W$ so that $p$
  becomes a coordinate. By \Cref{thm:Global_Normal_Form_Affine},
  $W\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(\sigma)$ for a section $\sigma$ of a bundle
  $F\to V$ over an open subset $V\subseteq\R^n$. The degree-zero homology
  of the Koszul model in \Cref{prop:Vector_Bundle_Koszul_Presentation} gives
  \[
    \pi_0\mathcal O(W)\cong
    \Cinfty(V)\big/\{\lambda(\sigma):\lambda\in\Gamma(V,F^\vee)\}.
  \]
  Thus $[p]\in\pi_0\Hom(W,\R)=\pi_0\mathcal O(W)$ lifts to a smooth function
  $g\colon V\to\R$. Choose a path from $g|_W$ to $p$ in $\Hom(W,\R)$.
  On $V\times\R$, first imposing $t-g(v)=0$ cuts out the ordinary graph
  of $g$ transversely. Imposing $\sigma=0$ on the graph then recovers $W$.
  Pasting the two pullbacks gives
  \[
    W\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s),
    \qquad
    s(v,t)=\bigl(\sigma(v),\,t-g(v)\bigr),
  \]
  where $s$ is a section of $\operatorname{pr}_V^*F\oplus\underline{\R}$.
  The coordinate $t$ restricts to $g|_W$, so the chosen path makes this
  an isomorphism over $\R$.

  Choose $\varepsilon>0$ within both collars, and replace the ambient
  manifold by $U=V\times(-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)$. Over the two collar
  intervals, $W$ is the ordinary manifold $M_i\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$
  (suitably translated). Its tangent complexes satisfy
  $H_{-1}(T_xW)=0$, so $s$ is transverse to the zero section at every zero
  over the collars. Choose $0<\delta<\min(\varepsilon,1/2)$. By properness of $p$, the
  zero set of $s$ over $[\delta,1-\delta]$ is compact. Choose a compact
  neighborhood $K$ of it inside $V\times(\delta/2,1-\delta/2)$. Every zero
  of $s$ outside the interior of $K$ lies over a collar, so $s$ is
  transverse on $U\smallsetminus\operatorname{int}K$. Relative transversality
  gives a transverse section $s'$ which agrees with $s$ outside $K$.

  The ordinary manifold $Z(s')$ agrees with $W$ on the smaller collars of
  radius $\delta/2$. Thus $0$ and $1$ are regular values of $t$, with fibers
  $M_0$ and $M_1$. Outside $K$ every zero of $s'$ is a zero of $s$, so
  \[
    Z(s')\cap t^{-1}([0,1])\ \subseteq\ K\cup p^{-1}([0,1]),
  \]
  where we view the underlying space of $p^{-1}([0,1])$ in the graph
  presentation. The right side is compact by properness and the left side
  is closed. Thus $Z(s')\cap\{0\le t\le1\}$ is an ordinary compact cobordism
  from $M_0$ to $M_1$. Two ordinary manifolds which become derived
  cobordant were therefore already classically cobordant, and $i_*$ is
  injective.

  For the relative theorem over $T$, the perturbation must also give a
  bordism over $T$. To see this, observe that a
  continuous map from the closed zero set $Z\subseteq U$ to the manifold $T$
  extends to a neighborhood of $Z$ in $U$. To see this, embed $T$ in a
  Euclidean space with a tubular-neighborhood retraction. Tietze extension
  extends the coordinate functions of the given map to $U$. The points
  sent into the tubular neighborhood form an open neighborhood of $Z$, and
  composing with the retraction gives the required extension. Choosing the
  compact sets $K$ inside this neighborhood, the perturbation and cobordism
  can then be carried out over $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A singular fiber of a Morse function]
  \label[example]{ex:Figure_Eight_Derived_Bordism}
  Let $f\colon T^2\to\R$ be a Morse function with exactly four critical
  points and distinct critical values, and let $c$ be its lower saddle
  value. The underlying space of the derived fiber
  \[
    T^2\mathbin{\times_{\R}^{\mathrm{der}}}\{c\}
    =Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f-c)
  \]
  is a figure eight. It is not the underlying space of a one-dimensional
  manifold. Choose $\delta>0$ so that $c$ is the only critical value in
  $[c-\delta,c+\delta]$. Applying
  \Cref{prop:Homotopy_Of_Sections_Derived_Cobordism} on $M=T^2$ with
  $s=f-c$ and $s'=f-c\mp\delta$ gives derived cobordisms from the singular
  fiber to the regular fibers $f^{-1}(c\pm\delta)$. One of these is one
  circle and the other is two circles. Thus the two regular fibers are
  classically cobordant, while the singular derived fiber remains a
  distinct geometric object. This is one of Spivak's examples
  \cite[Example~2.7]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.
\end{example}

\section{Intersection products and Euler classes}
\label[section]{sec:Intersection_Products_And_Euler_Classes}

The comparison theorem lets us compute a classical bordism class using
a derived representative. We apply it first to intersections, then to
the self-intersection of a zero section. The trivial and M\"obius line
bundles illustrate how the resulting class detects global bundle data.

Let $M$ be a closed $m$-manifold, and let $A,B\subseteq M$ be closed
submanifolds of dimensions $a$ and $b$. We use cohomological notation for
their Poincar\'e-dual unoriented bordism classes:
\[
  [A]\in MO^{\operatorname{codim}(A)}(M),
  \qquad
  [B]\in MO^{\operatorname{codim}(B)}(M).
\]
Bordism Poincar\'e duality identifies these cohomology classes with the
homology classes of the compact manifolds $A$ and $B$ over $M$. Likewise,
a compact quasi-smooth derived manifold $X$ of virtual dimension $d$ with a
map to $M$ has a class in $\Omega^{\mathrm{der}}_d(M)\cong MO_d(M)$, and we
write $[X]\in MO^{m-d}(M)$ for its Poincar\'e dual.

If $A$ and $B$ are transverse, their ordinary intersection represents the
cup product. Derived geometry removes the transversality condition from the
geometric representative.

\begin{theorem}[General cup-product formula]
  \label[theorem]{thm:General_Cup_Product_Derived_Intersection}
  Let $A$ and $B$ be closed submanifolds of a closed manifold $M$. Then
  \[
    [A]\smile[B]
    =
    [A\mathbin{\times_M^{\mathrm{der}}}B]
  \]
  in $MO^{2m-a-b}(M)$, where the right-hand side is the Poincar\'e dual of
  the class of $A\mathbin{\times_M^{\mathrm{der}}}B\to M$ in
  $MO_{a+b-m}(M)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Choose a smooth homotopy $h\colon\R\times A\to M$ which is constant in the
  parameter on $(-\infty,\varepsilon)$ and on $(1-\varepsilon,\infty)$, such
  that $h_0$ is the inclusion and $h_1$ is transverse to $B$. Write
  $A'\to M$ for $h_1$. Set
  \[
    W=(\R\times A)\mathbin{\times_M^{\mathrm{der}}}B,
  \]
  with $p\colon W\to\R$ the projection and the map to $M$ through $B$. The
  underlying space of $p^{-1}(L)$ is closed in $L\times A\times B$, so $p$ is
  proper; it is collared because $h$ is constant near the endpoints. Base
  change identifies its fibers over $0$ and $1$ with
  \[
    A\mathbin{\times_M^{\mathrm{der}}}B
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    A'\pitchfork B,
  \]
  the latter being the ordinary transverse fiber product. Hence the two
  objects determine the same class over $M$. The classical transverse
  intersection formula gives
  \[
    [A]\smile[B]=[A'\pitchfork B].
  \]
  Combining the two equalities proves the result.
\end{proof}

This is \citet[Corollary~3.13]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.

Let $M$ be a closed $m$-manifold and let $q\colon E\to M$ be a rank-$r$
vector bundle. Write $z\colon M\to E$ for the zero section. Its derived
self-intersection is
\[
  X_E
  =
  M\mathbin{\times_E^{\mathrm{der}}}M
  =
  Z^{\mathrm{der}}(z).
\]
The two maps to $E$ in this expression are both $z$. The underlying space
of $X_E$ is all of $M$. Since the derivative of $z$ in the fiber direction
vanishes, the tangent complex at $x$ is
\[
  \bigl[T_xM\xrightarrow{\;0\;}E_x\bigr],
\]
so the whole of $E$ appears as obstruction bundle.

Choose a transverse section $s'\colon M\to E$. Its graph is a perturbation of
one copy of the zero section. The transverse intersection of $z(M)$ and
$s'(M)$ is precisely the zero manifold $Z(s')$.
\Cref{cor:Homotopic_Sections_Derived_Cobordant} gives a derived cobordism
over $M$
\[
  X_E
  \sim_{\mathrm{bord}}
  Z(s').
\]

The bordism Euler class
\[
  e_{MO}(E)\in MO^r(M)
\]
is represented by the zero locus of a transverse section. We have proved the
following self-intersection formula.

\begin{corollary}[Zero-section self-intersection]
  \label[corollary]{cor:Zero_Section_Self_Intersection_Euler_Class}
  The cohomological bordism class represented by the derived
  self-intersection of the zero section is the Euler class:
  \[
    [M\mathbin{\times_E^{\mathrm{der}}}M]
    =
    e_{MO}(E).
  \]
  In homological notation, the corresponding class over $M$ is
  \[
    e_{MO}(E)\cap[M]
    =
    [Z(s')\longrightarrow M]
    \in MO_{m-r}(M).
  \]
\end{corollary}

The derived self-intersection is canonical, whereas the transverse section
$s'$ is a choice. Any two choices are joined by a homotopy of sections, so
their zero manifolds are cobordant. Derived geometry packages the class before
the choice of perturbation is made.

\begin{example}[The trivial line bundle]
  \label[example]{ex:Trivial_Line_Derived_Self_Intersection}
  Let $E=M\times\R$ be the trivial line bundle, so that the zero section is
  the zero function. Its derived self-intersection is
  \[
    X_{\underline{\R}}
    =
    M\mathbin{\times_{M\times\R}^{\mathrm{der}}}M.
  \]
  By \Cref{prop:Vector_Bundle_Koszul_Presentation}, its function ring is
  presented globally by the Koszul algebra
  \[
    \Cinfty(M)[e],
    \qquad
    |e|=1,
    \qquad
    de=0.
  \]
  Thus its underlying space and classical truncation are both $M$, while
  $\pi_1\cong\Cinfty(M)\,e$ is free of rank one over
  $\pi_0\cong\Cinfty(M)$. Its tangent complex is
  $[TM\xrightarrow{0}\underline{\R}]$.

  On the other hand, the zero section can be perturbed to the nowhere-zero
  constant section $1$. Its zero locus is empty. Hence
  \[
    [X_{\underline{\R}}]=0
  \]
  in bordism. The derived object is nonempty and nonclassical, with an
  obstruction line at every point, but its bordism class vanishes.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The M\"obius line bundle]
  \label[example]{ex:Mobius_Line_Derived_Self_Intersection}
  Return to the M\"obius obstruction bundle from
  \Cref{chap:Local_Derived_Geometry_Kuranishi_Charts}.
  Let $L\to S^1$ be this line bundle. A section can be described by a
  smooth function $f\colon\R\to\R$ satisfying
  \[
    f(t+1)=-f(t).
  \]
  The function $f(t)=\sin(\pi t)$ defines a transverse section. Modulo the
  identification $t\sim t+1$, it has one zero. Therefore the derived
  self-intersection of the zero section is derived cobordant to one point
  over $S^1$. Its mod-$2$ Euler number is nonzero.

  This complements the trivial-line example. For the trivial line, a
  nowhere-zero section makes the self-intersection class vanish. For the
  M\"obius line, every section has a zero and the transverse zero set
  detects the nontrivial unoriented Euler class.
\end{example}

\section{Fundamental classes}
\label[section]{sec:Fundamental_Classes_And_Their_Limits}

A compact derived manifold now determines a bordism class. Passing to
a chosen homology theory gives a fundamental class over a manifold
target. We describe the unoriented construction first, then the
orientation data needed for an integral class.

Let $X$ be a compact quasi-smooth derived manifold of virtual dimension $d$.
The comparison theorem supplies a class
\[
  [X]\in\Omega_d^{\mathrm{der}}
  \cong
  \Omega_d
  =MO_d.
\]
A continuous map from this compact $X$ to a manifold $T$ supplies a class
in $MO_d(T)$.

Suppose that a generalized homology theory $\mathbb E_*$ is equipped with a
morphism
\[
  MO\longrightarrow\mathbb E
\]
of spectra representing the two theories. This induces a
natural transformation
\[
  MO_*(T)\longrightarrow\mathbb E_*(T).
\]

\begin{definition}[Generalized fundamental class]
  \label[definition]{def:Generalized_Fundamental_Class_Derived_Manifold}
  The image of $[X\to T]$ under this natural transformation is the
  $\mathbb E$-homology fundamental class of $X$ over $T$:
  \[
    [X\to T]_{\mathbb E}
    \in
    \mathbb E_d(T).
  \]
\end{definition}

For example, the Thom orientation $MO\to H\mathbf F_2$ gives a
mod-$2$ homology class. A transverse perturbation computes this class by the
usual fundamental class of its zero manifold.

For an integral fundamental class, one needs an orientation. Spivak
defines an orientation of a compact derived manifold to be an orientation
of a normal bundle supplied by the normal-bundle axiom
\cite[Remark~3.8]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}. For a presentation
$X\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)$ with $E\to U\subseteq\R^N$, this is an
orientation of the real vector bundle $E|_{|X|}$. Spivak asserts the
oriented comparison theorem in the same remark and omits its proof.
Granting this extension, a morphism from $MSO$ to $\mathbb E$ transports the
oriented bordism class to $\mathbb E$-homology.

Orientations can be described intrinsically. The virtual tangent bundle
on the classical truncation of $X=Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)$ is represented
by the difference of $TU$ and $E$ restricted to $t_0X$. Its determinant line is
\[
  \bigl(\det(TU)\otimes\det(E)^\vee\bigr)|_{t_0X}.
\]
The standard orientation of $U\subseteq\R^N$ identifies orientations of
$E|_{|X|}$ with orientations of this line on $|X|$. Applying the
determinant functor to the intrinsic tangent complex restricted to $t_0X$
shows that the line is independent of the presentation
\cite[Theorem~2]{Knudsen_Mumford_Projectivity_Stable_Curves_I}.
For example, stabilizing a chart by a new variable
together with that variable as a new equation contributes an identity
complex, whose determinant has its canonical trivialization.

For a moduli problem, this construction applies once its representing
quasi-smooth derived manifold has been shown to be compact and supplied with
the desired orientation. Compactifications and objects with automorphisms
require further geometric input.

\section{Further remarks and exercises}
\label[section]{sec:Derived_Bordism_Summary_And_Supplements}

The remaining details explain why cobordisms compose and how the
comparison theorem relates to the Pontryagin--Thom construction. Two
exercises then return to the examples of this chapter.

\begin{proposition}
  \label[proposition]{prop:Derived_Cobordism_Equivalence_Relation}
  Derived cobordism is an equivalence relation on compact quasi-smooth
  derived manifolds, also over a fixed manifold $T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Reflexivity is represented by the projection
  \[
    X\times\R\longrightarrow\R.
  \]
  This map is proper when $X$ is compact, is collared everywhere, and has
  fiber $X$ over both $0$ and $1$. Over $T$ we compose the other
  projection $|X|\times\R\to |X|$ with the given map $|X|\to T$.

  Symmetry follows by reversing the parameter. If $p\colon W\to\R$ is a
  cobordism from $X_0$ to $X_1$, then $1-p$ is a cobordism from $X_1$ to
  $X_0$.

  For transitivity, write $p_{01}\colon W_{01}\to\R$ and
  $p_{12}\colon W_{12}\to\R$ for two cobordisms. Translate the second
  parameter by $1$, so that their common fiber $X_1$ lies over $1$. Choose
  $0<\varepsilon<1/2$ within both collars. Take the part
  $p_{01}<1+\varepsilon/2$ of the first cobordism and the part
  $p_{12}>1-\varepsilon/2$ of the second. Over
  $(1-\varepsilon/2,1+\varepsilon/2)$ both are identified with
  $X_1\times(-\varepsilon/2,\varepsilon/2)$ by the collars, and we glue
  along these identifications. Every time has a neighborhood whose
  preimage is the restriction of one of the two original families.
  Points over distinct times are separated in $\R$, and points over
  the same time are separated in such a restriction. Thus the glued
  underlying space is Hausdorff. The map to $\R$ is proper too, since
  properness is local on the target.

  Over $T$, write the two maps on the larger collars as $a_i(x,t)$,
  using the translated time $t$ on both. They agree at $t=1$.
  Choose a continuous function $\rho$ on $(1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)$
  equal to $1$ on the smaller collar and equal to $t$ near the two ends.
  Replacing $a_i(x,t)$ by $a_i(x,\rho(t))$ on each collar gives maps
  that agree on the overlap and glue to a continuous map to $T$.

  Objects of $\mathrm{DMfd}$ are affine, so the gluing is not a priori
  available there. It is available in Spivak's model
  \cite[Definition~2.1(3) and Proposition~9.2]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}, which
  contains both pieces. Call the result $W'$, with map $p'\colon W'\to\R$.
  Choose a bounded open interval $I$ containing $[0,2]$. Spivak's
  relative embedding theorem requires the compact set $\overline I$ to
  lie in the image of the map \cite[Corollary~3.4]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.
  Apply it to the proper surjection
  $p'\sqcup\mathrm{id}\colon W'\sqcup\R\to\R$. Restricting the resulting
  embedding over $I$ to its open-and-closed summand gives an embedding
  $p'^{-1}(I)\hookrightarrow\R^N\times I$, even if some fibers are empty.
  The normal-bundle axiom presents $p'^{-1}(I)$ as the derived zero locus
  of a section of a vector bundle on a neighborhood of its image.
  This pullback of ordinary manifolds is computed in locally ringed spaces
  by \cite[Theorem~8.15]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}, so it belongs to
  $\mathrm{DMfd}$ by \cite[Theorem~5.10]{Carchedi_Steffens_Universal_Derived_Manifolds}.
  Finally, compose
  with a diffeomorphism $\phi\colon I\to\R$ which is the identity on a
  neighborhood of $[0,2]$. The composite $\phi\circ p'$ is proper, because
  $\phi^{-1}$ of a compact set is a compact subset of $I$ and $p'$ is proper.
  It is collared at $0$ and $2$, with fibers $X_0$ and $X_2$. Dividing the
  parameter by $2$ gives the required cobordism.
\end{proof}

The gluing along collars is the argument of
\citet[Proposition~3.10]{Spivak_Derived_Smooth_Manifolds}.

For an ordinary compact submanifold of $\R^N$, the Pontryagin--Thom
collapse onto a tubular neighborhood gives a map from $S^N$ to the Thom
space of its normal bundle, and hence its class in $MO_*$. The same
construction can be applied to a derived presentation directly, without
choosing a perturbation.

Let $X\simeq Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)$ be compact of virtual dimension $d$, with
$s$ a section of a rank-$r$ bundle $E\to U$ over $U\subseteq\R^N$. Choose
a compact smooth domain $K\subset U$ with $Z(s)$ in its interior, and a
bundle metric on $E$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ so that $\|s\|\ge\epsilon$ on
$\partial K$. Then $x\mapsto s(x)/\max(\epsilon,\|s(x)\|)$ sends $K$
into the unit disk bundle $D(E|_K)$ and its boundary into the unit sphere
bundle $S(E|_K)$. Collapsing the complement of $\operatorname{int}K$ and
then applying this map of pairs gives
\[
  S^N\longrightarrow K/\partial K
  \longrightarrow D(E|_K)/S(E|_K)=\mathrm{Th}(E|_K).
\]
Here a quotient by the empty set adjoins a disjoint basepoint. The
classifying map of $E|_K$ induces a Thom map to $MO(r)$. The composite
represents an element of $\pi_N MO(r)$ whose stabilization lies in
$\pi_{N-r}MO=MO_d$. For a homotopy of sections without zeros on
$\partial K$, compactness of $[0,1]\times\partial K$ lets us use one
$\epsilon$ throughout. The resulting based maps are homotopic.

When $s$ is transverse, its vertical derivative identifies the normal
bundle $\nu$ of $Z(s)$ with $E|_{Z(s)}$. Take a closed tubular disk
neighborhood inside $\operatorname{int}K$. Identify the fibers of $E$
along each normal radius by parallel transport. In these coordinates the
sections $s(x,uv)/u$, for $0<u\le1$ and $v\in\nu_x$, extend at $u=0$
to $D_xs(v)$. They have no zeros on the boundary of the tube. Choose
$\epsilon$ small enough that the normalized sections send this boundary
to the sphere bundle throughout and that $s$ sends the complement of the
tube in $K$ to the basepoint after normalization and passage to the
quotient. Contracting the normal radii in the base identifies the
resulting Thom map with the tubular collapse classified by
$\nu\cong E|_{Z(s)}$. Thus it is the classical Pontryagin--Thom class of
$Z(s)$. For general $s$, the straight-line homotopy to a transverse
perturbation supported in $\operatorname{int}K$ fixes $s$ on $\partial K$.
The collapse therefore represents $[X]$ by
\Cref{thm:Spivak_Derived_Bordism_Comparison}.

\begin{exercise}
  Let $f\colon T^2\to\R$ and $c$ be as in
  \Cref{ex:Figure_Eight_Derived_Bordism}. Compute $H_0$ and $H_{-1}$ of the
  tangent complex of $Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f-c)$ at the saddle point and at the
  other points of the figure eight, and check that the Euler characteristic
  is the virtual dimension $1$ everywhere.
\end{exercise}

  Use \Cref{cor:Zero_Section_Self_Intersection_Euler_Class} to show that
  the derived self-intersection of the zero section of $TS^2$ is null-bordant,
  while that of $T\mathbb{RP}^2$ is derived cobordant to an odd number of
  points and hence is not null-bordant.
\end{exercise}
