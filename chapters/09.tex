\chapter{Stacks and local representability}
\label[chapter]{chap:Coherence_Descent_Local_Representability}

In this chapter, a moduli problem assigns to each derived manifold $T$
an anima of families over $T$. For example, a
family of zeros of $f\colon U\to\R^r$ parametrized by an ordinary
manifold $T$ is a map $u\colon T\to U$ such that $f\circ u=0$.
When $T$ is a derived manifold, we ask for $u$ together with a path from
$f\circ u$ to the zero map in $\Hom(T,\R^r)$. Precomposition along
$T'\to T$ pulls back both the map and the path.

This viewpoint defines a moduli problem before a geometric representing
object has been constructed. Descent expresses that families and their
isomorphisms can be glued. The main result of this chapter gives a way
to recognize when the resulting stack is a derived scheme: construct
an open cover by representable stacks.

This returns to the coherence problem posed in
\Cref{sec:Local_Zero_Loci_Not_Enough}. Starting with a globally defined
stack supplies the overlaps and their higher compatibilities; the work
is to construct open charts of that functor. We develop descent and
atlases with the vector bundle zero locus as a running example, then
formulate representability relative to a parameter stack. The distinction
between gluing families over a cover of a test object and gluing charts
of the moduli stack itself will be essential throughout. We end by
comparing smooth stacks with the stacks of this chapter, in preparation
for the analytic argument of
\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}.

The following three points are the main takeaways.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item A moduli functor is a stack if families and their identifications
 satisfy descent for open covers. Its values record families together with
 their identifications and all higher coherences.
 \item A stack is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme if and only
 if it admits an open atlas
 (\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}). The coherences needed to
 glue the charts are supplied by the stack itself.
 \item Representability relative to a parameter stack can be checked
 locally on that parameter stack
 (\Cref{cor:Local_Representability_Relative}).
\end{enumerate}
Some additional details on the coherent constructions used in this chapter
are given in \Cref{sec:Additional_Descent_Gluing}.

\section{The open topology and descent}

Descent concerns a cover of a test object: it reconstructs an
anima of global families from coherent local families. We specify the
test objects and covers first, then express this reconstruction as a limit.

We use derived manifolds as test objects and write $\mathrm{DMfd}$ for
their $\infty$-category. By the discussion following
\Cref{def:Derived_Cinfty_Scheme}, these are the affine derived
$\Cinfty$-schemes of finite presentation, that is, the spectra of
finitely presented animated $\Cinfty$-rings. An \emph{open embedding}
$T'\to T$ is the inclusion of an open subset of $|T|$ with the restricted
structure sheaf; algebraically it is described by smooth localization.
An open subobject of a derived manifold is again a derived manifold.
For a derived manifold $\Spec A$, such an open is of the form
$\Spec A[a^{-1}]$, since every open subset of $\R^n$ is the nonvanishing
locus of a smooth function. An \emph{open covering family}
$\{T_i\to T\}$ is a family of open embeddings whose images cover $|T|$.
Open embeddings and open covering families are stable under pullback.

\begin{convention}
\label[convention]{conv:Stacks_Chapter_Terminology}
In this chapter, a \emph{derived scheme} is a derived
$\Cinfty$-scheme locally of finite presentation in the sense of
\Cref{def:Derived_Cinfty_Scheme}; the affine derived schemes are exactly
the objects of $\mathrm{DMfd}$. A \emph{stack} is a stack on $\mathrm{DMfd}$ in the
sense of \Cref{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty} below. For a prestack
$\mathcal F$, an element of $\mathcal F(T)$ is called a \emph{family
over~$T$}.
\end{convention}

\begin{definition}[Prestack]
\label[definition]{def:Prestack_Derived_Cinfty}
A \emph{prestack} is a functor
$\mathcal F\colon\mathrm{DMfd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\An$.
\end{definition}

The anima $\mathcal F(T)$ contains the $T$-families, their isomorphisms,
and higher identifications between those isomorphisms.
Replacing it by its set of components loses automorphisms as well as
higher coherence. Pullback along a map of test objects is part
of the functor.

Fix an open covering family $\{T_i\to T\}_{i\in I}$. For each ordered
tuple $(i_0,\ldots,i_n)\in I^{n+1}$, write
\[
 T_{i_0\cdots i_n}=T_{i_0}\times_T\cdots\times_T T_{i_n}.
\]
This is the intersection of the indicated opens with the restricted
structure sheaf. For example, the two projections from $T_{ij}$ give
two ways to restrict a collection of local families to the double
overlaps. The augmented \v{C}ech nerve of the cover assembles its
intersections and their maps to $T$ into one diagram. Mapping this
diagram into $\mathcal F$ gives a cone over a cosimplicial anima,
indexed by the category $\Delta$ of nonempty finite ordinals and
order-preserving maps. Its degree-$n$ term is the product below.

\begin{definition}[Stack]
\label[definition]{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty}
A prestack $\mathcal F$ is a \emph{stack} if, for every open covering
family $\{T_i\to T\}_{i\in I}$, the restriction map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech}
 \mathcal F(T)\longrightarrow
 \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}
 \prod_{(i_0,\ldots,i_n)\in I^{n+1}}\mathcal F(T_{i_0\cdots i_n})
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism of animae.
\end{definition}

The arrow in \eqref{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech} sends a global
family to its restrictions to the intersections $T_{i_0\cdots i_n}$,
together with the canonical identifications between the various
restrictions to smaller intersections.
\Cref{sec:Additional_Cech_Details} constructs the cosimplicial diagram
and this map precisely, as the augmented \v{C}ech nerve of the cover.

For a set-valued functor this reduces to the usual sheaf condition:
local elements must agree on double overlaps.
For groupoid-valued functors one instead chooses isomorphisms on double
overlaps and imposes the cocycle condition on triple overlaps.
For general animae the cocycle condition itself is supplied by a homotopy,
followed by higher compatibility data. The limit in
\eqref{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech} packages all of it.

For example, suppose $x,y\in\mathcal F(T)$ and we want to identify
them. A path between their images in the limit consists of local
identifications $x|_{T_i}\simeq y|_{T_i}$ compatible on overlaps,
with all higher compatibilities. Descent says that the anima of such
data is the path anima from $x$ to $y$ in $\mathcal F(T)$.

The resulting $\infty$-category of stacks is denoted
$\mathrm{dSt}_{\Cinfty}^{\mathrm{fp}}$ here.
The superscript records our site of finitely presented test objects.
Steffens embeds this category fully faithfully into his category using
all affine derived smooth schemes, and calls its objects stacks locally
of finite presentation
\cite[Proposition~2.1.40 and Definition~2.1.41]
{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Our constructions take place in this subcategory.

\section{Represented stacks and the zero-locus example}

Representability asks whether all families arise as maps to a geometric
object. The universal property of a derived pullback answers this
question for the zero-locus functor and gives a model for the later
elliptic moduli problem.

For a derived scheme $X$, its functor
of points is
\[
 h_X(T)=\Hom(T,X).
\]
It satisfies descent because morphisms of locally ringed objects can
be glued over an open cover of their source.
More explicitly, the continuous maps on the underlying spaces glue
uniquely. For the resulting map $f\colon|T|\to|X|$, the maps
$f^{-1}\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_T$ glue as morphisms of sheaves,
including all homotopies between them; this is the mapping assertion of
\Cref{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves} below. The condition that
the maps on stalks are local can be checked on the same cover.
This proves descent for the entire mapping anima and hence the
subcanonical property of the open topology.
The functor $X\mapsto h_X$ is also fully faithful. For affine $X$
this is Yoneda. For general $X$, a transformation $h_X\to h_Y$
gives a map to $Y$ on each affine open of $X$ by Yoneda. Naturality
supplies their compatible restrictions to the intersections. Descent
for maps into $Y$ now identifies the anima of such transformations
with $\Hom(X,Y)$
\cite[Proposition~2.1.35(4)]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

\begin{definition}[Representability]
\label[definition]{def:Represented_Stack}
A stack $\mathcal F$ is \emph{represented} by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme
$X$ if there is an isomorphism $h_X\iso\mathcal F$ of stacks. In
particular, the resulting isomorphisms $\Hom(T,X)\simeq\mathcal F(T)$
are natural in the test object $T$.
\end{definition}

Full faithfulness gives uniqueness of the representing object among
derived schemes. For a derived scheme $X$ we
write $\mathcal F(X)=\Hom(h_X,\mathcal F)$, also when $X$ is
non-affine. One can compute this anima by descent on an affine open
cover of $X$. A representing isomorphism $h_X\iso\mathcal F$ is thus
itself a family over $X$. It is universal in the sense that its
pullback along $u\colon T\to X$ is precisely the family corresponding
to $u$ under $\Hom(T,X)\simeq\mathcal F(T)$.

Let $f\colon U\to\R^r$ be smooth. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Derived_Zero_Locus_Prestack}
 \mathcal Z_f(T)=\Hom(T,U)\times_{\Hom(T,\R^r)}\Hom(T,*),
\end{equation}
with pullback in animae.
A point is a map $u\colon T\to U$ together with a homotopy from $f\circ u$
to zero. Identifications in this pullback give the isomorphisms between
such data and their higher compatibilities. Limits of stacks are computed on each test object, so
$\mathcal Z_f$ is a stack.

By the universal property of a pullback,
\[
 \mathcal Z_f(T)\cong\Hom(T,U\times_{\R^r}^{\mathrm{der}}*).
\]
Thus it is represented by the derived zero locus computed in \Cref{chap:Derived_Zero_Loci_And_Tangent_Complexes}.
This is a simple instance of the representability question that will
later arise for differential equations.

For a section $s\colon U\to E$ of a vector bundle, the analogous stack is
the pullback
\[
 \mathcal Z_s=h_U\times_{h_E}h_U
\]
of $s$ and the zero section. It is represented by the derived zero locus
$U\times_E^{\mathrm{der}}U$. Over an open $U_i\subseteq U$ with a
trivialization $E|_{U_i}\cong U_i\times\R^r$, the section becomes a smooth
map $s_i\colon U_i\to\R^r$, and
$\mathcal Z_s\times_{h_U}h_{U_i}\simeq\mathcal Z_{s_i}$ has the Koszul
model of \Cref{chap:Derived_Zero_Loci_And_Tangent_Complexes}.
These local zero loci will form an open atlas of $\mathcal Z_s$.

We stress that these examples are representable for a formal reason.
Manifolds are test objects, and $\mathrm{DMfd}$ has finite limits, so
$\mathcal Z_f$ and $\mathcal Z_s$ are represented by derived manifolds
before any gluing takes place. We use them to watch the machinery of
atlases in a case where the answer is known. For the elliptic moduli
problems of \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models}, by contrast,
no global finite-dimensional presentation is available, and
representability must be established chart by chart.
\Cref{ex:Line_Two_Origins} gives a simple stack for which gluing is
genuinely needed.

\section{Open atlases and local representability}

We now turn from gluing families over a cover of a test object to presenting
the moduli stack itself by charts. Here a \v{C}ech diagram reconstructs
the stack as a colimit. Effectiveness expresses that the charts cover
the functor; openness makes it possible to glue their representing
objects into a derived scheme.
The proof uses two inputs. The local lifting criterion detects
effective epimorphisms on test objects. A gluing lemma constructs a
derived scheme from charts and open overlaps, once all their coherences
have been specified. The point of the proof is that a stack with an open
atlas specifies these coherences for us.

Let $p\colon\mathcal U\to\mathcal F$ be a morphism of stacks.
Applying the augmented \v{C}ech nerve construction of
\Cref{sec:Additional_Cech_Details} in the $\infty$-category of stacks
gives a simplicial stack over $\mathcal F$ with terms
\[
 \mathcal U_n=
 \underbrace{\mathcal U\times_{\mathcal F}\cdots
 \times_{\mathcal F}\mathcal U}_{n+1\text{ factors}}.
\]
The augmentation maps its geometric realization to $\mathcal F$.
Here geometric realization means a colimit in stacks, computed by
taking the presheaf colimit and then sheafifying.

\begin{definition}[Effective epimorphism]
\label[definition]{def:Effective_Epimorphism}
The map $p$ is an effective epimorphism if
$|\mathcal U_\bullet|\to\mathcal F$ is an isomorphism.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Local lifting criterion]
\label[lemma]{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}
A map $p\colon\mathcal U\to\mathcal F$ is an effective epimorphism
if and only if, for every derived manifold $T$ and every $x\in\mathcal F(T)$,
there is an open cover $\{T_a\to T\}$ on which $x$ lifts to
$\mathcal U$. A lift includes an identification of its image with
$x|_{T_a}$.
Effective epimorphisms are preserved by pullback.
\end{lemma}

This is a standard sheaf-theoretic criterion, whose proof we sketch in
\Cref{sec:Local_Lifts_And_Sheaf_Gluing}. For the reduction to sheaves on
underlying topological spaces, see
\citet[Lemma~2.1.38]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli};
the criterion is used explicitly in the proof of Corollary~2.1.49 there.

For a family $\{\mathcal U_i\to\mathcal F\}$, the criterion says
that each family in $\mathcal F(T)$ locally lifts to one of the
$\mathcal U_i$. A map into a coproduct of stacks locally lands in
one summand, since that coproduct is the sheafification of the
pointwise coproduct. This accounts for the possible refinement of
the open cover of the test object when applying the criterion to a family.

Starting with a globally defined stack supplies the overlap data.
If $U_i\to\mathcal F$ and $U_j\to\mathcal F$ are two charts, their
overlap is $U_i\times_{\mathcal F}U_j$. Its associativity and higher
compatibilities come from pullbacks in the ambient $\infty$-category.
The following condition makes these overlaps geometric open subsets.

\begin{definition}[Open atlas]
\label[definition]{def:Open_Atlas}
A morphism $\mathcal V\to\mathcal F$ is an open inclusion if, for every
derived manifold $T$ and map $h_T\to\mathcal F$, the pullback
$h_T\times_{\mathcal F}\mathcal V$ is represented by an open subobject of $T$.
An open atlas is a small family of open inclusions $h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$
from derived schemes such that
$\coprod_i h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$ is an effective epimorphism.
\end{definition}

Concretely, a family $x\colon h_T\to\mathcal F$ determines an open
subobject $T_i(x)\subseteq T$ by
\[
 h_{T_i(x)}\simeq h_T\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_i}.
\]
For any $R\to T$, a factorization through $T_i(x)$ amounts to a lift of
$x|_R$ to the chart, including the identification of its image with
$x|_R$. The local lifting criterion says exactly that these opens
cover $T$. This is how a cover of the moduli stack induces an ordinary
open cover of the underlying space of each test object.

The word \emph{open} is crucial for representing a stack by a scheme.
For example, a nontrivial finite group $G$ has a classifying stack $BG$.
The map $*\to BG$ is an effective epimorphism, expressing local
triviality of principal $G$-bundles. It is not an open inclusion:
its pullback along itself is $G\to*$.
The automorphisms of the trivial bundle also show that $BG$ cannot be
represented by a derived scheme, whose real points form a set.
More general notions of atlas lead to orbifolds and other geometric
stacks, but an arbitrary effective epimorphism is insufficient for
scheme representability.

To glue charts we use the following lemma. Recall that a simplicial
object $C_\bullet$ is a \emph{groupoid object} if its Segal maps
$C_n\to C_1\times_{C_0}\cdots\times_{C_0}C_1$ are isomorphisms and the
map $(d_2,d_1)\colon C_2\to C_1\times_{C_0}C_1$, which records two arrows
with a common source, is an isomorphism as well; the latter condition
expresses that arrows are invertible
\cite[Definition~2.18]{Hoyois_Cnossen_Lectures_Topos}. The \v{C}ech
nerve of a map is a groupoid object
\cite[Lemma~2.26]{Hoyois_Cnossen_Lectures_Topos}.

\begin{lemma}[Gluing derived schemes]
\label[lemma]{lem:Gluing_Derived_Schemes}
Let $I$ be a set, and let $C_\bullet$ be a groupoid object in derived
schemes with a map to the \v{C}ech nerve
$[n]\mapsto\coprod_{I^{n+1}}*$ of $\coprod_{i\in I}*\to*$. Write
$U_{i_0\cdots i_n}$ for the fibre of $C_n$ over
$(i_0,\ldots,i_n)\in I^{n+1}$, so that
$C_n=\coprod_{(i_0,\ldots,i_n)}U_{i_0\cdots i_n}$. Suppose that every
component $U_{i_0\cdots i_n}\to U_{j_0\cdots j_m}$ of a simplicial
structure map is an open embedding. Then there is a derived scheme $X$
with an open cover $\{U_i\to X\}_{i\in I}$ whose \v{C}ech nerve is
$C_\bullet$, and $h_X\simeq|h_{C_\bullet}|$ in stacks.
\end{lemma}

This is a special case of
\citet[Propositions~2.1.18(2), 2.1.31(5), and~2.1.35(2)]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
The injectivity hypothesis concerns the map of topological spaces
$|C_1|\to |C_0|\times |C_0|$. The two chart indices determine the
summand $|U_{ij}|$, and on this summand the first projection is injective.
Remark~2.1.19 there explains the special case relevant to us.
Concretely, $X$ is obtained by gluing the $U_i$ along the open subsets
$|U_{ij}|\subseteq|U_i|$, and the higher terms $C_2,C_3,\ldots$ supply
the cocycle condition and its higher coherences. We sketch the
construction in \Cref{sec:Local_Lifts_And_Sheaf_Gluing}.


\begin{theorem}[Open local representability]
\label[theorem]{thm:Open_Local_Representability}
A stack is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme
locally of finite presentation if and only if it has an open atlas
by affine derived schemes of finite presentation.
One may equivalently allow derived schemes locally of finite presentation
as the atlas objects.
\end{theorem}

This is \citet[Proposition~2.1.48]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\mathcal F=h_X$, and choose an affine open cover
$\{U_i\to X\}$. Each $h_{U_i}\to h_X$ is an open inclusion:
its pullback along $T\to X$ is the open subscheme $T\times_X U_i$.
These pullbacks cover $T$. Refining them by affine opens gives local
lifts of every $T\to X$ to the charts. By
\Cref{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}, the chart family is
an effective epimorphism and hence an open atlas.

Conversely, let $p_i\colon h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$, for $i\in I$, be an
affine open atlas, and write
$p\colon\mathcal U=\coprod_i h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$. Since $p$ is an
effective epimorphism, $\mathcal F\simeq|\check C(p)_\bullet|$. We show
that the \v{C}ech nerve $\check C(p)_\bullet$ is the functor of points
of a simplicial derived scheme to which
\Cref{lem:Gluing_Derived_Schemes} applies.

We first compute its terms. Pullbacks of stacks distribute over
coproducts, since locally on a test object a map into a coproduct
chooses a summand. Hence
\[
 \check C(p)_n\simeq\coprod_{(i_0,\ldots,i_n)\in I^{n+1}}
 h_{U_{i_0}}\times_{\mathcal F}\cdots\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_{i_n}}.
\]
This decomposition is compatible with the simplicial structure: it is
the decomposition into fibres of the map of \v{C}ech nerves induced by
the map from $p$ to $\coprod_{i\in I}*\to*$. For $n=1$, the summand is
represented by an open subscheme $U_{i_0i_1}\subseteq U_{i_0}$, by the
definition of an open inclusion applied to $p_{i_1}$ and the family
$p_{i_0}$. In general, the summand is the iterated pullback of the
$h_{U_{i_0i_k}}$ over $h_{U_{i_0}}$ for $k=1,\ldots,n$. Since functors
of points preserve pullbacks, it is represented by the intersection
$U_{i_0\cdots i_n}=U_{i_0i_1}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_0i_n}$. Functors of points
also preserve coproducts
\cite[Proposition~2.1.35(3)]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli},
so $\check C(p)_n\simeq h_{C_n}$ for
$C_n=\coprod_{(i_0,\ldots,i_n)}U_{i_0\cdots i_n}$.

Next we consider the structure maps. An open inclusion is a
monomorphism: on each test object its fibres are either empty or
contractible, since a factorization through an open subscheme is unique
when it exists. Consequently, a component of a degeneracy map, which
repeats an index, is an isomorphism. For example, the diagonal
$h_{U_i}\to h_{U_i}\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_i}$ is an isomorphism.
A component of a face map, which omits an index, is a pullback of one
of the $p_i$, and hence an open inclusion. Every component of a
structure map is therefore an open inclusion between representable
stacks. Such a map $h_V\to h_W$ comes from an open embedding: its
pullback along the identity of $h_W$ is $h_{W'}$ for an open subscheme
$W'\subseteq W$, and full faithfulness identifies $V$ with $W'$ over~$W$.

Now we lift. The functor of points is fully faithful on derived schemes,
so it is an equivalence onto its essential image. Every term of
$\check C(p)_\bullet$ lies in this image. Hence the entire simplicial
stack, with all its coherences, lifts uniquely to a simplicial derived
scheme $C_\bullet$ with $h_{C_\bullet}\simeq\check C(p)_\bullet$, and
the same holds for its map to the \v{C}ech nerve of $\coprod_i*\to*$.
The pullback squares expressing that $\check C(p)_\bullet$ is a groupoid
object have all their vertices in the essential image, and a fully
faithful functor reflects limits. Thus $C_\bullet$ is a groupoid object
in derived schemes, and by the previous paragraph it satisfies the
hypotheses of \Cref{lem:Gluing_Derived_Schemes}.

This step solves the coherence problem of
\Cref{sec:Local_Zero_Loci_Not_Enough}. The cocycle condition on triple
overlaps and all higher compatibilities between the charts are part of
the simplicial structure of $\check C(p)_\bullet$. The map from the
charts to $\mathcal F$ supplies their \v{C}ech nerve. The atlas condition
identifies its realization with $\mathcal F$.

\Cref{lem:Gluing_Derived_Schemes} now gives a derived scheme $X$ with an
open cover by the $U_i$, and
\[
 h_X\simeq|h_{C_\bullet}|\simeq|\check C(p)_\bullet|\simeq\mathcal F.
\]
This is an isomorphism of stacks, and thus identifies the animae of
families on every test object.

Finally, suppose the atlas objects are only locally of finite
presentation. Choose an affine open cover of each $U_i$.
The composites of these open inclusions with $p_i$ are open.
A family in $\mathcal F(T)$ first locally lifts to some $U_i$,
and then, after pulling back its affine cover, locally lifts to
one of these affine charts. The local lifting criterion shows that
the refined family is again an effective epimorphism. The affine
case just proved applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The line with two origins]
\label[example]{ex:Line_Two_Origins}
Let $V=\R\setminus\{0\}$, and let $\mathcal F=h_\R\sqcup_{h_V}h_\R$ be
the pushout of stacks along two copies of the inclusion $V\subseteq\R$.
The two maps $h_\R\to\mathcal F$ form an open atlas, with overlap
$h_\R\times_{\mathcal F}h_\R\simeq h_V$; this is verified in the
exercises at the end of the chapter.
\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability} represents $\mathcal F$ by the
derived scheme obtained by gluing two copies of $\R$ along $V$: the line
with two origins. Its underlying space is not Hausdorff. Hence it is not
affine, since the underlying space of a test object is a closed subset of
some $\R^n$, and it is not a derived manifold. This is why no global
Hausdorff or second-countability condition is imposed on a derived
scheme: imposing either would require an additional hypothesis on the
atlas.
\end{example}

In the bundle-section example, the charts are the zero loci over a
trivializing cover of $U$. Their overlaps are the zero loci over the
corresponding intersections. The transition maps of the bundle identify
the Koszul models, and the theorem recovers the global derived zero locus.

For instance, given a family of zeros over $T$ with underlying map
$u\colon T\to U$, the part of $T$ on which it belongs to the chart
over $U_i$ is $T\times_U U_i$. These opens cover $T$, independently
of the chosen nullhomotopy of the section. Quasi-smoothness can also
be read off from the charts, since the cotangent complex restricts
to the cotangent complex of each open subscheme.

The local representability criterion does not make the construction of
a chart automatic.
One must identify the entire derived functor on an open neighbourhood,
including its values on derived test objects. An identification of real
solution sets, or even of their tangent complexes, proves less.
The later Kuranishi argument is designed to establish precisely
this functorial identification.

This is how the theorem is used in
\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models}. For each real solution
$t$ of an elliptic equation, one constructs an open substack
$\Sol(\mathcal E)_t$ of the solution stack and checks that these
substacks jointly form an effective epimorphism. The theorem then reduces
representability of the solution stack to representability of each
$\Sol(\mathcal E)_t$, which occupies the following two chapters.

\section{Relative representability and base change}

Moduli problems often have a parameter stack $S$.
For example, the metric, the domain manifold, or a coefficient in an
equation may vary. The correct representability statement is then
relative to $S$.

\begin{definition}
\label[definition]{def:Relative_Representability}
A map $\mathcal F\to S$ is representable by derived $\Cinfty$-schemes
locally of finite presentation if, for each derived manifold $T$ and each
map $h_T\to S$, the pullback $\mathcal F\times_S h_T$ is represented by such
a scheme. It is relatively quasi-smooth if these representing
morphisms to $T$ are quasi-smooth.
\end{definition}

For example, let $S$ be a manifold and let
$f\colon U\times S\to\R^r$ be a smooth family of equations on $U$.
Projection makes the stack $\mathcal Z_f$ of
\eqref{eq:Derived_Zero_Locus_Prestack} into a stack over $h_S$. For a
derived manifold $T$ with a map $\sigma\colon T\to S$, the pullback
$\mathcal Z_f\times_{h_S}h_T$ is the zero-locus stack of the
$T$-parametrized family
$f\circ(\mathrm{id}_U\times\sigma)\colon U\times T\to\R^r$. It is
therefore represented by a derived manifold over $T$, which is
quasi-smooth over $T$. Over a real point $s\in S$ it is the derived zero
locus of the single equation $f(-,s)$. Here the total stack
$\mathcal Z_f$ is itself representable. The relative notion becomes
essential when the base is a genuine stack, as in
\Cref{ex:Finite_Quotient_Relative} below.

\begin{corollary}[Locality on the base]
\label[corollary]{cor:Local_Representability_Relative}
Representability by derived schemes locally of finite presentation
can be checked after an effective epimorphic covering family of the base.
The same holds with the additional requirement of relative
quasi-smoothness.
\end{corollary}

The maps $S_i\to S$ in this criterion can have many lifts of the same
parameter. What we need in order to glue schemes is an open cover
of the derived manifold $T$. Effectiveness produces just such a cover on which
we can choose lifts.

\begin{proof}
Let $\{S_i\to S\}$ be a family whose coproduct is an effective
epimorphism, and suppose each
$\mathcal F_i=\mathcal F\times_S S_i\to S_i$ is representable.
Fix a derived manifold $T$ with a map $h_T\to S$. By
\Cref{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}, there is an open
cover $\{T_a\to T\}$ and, for each $a$, a lift
$T_a\to S_{i(a)}$ with a specified identification over $S$.
Each $T_a$ is again a derived manifold.
The pullback identity
\[
 \mathcal F\times_S T_a
 \cong\mathcal F_{i(a)}\times_{S_{i(a)}}T_a
\]
and the assumed relative representability give a representing
scheme $X_a$ locally of finite presentation.

Put $\mathcal G=\mathcal F\times_S T$.
Each $h_{X_a}\to\mathcal G$ is the pullback of the open inclusion
$T_a\to T$, so is open. Their coproduct is an effective epimorphism:
for any test map $R\to\mathcal G$, the opens $R\times_T T_a$
cover $R$ and give local lifts to the $h_{X_a}$.
\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}, including its non-affine
atlas formulation, therefore represents $\mathcal G$ by a scheme
$X$ locally of finite presentation. This works for every $T\to S$
and proves the required relative representability.

If every $\mathcal F_i\to S_i$ is relatively quasi-smooth, then
$X_a\to T_a$ is quasi-smooth by relative quasi-smoothness applied to
the map $T_a\to S_{i(a)}$.
Moreover $X_a=X\times_T T_a$. Base change for relative cotangent
complexes identifies $\mathbb L_{X/T}|_{X_a}$ with
$\mathbb L_{X_a/T_a}$. Perfection and Tor-amplitude in $[0,1]$
can be checked on this open cover of $X$, so $X\to T$ is
quasi-smooth. Conversely, representability and relative
quasi-smoothness are preserved by base change, so the asserted
conditions are necessary as well.
\end{proof}

The source formulation is
\cite[Definition~2.1.42 and Corollary~2.1.49]
{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
The addendum on relative quasi-smoothness is not part of Corollary~2.1.49
there; it follows from the base change argument in the last paragraph of
the proof. The corollary permits a proof over a smooth stack to be
carried out on manifold charts of the base, provided the moduli
construction is compatible with pullback.

\begin{example}[Quotients by finite groups]
\label[example]{ex:Finite_Quotient_Relative}
Let a finite group $G$ act on $U$ such that $f\colon U\to\R^r$ is
invariant, $f(gu)=f(u)$. Then $G$ acts on $\mathcal Z_f$, and we can
form the quotient stack $[\mathcal Z_f/G]$: the geometric realization in
stacks of the action groupoid with terms $G^n\times\mathcal Z_f$. It
maps to $BG=[*/G]$. Since groupoid objects in stacks are effective, the
pullback of $[\mathcal Z_f/G]\to BG$ along $*\to BG$ is $\mathcal Z_f$,
which is represented by a quasi-smooth derived manifold. As $*\to BG$ is
an effective epimorphism, \Cref{cor:Local_Representability_Relative}
shows that $[\mathcal Z_f/G]\to BG$ is representable and relatively
quasi-smooth. The total stack is not representable in general. For the
trivial action, $[\mathcal Z_f/G]\simeq\mathcal Z_f\times BG$, and every
real point has automorphism group $G$, whereas the real points of a
derived scheme form a set. Representability is a property of the map:
it holds once the parameter in $BG$, including its identification with
a chosen torsor, is fixed.
\end{example}

\section{Smooth stacks as derived stacks}
\label[section]{sec:Smooth_Stacks_As_Derived_Stacks}

The analytic part of the representability proof regards Fr\'echet
spaces of sections as smooth stacks, which are only defined on ordinary
manifolds (\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}).
We therefore compare smooth stacks with the stacks of this chapter.

A smooth stack is a sheaf of animae on ordinary manifolds with their
open-cover topology. Write $j\colon\mathrm{Mfd}\hookrightarrow
\mathrm{DMfd}$ for the inclusion. Restriction along $j$ takes a derived
stack $\mathcal G$ to the smooth stack $M\mapsto\mathcal G(jM)$,
since an open cover of $M$ gives an open cover of $jM$ with the same
intersections. Its left adjoint
\[
 j_!\colon\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{Mfd})
 \longrightarrow\mathrm{dSt}_{\Cinfty}^{\mathrm{fp}}
\]
extends the families of a smooth stack $\mathcal F$ to all test objects.
To see how, fix a derived manifold $T$. A map $u\colon T\to jM$ and a
family $x\in\mathcal F(M)$ should give a family over $T$ by pullback.
The left Kan extension along $j^{\mathrm{op}}$ assembles these families
into the anima
\[
 (\operatorname{Lan}_{j^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathcal F)(T)
 \simeq \operatorname*{colim}_{(T\to jM)\in(T\downarrow j)^{\mathrm{op}}}
 \mathcal F(M).
\]
Here $(T\downarrow j)$ is the $\infty$-category of maps from $T$ to
ordinary manifolds. For a smooth map $g\colon M\to N$, a triangle
identifying $v\colon T\to jN$ with $j(g)\circ u$ gives the transition
map $g^*\colon\mathcal F(N)\to\mathcal F(M)$. Thus pulling a family
back along $v$ agrees coherently with first pulling it back along $g$
and then along $u$. Sheafifying this presheaf for open covers of
test objects gives $j_!\mathcal F$.

The functor $j_!$ is fully faithful
\cite[Proposition~2.1.40]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
We sketch the argument. First, $j_!h_M\simeq h_{jM}$, since by
adjunction and Yoneda maps from either stack to a stack $\mathcal G$
form the anima $\mathcal G(jM)$. As $j$ is fully faithful,
$j^*h_{jM}\simeq h_M$, so the unit $\mathcal F\to j^*j_!\mathcal F$ is
an isomorphism for representable $\mathcal F$. Second, $j^*$ preserves
colimits of stacks, not merely limits. This is the nontrivial input.
It holds because colimits of stacks can be detected after restriction
to the open subsets of each test object, and the open subsets of an
ordinary manifold are again ordinary manifolds
\cite[Lemma~2.1.38 and Proposition~2.1.40(2)]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Since $j_!$ preserves colimits as a left adjoint, the smooth stacks on
which the unit is an isomorphism are closed under colimits. Every smooth
stack is a colimit of representables, so the unit is always an
isomorphism. This is the full faithfulness of $j_!$.
In particular, $(j_!h_M)(T)\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{DMfd}}(T,jM)$.

Restriction to ordinary manifolds forgets information, even for derived
schemes with a discrete ring of functions. Let
$D=\Spec\R[x]/(x^2)$ be the double point. A map $jM\to D$ from an
ordinary manifold is a smooth function $g$ on $M$ with $g^2=0$, hence
$g=0$. Thus the inclusion $*\to D$ of the reduced point becomes an
isomorphism $j^*h_*\iso j^*h_D$, although it is not an isomorphism:
the identity of $D$ is a family over $D$ that does not factor through
the point. In particular, $j^*$ is not conservative, and a stack is not
determined by its values on ordinary manifolds.

There is a related issue with pullbacks. In smooth stacks,
\[
 *\times_\R *=*
\]
for the two origins. After passing to derived stacks, the pullback
is the derived self-intersection, with algebra $\R[\varepsilon]$,
where $|\varepsilon|=1$ and $d\varepsilon=0$.
Thus $j_!$ does not preserve arbitrary pullbacks.
Both the derived self-intersection and the ordinary point restrict to
the point stack on ordinary manifolds, since restriction preserves limits.
The pullbacks needed in the analytic argument will be compared using
submersiveness in \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}.

\section{Additional details on descent and gluing}
\label[section]{sec:Additional_Descent_Gluing}

We give the construction behind \Cref{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty}, then
sketch the proofs of the two inputs to
\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}: the local lifting criterion
and the gluing lemma for derived schemes.

\subsection{The \v{C}ech nerve of an open cover}
\label[subsection]{sec:Additional_Cech_Details}

We first construct the augmented nerve of a single arrow
$p\colon U\to X$ in an $\infty$-category $C$ with pullbacks, following
\cite[Construction~2.24]{Hoyois_Cnossen_Lectures_Topos}.
Write $\Delta_+$ for the category obtained from $\Delta$ by adjoining
the empty ordinal $[-1]$ as an initial object. In $\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}}$
the full subcategory $K$ on $[0]$ and $[-1]$ consists of a single
nonidentity arrow $[0]\to[-1]$. Thus $p$ itself is a functor $K\to C$.
For the inclusion $j\colon K\hookrightarrow\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}}$,
define the \emph{augmented \v{C}ech nerve} by
\[
 U^+_\bullet=\operatorname{Ran}_j(p)
 \quad\in\Fun(\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}},C).
\]
Here $\operatorname{Ran}_j$ is right adjoint to restriction along $j$.
In particular, for every augmented simplicial object $A$ there is a
natural isomorphism of animae
\[
 \Hom_{\Fun(\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}},C)}(A,U^+_\bullet)
 \simeq \Hom_{\Fun(K,C)}(j^*A,p).
\]
Since $j$ is fully faithful, the counit identifies $j^*U^+_\bullet$
with $p$. This universal property determines the entire functor,
including its augmentation, uniquely with that identification.

To see that the extension exists and compute its terms, fix $n\geq0$.
The pointwise formula takes the limit over the comma category
$([n]\downarrow j)$. Its objects are the maps $[0]\to[n]$ and
$[-1]\to[n]$ in $\Delta_+$. The first are the vertices $0,\ldots,n$,
and the second is the unique empty map. There is one nonidentity
arrow from each vertex to the empty map in the comma category.
Thus the diagram whose limit we take consists of $n+1$ copies of
$p\colon U\to X$ with common target. Pullbacks in $C$ give its limit
\[
 U_n^+\simeq\underbrace{U\times_X\cdots\times_X U}_{n+1\text{ factors}}.
\]
In degree $-1$ the comma category has only the empty map and the
limit is $X$. For $\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]$ in $\Delta$, composition
with $\alpha$ sends the vertex $r$ of $[m]$ to the vertex $\alpha(r)$
of $[n]$ and the empty map to the empty map. Restriction of limit
cones along this functor $([m]\downarrow j)\to([n]\downarrow j)$
gives $U_n^+\to U_m^+$. This computes the action of the right Kan
extension on $\alpha$. In particular, omitting an index gives a
projection and repeating an index gives a diagonal. For instance,
in $\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}}$ the unique arrow $[1]\to[-1]$ factors through
each of the two arrows $[1]\to[0]$. Applying $U^+_\bullet$ to these
two triangles identifies $p\circ\mathrm{pr}_0$ and
$p\circ\mathrm{pr}_1$ with the same augmentation $U_1^+\to X$.
Applying the same functor to longer strings of arrows gives the
higher compatibilities for these triangles.

Now take $C=\Fun(\mathrm{DMfd}^{\mathrm{op}},\An)$. For the cover
$\{T_i\to T\}_{i\in I}$ of \Cref{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty}, use the arrow
\[
 p\colon U=\coprod_{i\in I}h_{T_i}\longrightarrow h_T .
\]
This coproduct exists in presheaves for every index set $I$.
Write $i\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}}$
for the inclusion and $U_\bullet=i^*U^+_\bullet$ for the underlying
simplicial presheaf. Pullbacks distribute over coproducts in animae,
presheaf limits and colimits are computed pointwise, and Yoneda
preserves pullbacks. The pointwise formula above therefore gives
\[
 U_n\simeq \coprod_{(i_0,\ldots,i_n)\in I^{n+1}}h_{T_{i_0\cdots i_n}}.
\]

For any prestack $\mathcal F$, the composite
\[
 \Delta_+\xrightarrow{(U^+_\bullet)^{\mathrm{op}}}C^{\mathrm{op}}
 \xrightarrow{\Hom_C(-,\mathcal F)}\An
\]
is an augmented cosimplicial anima. Its value at $[-1]$ is
$\Hom_C(h_T,\mathcal F)\simeq\mathcal F(T)$. In degree $n\geq0$,
mapping out of the coproduct and applying Yoneda gives precisely
the product $C^n$ in \eqref{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech}.
For $\alpha\colon[m]\to[n]$, the $(i_0,\ldots,i_n)$-component of
$C^m\to C^n$ takes the $(i_{\alpha(0)},\ldots,i_{\alpha(m)})$-component
and restricts it along
\[
 T_{i_0\cdots i_n}\longrightarrow T_{i_{\alpha(0)}\cdots i_{\alpha(m)}}.
\]
In particular, the two maps $C^0\rightrightarrows C^1$ send a family
$(x_i)$ to $(x_i|_{T_{ij}})_{i,j}$ and $(x_j|_{T_{ij}})_{i,j}$.
A point of the limit supplies paths between these restrictions. On
$T_{ijk}$ it supplies a homotopy from the composite of the paths for
$(i,j)$ and $(j,k)$ to the path for $(i,k)$, all restricted to that
triple overlap. The higher terms supply the compatibilities among
these homotopies.

Since $[-1]$ is initial in $\Delta_+$, the augmented cosimplicial
anima is a cone on $C^\bullet$ with vertex $\mathcal F(T)$. We can
identify its map to the limit explicitly. For the adjunction
$\operatorname{Lan}_i\dashv i^*$ in presheaf-valued diagrams, evaluate
the counit $\operatorname{Lan}_i i^*U^+_\bullet\to U^+_\bullet$ at
$[-1]$. Every $[n]$ admits a unique map to $[-1]$ in
$\Delta_+^{\mathrm{op}}$, so $(i\downarrow[-1])\cong\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$.
The pointwise left Kan extension at $[-1]$ is consequently the
realization $|U_\bullet|$, and the counit there is a map
$|U_\bullet|\to h_T$. Precomposing with this component gives
\[
 \mathcal F(T)\simeq\Hom(h_T,\mathcal F)
 \longrightarrow\Hom(|U_\bullet|,\mathcal F)
 \simeq\lim_{[n]\in\Delta}\Hom(U_n,\mathcal F).
\]
The last isomorphism takes a map out of the realization to its cocone
of restrictions. Applied to a global family $x\colon h_T\to\mathcal F$,
it gives the augmentation cocone followed by $x$. This identifies
the displayed arrow with the restriction map of
\Cref{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty}. Given a coherent local family, the fibre of this
map over it is the anima of global families equipped with an
identification with the prescribed descent datum. The stack condition
says that each such fibre is contractible.

\subsection{Local lifts and gluing}
\label[subsection]{sec:Local_Lifts_And_Sheaf_Gluing}

Two uses of the \v{C}ech nerve enter the atlas argument. Realizing
the nerve of a map detects the families in its image after passage
to an open cover. Taking a limit over the intersections of the
charts glues their structure sheaves, and with it the charts
themselves.

\begin{proof}[Explanation of \Cref{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}]
For a map of animae $A\to B$, the realization of its \v{C}ech nerve
is the union of the components of $B$ met by $A$. Indeed, its fibre
over $b\in B$ is the realization of the \v{C}ech nerve of
$A_b\to *$. This is empty if $A_b$ is empty. Otherwise, choosing a
point of $A_b$ contracts the augmented nerve by inserting that point,
so its realization is a point.

Apply this observation on each test object. The presheaf realization
of $\mathcal U_\bullet$ is the subpresheaf $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal F$
whose value on $T$ consists of the components containing an image
of $\mathcal U(T)$. Its sheafification is the substack of families
which locally belong to $\mathcal I$: this is a local condition,
and all identifications between such families are inherited from
$\mathcal F$. A map from $\mathcal I$ to any stack extends uniquely
to this substack by descent on the covers witnessing local membership,
which verifies the universal property of sheafification.
This substack is all of $\mathcal F$ precisely when
the stated local lifts exist. Since realization in stacks is
presheaf realization followed by sheafification, this proves the criterion.

Suppose $p$ is effective and let $\mathcal G\to\mathcal F$ be any map.
For $y\in\mathcal G(T)$, choose local lifts of its image in
$\mathcal F(T)$ to $\mathcal U$. Each lift, together with the given
identification over the open on which it is defined, pairs with the
restriction of $y$ to give a lift to $\mathcal U\times_{\mathcal F}\mathcal G$. Thus the criterion also
proves stability under pullback.
\end{proof}

To glue derived schemes, we must glue both their topology and their
animated structure sheaves. For the sheaves we use the following
descent statement. In it, coherent identifications mean
isomorphisms on double intersections, homotopies between their
composites on triple intersections, and all higher compatibilities.

\begin{lemma}[Gluing structure sheaves]
\label[lemma]{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves}
Let $Y$ be a topological space with a small open cover $\{V_i\}$.
Suppose that each $V_i$ carries a sheaf $\mathcal A_i$ of animated
$\Cinfty$-rings, with coherent identifications of their restrictions
to the intersections. There is a sheaf $\mathcal A$ on $Y$ whose
restrictions, with their identifications, recover this datum.
It is unique, and maps between such sheaves are likewise determined
by their coherent restrictions to the cover.
\end{lemma}

It is the sheaf gluing mechanism in the proof of
\citet[Proposition~2.1.18]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli};
Remark~2.1.19(c) there describes the structure sheaf by the limit
formula in \eqref{eq:Glued_Animated_Structure_Sheaf}.

More precisely, write $\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{rings}}(V)$ for the
$\infty$-category of sheaves of animated $\Cinfty$-rings on $V$.
The lemma says that restriction induces an equivalence of
$\infty$-categories
\[
 \mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{rings}}(Y)\longrightarrow
 \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}\prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
 \mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{rings}}(V_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i_n}).
\]
An object of this limit is what we mean by a coherent gluing datum.
The assertion about maps is the full faithfulness of restriction.

\begin{proof}[Explanation of \Cref{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves}]
Write $V_{i_0\cdots i_n}=V_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i_n}$.
Let $W\subseteq Y$ be open. Restrict the given gluing datum to $W$.
By the definition of a coherent gluing datum, this gives an object of the
limit of the cosimplicial $\infty$-category
\[
 D_W^n=\prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
 \mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{rings}}(W\cap V_{i_0\cdots i_n}).
\]
Write $\mathcal A_W^n$ for its degree-$n$ component. We explain how
to take sections of this datum coherently. For any animated smooth
ring $B$, the constant sheaf with value $B$ on each intersection
defines another object of the same limit, with components
$\underline B_W^n$. Indeed, these sheaves are pulled back from the
constant sheaf on $Y$. Taking the Hom anima between the two objects
degreewise gives a functor
\[
 [n]\longmapsto\Hom_{D_W^n}(\underline B_W^n,\mathcal A_W^n).
\]
Here we use functoriality of the Hom anima in a category with a
specified pair of objects. The two objects of the limit supply such
a pair over the whole diagram $\Delta\to\Cat$. On the
$(i_0,\ldots,i_n)$-factor, the inclusion of the first vertex
$[0]\to[n]$ identifies $\mathcal A_W^n$ with the restriction of
$\mathcal A_{i_0}$. By the adjunction between constant sheaves and
global sections, the displayed functor is therefore represented in
the variable $B$, in degree $n$, by the product of rings below.
Since Yoneda is fully faithful, the diagram of these representable
functors determines a cosimplicial animated smooth ring. Its limit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Glued_Animated_Structure_Sheaf}
 \mathcal A(W)=
 \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}
 \prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
 \mathcal A_{i_0}(W\cap V_{i_0\cdots i_n}).
\end{equation}
For example, the two maps to the $(i,j)$-factor must both take values
in $\mathcal A_i(W\cap V_i\cap V_j)$. A section of $\mathcal A_i$
can be restricted directly. For a section of $\mathcal A_j$ we first
restrict to the same open and then use the given identification
with $\mathcal A_i$ there. On triple overlaps the homotopies in the
gluing datum compare the two ways of changing the first index.
Each functor of $W$ occurring on the right is a sheaf on $Y$:
an open cover of $W$ restricts to an open cover of its intersection
with $V_{i_0\cdots i_n}$. Limits of sheaves are computed on opens,
because limits commute with the limits expressing descent.
Thus the formula defines a sheaf.

If $W\subseteq V_k$, the transition identifications identify the
diagram with the descent diagram of $\mathcal A_k$ for the cover
$\{W\cap V_i\}$ of $W$. Hence the sheaf condition gives
$\mathcal A(W)\cong\mathcal A_k(W)$. These isomorphisms recover
the specified transition data, including its higher coherences.

Conversely, a sheaf on $Y$ is recovered from its restrictions by
the same formula, using descent for $\{W\cap V_i\}$. The construction
is functorial in the coherent restriction data, so gluing and
restriction are inverse functors. This proves both uniqueness and
the assertion about maps, including homotopies between them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of \Cref{lem:Gluing_Derived_Schemes}]
For $i,j\in I$, the components $U_{ij}\to U_i$ and $U_{ij}\to U_j$ of
the two face maps are open embeddings. Write $W_{ij}\subseteq|U_i|$ for
the image of the first and $\varphi_{ij}\colon W_{ij}\to W_{ji}$ for the
resulting homeomorphism. The degeneracy $U_i\to U_{ii}$ is an open
embedding whose composite with either face map is the identity, so
$U_{ii}\to U_i$ is an isomorphism. The degeneracies, the inverses, and
the composition $U_{ij}\times_{U_j}U_{jk}\simeq U_{ijk}\to U_{ik}$ of the
groupoid give $\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$,
$\varphi_{ji}=\varphi_{ij}^{-1}$, and
$\varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{ik}$ wherever the composite is
defined. Passing to underlying topological spaces turns the specified
homotopies into equalities.

Define a topological space
\[
 |X|=\left(\coprod_i|U_i|\right)\big/\sim,
 \qquad
 x\sim\varphi_{ij}(x)\quad(x\in W_{ij}),
\]
with the quotient topology. The preceding identities show that the
displayed relation is already an equivalence relation. It identifies
no two distinct points of a single $|U_i|$, because $W_{ii}=|U_i|$
and $\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$. Each induced map
$q_i\colon|U_i|\to|X|$ is therefore injective.
It is also open: for $O\subseteq|U_i|$ open, the inverse image
of $q_i(O)$ in $|U_j|$ is
$\varphi_{ij}(O\cap W_{ij})$, which is open. The definition of
the quotient topology then makes $q_i(O)$ open in $|X|$.
Thus $q_i$ is a homeomorphism onto an open subset $V_i$, the
$V_i$ cover $|X|$, and $q_i^{-1}(V_i\cap V_j)=W_{ij}$.

Transport $\mathcal O_{U_i}$ to $V_i$ along $q_i$.
The two open embeddings from $U_{ij}$ identify the restrictions
of $\mathcal O_{U_i}$ and $\mathcal O_{U_j}$ on $V_i\cap V_j$.
To see the compatibility on a triple intersection, pull the three
chart sheaves back to $U_{ijk}$. Each pullback is the structure sheaf of
$U_{ijk}$, and the identifications from the double overlaps pull back
along the face maps to comparisons through this common sheaf. In every
degree, restriction to the smaller open is functorial in the simplicial
structure maps. This gives the coherent gluing datum to which we apply
\Cref{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves} to obtain $\mathcal O_X$.
We emphasize that this datum is obtained by applying the structure
sheaf to the entire simplicial diagram $C_\bullet$ at once. Making this
precise, by passing to the $\infty$-topoi of sheaves on the
$|U_{i_0\cdots i_n}|$, is the content of the proof of
\citet[Proposition~2.1.18]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
At a point in $V_i$, the stalk of $\mathcal O_X$ is the corresponding
stalk of $\mathcal O_{U_i}$, so its degree-zero ring is local with the
same residue map to $\R$. Consequently
$X=(|X|,\mathcal O_X)$ is a locally animated smooth-ringed space with
$(V_i,\mathcal O_X|_{V_i})\cong U_i$. Since the $U_i$ are derived schemes,
so is $X$.

It remains to identify $C_\bullet$ with the \v{C}ech nerve of the chart
map $q\colon C_0=\coprod_i U_i\to X$. Here we use that the gluing
construction above exhibits $X$ as the colimit of $C_\bullet$ in
derived locally $\Cinfty$-ringed spaces
\cite[Proposition~2.1.18(2) and Remark~2.1.19]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Its colimit cocone gives an augmented simplicial diagram $C^+_\bullet$
with degree-$(-1)$ term $X$ and augmentation $q$ in degree zero.
Let $D^+_\bullet$ be the augmented \v{C}ech nerve of $q$. As in
\Cref{sec:Additional_Cech_Details}, restriction $j^*$ to degrees
$0,-1$ has right adjoint $\operatorname{Ran}_j$. The unit gives a map
of augmented diagrams
\[
 C^+_\bullet\longrightarrow \operatorname{Ran}_j(j^*C^+_\bullet)
 \simeq D^+_\bullet
\]
which is the identity on $q$ under the displayed identification.

In degree one this map restricts on the $(i,j)$-summand to
$U_{ij}\to U_i\times_X U_j$. It lies over $U_i$, where both source
and target are the open subobject with underlying open $W_{ij}$:
for the target this follows by pulling back $U_j\to X$ along
$U_i\to X$. Thus this component is an isomorphism, and so is
$C_1\to D_1$. For $n\geq 2$, naturality of the Segal maps identifies
$C_n\to D_n$ with the induced map between the $n$-fold pullbacks
of $C_1$ over $C_0$ and of $D_1$ over $D_0$. It is an isomorphism
since the maps in degrees zero and one are isomorphisms. This proves
$C_\bullet\simeq D_\bullet$.

Finally, functors of points preserve pullbacks, so $h_{D_\bullet}$ is
the \v{C}ech nerve of $h_q\colon h_{C_0}\to h_X$. For every test map
$T\to X$ the open subschemes $T\times_XU_i$ cover $T$, so $h_q$ is an
effective epimorphism by \Cref{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}.
Therefore $h_X\simeq|h_{D_\bullet}|\simeq|h_{C_\bullet}|$.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal F=h_\R\sqcup_{h_V}h_\R$ be as in \Cref{ex:Line_Two_Origins}.
Using that pushouts along monomorphisms of stacks are also pullbacks,
show that $h_\R\times_{\mathcal F}h_\R\simeq h_V$ for the two different
maps, and that each map $h_\R\to\mathcal F$ is a monomorphism. Deduce
with the local lifting criterion that the two maps form an open atlas.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
For a nontrivial finite group $G$, calculate $*\times_{BG}*$ and
explain both the effectiveness of $*\to BG$ and its failure to be
an open inclusion.
\end{exercise}
