\chapter{Stacks and local representability}
\label[chapter]{chap:Coherence_Descent_Local_Representability}

Talk 8 ended with local Kuranishi presentations of quasi-smooth derived
objects. It also exposed their central limitation. A presentation
$(U,E,s)$ contains auxiliary choices, and pairwise changes of presentation do
not by themselves provide the homotopies and higher compatibilities required
on multiple overlaps.

This chapter changes the order of construction. We first define an intrinsic
moduli problem through its families over every test object. These families
form a stack, so their descent data already include all higher coherence. We
then ask whether the stack is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme.

The main result is open local representability. If a stack is represented on
an effective open atlas, its local representatives glue to a global derived
scheme. The charts are therefore witnesses of local representability, not the
primary definition of the moduli object.

\section{From the coherence problem to stacks}
\label[section]{sec:Overlap_Data_To_Cech_Nerve}

On a double overlap of two Kuranishi charts, one wants an equivalence of their
restrictions. On a triple overlap, the two resulting composites need a
homotopy. On a quadruple overlap, those homotopies must be compatible, and
the pattern continues. Talk 8 ended at precisely this point.

A Cech nerve packages this tower whenever a global object or stack is already
present. For a cover $U\to X$, its terms are
\[
  U_n=\underbrace{U\times_X\cdots\times_XU}_{n+1\text{ factors}}.
\]
Degree zero records charts, degree one records double overlaps, and the higher
degrees record all higher intersections and compatibilities.

This observation does not strictify arbitrary Kuranishi data. It suggests a
different strategy: define the intrinsic functor of families first, verify
descent, and only then construct local representatives. Detailed Cech
constructions and the source status of strict Kuranishi atlases are recorded
in the supplements.

\subsection{Prestacks}
\label[subsection]{sec:Prestacks_Derived_Cinfty}

Write $\mathrm{dAff}_{\Cinfty}$ for a chosen category of affine derived
$\Cinfty$-schemes large enough to contain the test objects under discussion.
Its precise size is irrelevant for the present chapter.

\begin{definition}[Prestack]
  \label[definition]{def:Prestack_Derived_Cinfty}
  A \emph{derived $\Cinfty$-prestack} is a functor
  \[
    \mathcal F\colon
    \mathrm{dAff}_{\Cinfty}\catop
    \longrightarrow
    \An.
  \]
\end{definition}

We interpret $\mathcal F(T)$ as the anima of families parametrized by $T$.
Its points are $T$-families, its paths are equivalences between families, and
its higher homotopies record compatibilities among equivalences. For a map
$a\colon T'\to T$, the induced map
\[
  a^*\colon\mathcal F(T)\longrightarrow\mathcal F(T')
\]
is pullback of families.

It is important that $\mathcal F(T)$ is an anima rather than merely a set of
equivalence classes. Passing immediately to equivalence classes discards
automorphisms and the coherence needed for descent.

\subsection{Descent on the parameter object}
\label[subsection]{sec:Descent_On_Parameter_Object}

Let $\{T_i\to T\}_{i\in I}$ be an open cover and put
\[
  T_0=\coprod_iT_i.
\]
Its Cech nerve is
\[
  T_n
  =
  \coprod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
  T_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap T_{i_n}.
\]
Applying a prestack contravariantly gives a cosimplicial anima
$\mathcal F(T_\bullet)$.

Before giving the definition, let us compare this diagram with the ordinary
sheaf condition. For a set-valued presheaf $F$, the condition for the cover
$\{T_i\to T\}_{i\in I}$ is often written as the equalizer formula
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Classical_Sheaf_Equalizer}
  F(T)
  \iso
  \operatorname{Eq}\left(
    \prod_i F(T_i)
    \rightrightarrows
    \prod_{i,j}F(T_i\cap T_j)
  \right).
\end{equation}
The two arrows restrict a tuple of local sections along the two projections
$T_i\cap T_j\to T_i$ and $T_i\cap T_j\to T_j$. Thus the equalizer consists of
local sections whose restrictions agree on every double overlap. In sets,
this pairwise agreement automatically satisfies the compatibility conditions
on triple and higher overlaps. Equivalently, the right side of
\Cref{eq:Classical_Sheaf_Equalizer} computes the limit of the entire
cosimplicial set obtained from the Cech nerve.

\begin{definition}[Stack]
  \label[definition]{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty}
  A derived $\Cinfty$-prestack $\mathcal F$ is a \emph{stack for the open
  topology} if, for every open cover as above, the restriction map
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech}
    \mathcal F(T)
    \longrightarrow
    \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}\mathcal F(T_n)
  \end{equation}
  is an equivalence of animae.
\end{definition}

This is the higher-categorical version of
\Cref{eq:Classical_Sheaf_Equalizer}. Sets are replaced by animae, equality of
restrictions is replaced by a chosen equivalence, and an ordinary equalizer is
replaced by the limit of the full cosimplicial diagram. The higher terms can
no longer be discarded: they record the coherence of the chosen
equivalences. Thus the limit on the right is the anima of coherent descent
data. Its first levels have a familiar interpretation.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Choose a family $\xi_i\in\mathcal F(T_i)$ on every open.
  \item Choose an equivalence between $\xi_i|_{T_{ij}}$ and
    $\xi_j|_{T_{ij}}$ on every double overlap.
  \item Choose a homotopy comparing the two induced equivalences on every
    triple overlap.
  \item Choose all higher compatibilities on higher overlaps.
\end{enumerate}

The descent condition says more than existence of a glued family. It says
that the anima of global families is equivalent to the anima of descent data.
Consequently, morphisms, automorphisms, and all higher identifications glue as
well.

\begin{proposition}[Representable prestacks satisfy descent]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Representable_Prestacks_Satisfy_Descent}
  If $X$ is a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme, then
  \[
    h_X(T)=\Hom(T,X)
  \]
  is a stack for the open topology.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Maps from $T$ to $X$ are local on $T$. More precisely,
  \Cref{prop:Cech_Reconstruction_Open_Cover} gives
  $T\simeq|T_\bullet|$. Since $\Hom(-,X)$ carries colimits in its first
  variable to limits of animae,
  \[
    \Hom(T,X)
    \simeq
    \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}\Hom(T_n,X).
  \]
\end{proof}

The descent condition above concerns a cover of the parameter object $T$.
Later, an atlas $U\to\mathcal F$ will cover the moduli stack itself. These are
two uses of the same Cech construction, but they answer different questions.
The precise distinction is recorded in
\Cref{sec:Supplement_Two_Cech_Nerves_Talk_Nine}.

\section{Representability and universal families}
\label[section]{sec:Representability_Universal_Families}

\subsection{From a moduli stack to a moduli object}
\label[subsection]{sec:Moduli_Stack_To_Moduli_Object}

A stack can be defined without knowing whether it comes from a derived
scheme. This separation is the main methodological advantage of the
functorial approach.

\begin{definition}[Represented stack]
  \label[definition]{def:Represented_Stack}
  A derived $\Cinfty$-stack $\mathcal F$ is \emph{represented} by a derived
  $\Cinfty$-scheme $X$ if there is a natural equivalence
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Representability_Natural_Equivalence}
    \mathcal F(T)
    \simeq
    \Hom(T,X)
  \end{equation}
  for all affine derived $\Cinfty$-schemes $T$.
\end{definition}

The left side of \Cref{eq:Representability_Natural_Equivalence} is defined in
terms of families. The right side is the functor of points of a geometric
object. A representability theorem proves that these two independently
defined constructions agree.

\begin{proposition}[Universal family]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Universal_Family_Yoneda}
  Suppose that $\mathcal F$ is represented by $X$. There is a family
  \[
    \xi_{\mathrm{univ}}\in\mathcal F(X)
  \]
  such that, for every test object $T$, pullback induces an equivalence
  \[
    \Hom(T,X)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal F(T),
    \qquad
    f\longmapsto f^*\xi_{\mathrm{univ}}.
  \]
  The pair $(X,\xi_{\mathrm{univ}})$ is unique.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Under the representing equivalence
  \[
    \mathcal F(X)\simeq\Hom(X,X),
  \]
  let $\xi_{\mathrm{univ}}$ correspond to $\operatorname{id}_X$. Naturality
  identifies the map associated to $f\colon T\to X$ with pullback of this
  family along $f$. The uniqueness statement is the Yoneda lemma.
\end{proof}

Thus every family is obtained by pulling back one universal family. This is
the precise content of the phrase \emph{moduli object}.

\subsection{The derived zero-locus functor}
\label[subsection]{sec:Derived_Zero_Locus_Functor}

We now work out one complete example. Let
\[
  f\colon U\longrightarrow\R^r
\]
be a smooth map and let $0\colon *\to\R^r$ be the origin. Define a prestack
$\mathcal Z_f$ by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Derived_Zero_Locus_Prestack}
  \mathcal Z_f(T)
  =
  \Hom(T,U)
  \times_{\Hom(T,\R^r)}
  \Hom(T,*).
\end{equation}

Because this pullback is formed in animae, a point of $\mathcal Z_f(T)$
consists of

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A map $x\colon T\to U$.
  \item The unique map $T\to *$.
  \item A path
    \[
      \eta\colon f\circ x\simeq0
    \]
    in $\Hom(T,\R^r)$.
\end{enumerate}

Thus $\mathcal Z_f(T)$ is the anima of $T$-families of solutions together
with a chosen derived equality between the equation and zero.

\begin{theorem}[The zero-locus functor is represented]
  \label[theorem]{thm:Zero_Locus_Functor_Represented}
  The stack $\mathcal Z_f$ is represented by the derived zero locus
  \[
    Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f)
    =
    U\times_{\R^r}*.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The defining universal property of the pullback gives, naturally in $T$,
  \[
    \begin{aligned}
      \Hom\bigl(T,Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f)\bigr)
      &\simeq
      \Hom(T,U)
      \times_{\Hom(T,\R^r)}
      \Hom(T,*)\\
      &=\mathcal Z_f(T).
    \end{aligned}
  \]
  This is exactly the representing equivalence of
  \Cref{def:Represented_Stack}.
\end{proof}

The universal family on $Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f)$ consists of the projection
\[
  q\colon Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f)\longrightarrow U
\]
together with the canonical path
\[
  f\circ q\simeq0
\]
provided by the pullback square. Pulling this pair back along
$T\to Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f)$ recovers $(x,\eta)$.

If $T$ is an ordinary manifold, the mapping animae among the ordinary
manifolds in \Cref{eq:Derived_Zero_Locus_Prestack} are discrete. The path
$f\circ x\simeq0$ is then an ordinary equality, so one recovers classical
smooth families of zeros. For a derived test object $T$, the same path can
carry genuine derived information.

\subsection{Sections of vector bundles}
\label[subsection]{sec:Zero_Locus_Functor_Vector_Bundle_Section}

Let $p\colon E\to U$ be a vector bundle and let
$s,0\colon U\to E$ be a section and the zero section. The derived zero locus
is
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)=U\times_EU.
\]
Its functor of points is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Section_Zero_Locus_Functor_Of_Points}
  \Hom\bigl(T,Z^{\mathrm{der}}(s)\bigr)
  \simeq
  \Hom(T,U)
  \times_{\Hom(T,E)}
  \Hom(T,U),
\end{equation}
where the two maps to $\Hom(T,E)$ are induced by $s$ and $0$.

A point of the right side is initially a pair of maps $x,y\colon T\to U$
and a path $s\circ x\simeq0\circ y$. Applying $p$ to this path gives a path
$x\simeq y$. Consequently, the data can equivalently be described as one map
$x\colon T\to U$ together with a path from the pulled-back section $x^*s$ to
the zero section of $x^*E$.

Locally trivializing $E$ turns this into the preceding construction for a
map to $\R^r$. The global expression
\Cref{eq:Section_Zero_Locus_Functor_Of_Points} shows that the local
descriptions are restrictions of one intrinsic stack. Their changes of frame
are therefore not additional gluing choices.

\section{The local representability theorem}
\label[section]{sec:Local_Representability_Theorem}

The derived zero-locus example was represented globally from the start. In
elliptic moduli problems, one instead constructs finite-dimensional
representatives only after restricting to neighborhoods in the moduli stack
or in its base. We now formulate the theorem that glues them.

\subsection{Representable morphisms}
\label[subsection]{sec:Representable_Morphisms_Stacks}

\begin{definition}[Representable morphism]
  \label[definition]{def:Representable_Morphism_Stacks}
  A morphism
  \[
    p\colon\mathcal X\longrightarrow\mathcal Y
  \]
  of derived $\Cinfty$-stacks is \emph{representable by derived
  $\Cinfty$-schemes} if, for every derived $\Cinfty$-scheme $T$ and every map
  $T\to\mathcal Y$, the pullback
  \[
    T\times_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal X
  \]
  is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme.
\end{definition}

This is a relative condition. The stack $\mathcal Y$ need not itself be
represented. Every representable family of parameters mapping to
$\mathcal Y$ must nevertheless see an honest derived scheme after base
change.

\begin{definition}[Open substack]
  \label[definition]{def:Open_Substack}
  A morphism $\mathcal U\to\mathcal X$ is an \emph{open substack inclusion}
  if it is representable and every base change along a map
  $T\to\mathcal X$ from an affine derived $\Cinfty$-scheme is represented by
  an open embedding of derived $\Cinfty$-schemes.
\end{definition}

An open substack is the functorial form of an open subset. In particular, it
is stable under arbitrary base change.

\begin{definition}[Effective epimorphism and atlas]
  \label[definition]{def:Effective_Epimorphism_Atlas}
  A morphism $q\colon\mathcal U\to\mathcal X$ of stacks is an
  \emph{effective epimorphism} if the canonical map
  \[
    |\check C(q)_\bullet|\longrightarrow\mathcal X
  \]
  from the geometric realization of its Cech nerve is an equivalence. If
  $\mathcal U=\coprod_i\mathcal U_i$ and every
  $\mathcal U_i\to\mathcal X$ is an open substack inclusion, we call the
  family an \emph{open atlas}.
\end{definition}

The terminology generalizes a familiar fact about sets. A surjection
$q\colon U\to X$ exhibits $X$ as the coequalizer of its kernel pair:
\[
  U\times_XU
  \rightrightarrows
  U
  \longrightarrow
  X.
\]
The two copies of an element of $U$ are identified precisely when they have
the same image in $X$. In an $\infty$-category, the kernel pair alone does not
record all coherence among these identifications. It is replaced by the full
Cech nerve, and the coequalizer is replaced by its geometric realization.
The adjective \emph{effective} says that this quotient construction really
recovers the target.

For the open topology, one may read effective epimorphism as follows: every
family in $\mathcal X(T)$ is locally on $T$ pulled back from some
$\mathcal U_i$, and these local lifts satisfy descent. In particular, an open
cover $T_0\to T$ is an effective epimorphism after passing to representable
stacks. Under the Yoneda lemma, the stack condition is obtained by applying
$\Hom(-,\mathcal F)$ to the Cech realization
$|h_{T_\bullet}|\simeq h_T$. Mapping out of this realization turns it into the
limit of coherent descent data.

\begin{definition}[Locally representable morphism]
  \label[definition]{def:Locally_Representable_Morphism_Stacks}
  A morphism $p\colon\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ is \emph{locally
  representable} if there is an effective epimorphism
  \[
    \coprod_i\mathcal U_i\longrightarrow\mathcal Y
  \]
  such that every base change
  \[
    \mathcal U_i\times_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal X
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal U_i
  \]
  is representable.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Open atlases glue}
\label[subsection]{sec:Open_Atlases_Glue}

We can now state the engine of the chapter. Let $\mathcal L$ denote a class
of affine derived $\Cinfty$-schemes stable under open restriction. The main
examples later will be affine quasi-smooth derived $\Cinfty$-schemes of finite
presentation.

\begin{theorem}[Open local representability]
  \label[theorem]{thm:Open_Local_Representability}
  Let $\mathcal X$ be a derived $\Cinfty$-stack locally having the property
  $\mathcal L$ in the following sense: it is obtained from a sheaf on the
  affine objects in $\mathcal L$ by extension to all affine derived
  $\Cinfty$-schemes. The following conditions are equivalent.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The stack $\mathcal X$ is represented by a derived
      $\Cinfty$-scheme locally modeled on objects of $\mathcal L$.
    \item The stack $\mathcal X$ admits an effective open atlas by derived
      $\Cinfty$-schemes locally modeled on $\mathcal L$.
    \item The stack $\mathcal X$ admits an effective open atlas by affine
      derived $\Cinfty$-schemes in $\mathcal L$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

This is Steffens, Proposition~2.1.48
\cite[Proposition~2.1.48]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

\begin{proof}
  The implication from the first condition to the second follows by choosing
  an open cover of the representing derived scheme. Refining every chart by
  affine opens gives the third condition.

  Conversely, suppose that
  \[
    U=\coprod_iU_i\longrightarrow\mathcal X
  \]
  is an effective open atlas as in the third condition. Form its Cech nerve
  $U_\bullet$. In degree one,
  \[
    U_1
    =
    U\times_{\mathcal X}U
    \simeq
    \coprod_{i,j}U_i\times_{\mathcal X}U_j.
  \]
  Since each $U_i\to\mathcal X$ is an open substack inclusion, every
  $U_i\times_{\mathcal X}U_j$ is represented by an open derived subscheme of
  both $U_i$ and $U_j$. The same argument shows that every $U_n$ is a
  coproduct of representable iterated open intersections and that every face
  map is open.

  Thus $U_\bullet$ is an open groupoid object in derived
  $\Cinfty$-schemes. We now quote the effectivity of open groupoid objects:
  its geometric realization exists as a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme $X$, and
  each $U_i\to X$ is an open embedding. The resulting scheme is locally
  modeled on $\mathcal L$, because the charts are.

  Finally, effective epimorphy gives
  \[
    \mathcal X
    \simeq
    |U_\bullet|
    \simeq
    h_X.
  \]
  Hence $X$ represents $\mathcal X$.
\end{proof}

The only quoted step in this proof is the effectivity theorem for open
groupoid objects. It is the derived version of the familiar fact that open
subschemes can be glued along open overlaps. The rest of the proof explains
why the Cech nerve of the atlas has precisely the form to which that theorem
applies.

\subsection{The relative consequence}
\label[subsection]{sec:Relative_Local_Representability_Consequence}

The form needed for elliptic moduli problems is relative.

\begin{corollary}[Local representability is relatively representable]
  \label[corollary]{cor:Local_Representability_Relative}
  Let $\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ be a locally representable morphism of
  derived $\Cinfty$-stacks in the setting of
  \Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}. For every representable map
  $T\to\mathcal Y$, the pullback
  \[
    T\times_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal X
  \]
  is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Choose an effective epimorphism
  $\coprod_i\mathcal U_i\to\mathcal Y$ witnessing local representability.
  After pulling it back to $T$, choose an open cover $\{V_j\to T\}$ such that
  every $V_j\to T\to\mathcal Y$ factors through some $\mathcal U_i$.
  Then
  \[
    V_j\times_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal X
  \]
  is represented, since it is a base change of the representable morphism
  $\mathcal U_i\times_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal X\to\mathcal U_i$.

  These representatives form an effective open atlas of
  $T\times_{\mathcal Y}\mathcal X$. Apply
  \Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}.
\end{proof}

This is Steffens, Corollary~2.1.49
\cite[Corollary~2.1.49]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

There is a small finiteness point to retain. Finite presentation is stable
under open restriction, but Steffens notes that it is not a local property of
affine objects in the stronger sense used for some other geometric
properties. Accordingly, the natural conclusion is that the representing
scheme is \emph{locally} of finite presentation
\cite[Remark~2.1.51]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

\subsection{Automatic coherence from one stack}
\label[subsection]{sec:Functorial_Method_Supplies_Coherence}

Suppose that $p\colon\mathcal F\to S$ is a stack over a derived
$\Cinfty$-scheme and that $\{S_i\to S\}$ is an open cover. Assume that
$S_i\times_S\mathcal F$ is represented by $X_i$.

On a double overlap $S_{ij}$, both $X_i|_{S_{ij}}$ and
$X_j|_{S_{ij}}$ represent the same restricted stack. The Yoneda lemma gives a
unique equivalence between them compatible with their universal families. On
a triple overlap, the composite and direct equivalences are two comparisons
between the same representing pairs, so the homotopy between them is unique.
The same argument supplies every higher compatibility.

\begin{proposition}[Representing objects carry coherent descent data]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Representing_Objects_Canonical_Descent_Data}
  Local representatives of one stack assemble uniquely into a coherent
  descent diagram. If they form an effective open atlas, that diagram is
  represented by the derived $\Cinfty$-scheme supplied by
  \Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}.
\end{proposition}

The functorial method does not turn arbitrary charts into a global object. One
must still define the stack intrinsically, prove that the proposed charts
represent its open restrictions, and verify the hypotheses of the
representability theorem. What it removes is the separate construction of
comparison maps once local representability is known.

\subsection{Handoff to section and solution stacks}
\label[subsection]{sec:Handoff_Section_Solution_Stacks}

Talk 10 will define the intrinsic stack associated to a differential equation.
A family of fields will be a point of a section stack, a finite-order
differential operator will induce a morphism between section stacks, and the
solution stack will be their derived pullback. Its representability will
remain a theorem for later talks.

\section{Summary and supplementary materials}
\label[section]{sec:Coherence_Descent_Summary_Supplementary_Materials}

\subsection{Takeaways}
\label[subsection]{sec:Coherence_Descent_Takeaways}

The live route can be compressed into nine statements.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pairwise transition equivalences are only the first layer of descent
    data.
  \item The Cech nerve packages double overlaps, triple overlaps, and all
    higher compatibilities in one simplicial object.
  \item A stack is a functor of families satisfying coherent descent on the
    parameter object.
  \item A stack need not be represented by a derived scheme.
  \item Representability means that all families are pullbacks of one
    universal family.
  \item The derived zero-locus functor is represented by the derived
    pullback.
  \item An effective open atlas by representable derived schemes glues to a
    representing derived scheme.
  \item Local representatives of one intrinsic stack acquire all transition
    data from the uniqueness of representing objects.
  \item Representability is separate from compactness, orientation data, and
    virtual fundamental classes.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Supplement: Cech nerves and reconstruction}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Cech_Reconstruction_Talk_Nine}

Let $p\colon U\to X$ be a morphism in an $\infty$-category with pullbacks.
Its Cech nerve is the simplicial object with
\[
  U_n=\underbrace{U\times_X\cdots\times_XU}_{n+1\text{ factors}}.
\]
The face maps forget factors and the degeneracy maps repeat them. If
$U=\coprod_iU_i$ is an open cover of an existing derived scheme, then
\[
  U_n\simeq
  \coprod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
  U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_n}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Cech reconstruction]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Cech_Reconstruction_Open_Cover}
  Let $X$ be a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme and let $U\to X$ be an open cover.
  Then the canonical morphism
  \[
    |U_\bullet|\longrightarrow X
  \]
  is an equivalence of derived $\Cinfty$-stacks.
\end{proposition}

The statement begins with an existing $X$. It does not assert that arbitrary
local Kuranishi presentations and proposed higher comparison data arise from
a global derived object.

\subsection{Supplement: two uses of Cech nerves}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Two_Cech_Nerves_Talk_Nine}

There are two Cech nerves in the chapter.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A cover $T_0\to T$ of a test object is used in the stack condition
    \[
      \mathcal F(T)\simeq\lim_{[n]\in\Delta}\mathcal F(T_n).
    \]
    It asks whether a family over $T$ can be reconstructed from local
    families.
  \item An atlas $U\to\mathcal F$ of the moduli stack is used in the
    representability theorem. It asks whether the stack itself can be
    reconstructed from representable local charts.
\end{enumerate}

Confusing them obscures the logical order. Stack descent is part of defining
the moduli functor. An effective representable atlas is extra geometric input
used to prove representability.

\subsection{Supplement: gluing two open derived schemes}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Gluing_Two_Open_Derived_Schemes}

The simplest instance of
\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability} is a cover by two opens. Let $U$ and
$V$ be derived $\Cinfty$-schemes, let $W_U\subseteq U$ and $W_V\subseteq V$
be open derived subschemes, and let
\[
  \varphi\colon W_U\longrightarrow W_V
\]
be an isomorphism of derived $\Cinfty$-schemes. The ordinary topological
spaces glue to
\[
  |X|=|U|\coprod_{|W_U|\cong|W_V|}|V|.
\]
The structure sheaves glue because, on an open subset assembled from
$A\subseteq|U|$ and $B\subseteq|V|$, sections are the pullback
\[
  \mathcal O_U(A)
  \times_{\mathcal O_W(A\cap|W_U|)}
  \mathcal O_V(B),
\]
with the evident interpretation of the restrictions. Descent for animated
$\Cinfty$-rings supplies the higher compatibility.

The resulting locally animated $\Cinfty$-ringed space is a derived
$\Cinfty$-scheme because it is covered by the open derived subschemes $U$ and
$V$. The Cech nerve of $U\coprod V\to X$ repeats $U$, $V$, and $W$ in higher
degrees and records the associativity of this gluing.

This construction still begins with an isomorphism of the overlaps. In the
functorial approach, that isomorphism comes from the fact that the two
restrictions represent the same stack.

\subsection{Supplement: effective epimorphisms and open atlases}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Effective_Epimorphisms_Open_Atlases}

An effective epimorphism need not be an open atlas. It is a morphism whose
Cech nerve reconstructs the target stack. In many geometric contexts, atlases
may be smooth, etale, or submersive rather than open.

The proof of \Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability} uses more than effective
epimorphy. It uses that every chart map is an open substack inclusion. This
ensures that all iterated overlaps are represented by open derived
subschemes, so the Cech nerve can be realized inside the category of derived
$\Cinfty$-schemes.

Steffens's Definition~2.1.42 defines local representability using an effective
epimorphism of the base. Remark~2.1.44 explains that the cover can be chosen
with representable sources
\cite[Definition~2.1.42 and Remark~2.1.44]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
In the proof of the relative corollary, one then refines the pullback of this
cover by genuine open covers of each representable test object.

\subsection{Supplement: the finite-presentation qualification}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Finite_Presentation_Local_Representability}

Let $\mathcal L$ be the class of affine quasi-smooth derived
$\Cinfty$-schemes of finite presentation. This class is stable under principal
open restriction and under the affine overlap operations used in the Cech
nerve. Therefore an open atlas by objects of $\mathcal L$ produces a derived
$\Cinfty$-scheme which is locally quasi-smooth and locally of finite
presentation.

One should not strengthen this conclusion to affineness or global finite
presentation. Open gluing of affine objects usually produces a nonaffine
scheme. Likewise, a scheme covered by finitely presented affine opens is
locally of finite presentation, but additional quasi-compactness data would
be needed for a global finiteness statement.

This is why the relative elliptic representability theorem is naturally
formulated using quasi-smooth derived $\Cinfty$-schemes \emph{locally} of
finite presentation.

\subsection{Supplement: source status of strict Kuranishi atlases}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Source_Status_Strict_Kuranishi_Atlases}

Section~1.0.2 of Steffens's paper contrasts a strict Kuranishi atlas with a
coherent atlas and explains why derived $\Cinfty$-schemes provide the
intrinsic target. It then lists three further assertions: strict atlases should
determine coherent ones, commensurate atlases should determine equivalent
derived schemes, and suitable quasi-smooth derived schemes should admit
strict atlases. The paper immediately states that these assertions will not be
proved there
\cite[Section~1.0.2]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

The established direction used in the seminar is different. Once a derived
$\Cinfty$-stack with an open representable atlas has been constructed,
\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability} proves that it is represented by a
derived $\Cinfty$-scheme. No strictification of arbitrary chart data is
required.

\subsection{Related literature}
\label[subsection]{sec:Coherence_Descent_Related_Literature}

The primary source for local representability is Steffens,
\emph{Representability of Elliptic Moduli Problems in Derived
$C^\infty$-Geometry}. Definition~2.1.42 introduces representable and locally
representable morphisms, Proposition~2.1.48 proves the open-atlas criterion,
and Corollary~2.1.49 gives the relative consequence
\cite[Definition~2.1.42, Proposition~2.1.48, and Corollary~2.1.49]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Section~1.0.2 supplies the Kuranishi-atlas motivation, with the source-status
qualification explained above.

Pardon's \emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm analysis}
gives a short conceptual introduction to the functorial strategy. It contrasts
the delicate construction of compatible Kuranishi charts with the definition
of a moduli functor and emphasizes that representability is a local property
\cite[Introduction]{Pardon_Representability_Elliptic_Fredholm}. The article is
a research summary, and its pseudo-holomorphic representability theorem is
presented with a proof sketch rather than a complete proof.

The general language of effective epimorphisms, Cech nerves, and descent
belongs to higher topos theory. The live route has used only their behavior
for open covers and has quoted the effectivity theorem needed for open gluing.
