\chapter{Stacks and local representability}
\label[chapter]{chap:Coherence_Descent_Local_Representability}

A moduli problem assigns families to parameter objects. For example, a
family of zeros of $f\colon U\to\R^r$ parametrized by $T$ is a map
$T\to U$ satisfying the equation. In derived geometry the equation
includes a specified homotopy to zero. Pulling the family back along
$T'\to T$ gives a family over $T'$.

This viewpoint defines a moduli problem before a geometric representing
object has been constructed. Descent expresses that families and their
isomorphisms can be glued. The main result of this chapter gives a way
to recognize when the resulting stack is a derived scheme: construct
an open cover by representable stacks.

This returns to the coherence problem posed in
\Cref{sec:Local_Zero_Loci_Not_Enough}. Starting with a globally defined
stack supplies the overlaps and their higher compatibilities; the work
is to construct open charts of that functor. We develop descent and
atlases with the vector bundle zero locus as a running example, then
formulate representability relative to a parameter stack. The distinction
between gluing families and gluing charts will be essential throughout.

\section{The open topology and descent}

Descent concerns a cover of a parameter object: it reconstructs an
anima of global families from coherent local families. We specify the
test objects and covers first, then express this reconstruction as a limit.

We use the category $\mathrm{DMfd}$ of derived manifolds as test objects.
By the affine comparison, these are spectra of finitely presented animated
smooth rings. An open embedding is an inclusion of an open subobject
with its restricted structure sheaf. An \emph{open covering family}
$\{T_i\to T\}$ is a family of open embeddings whose underlying images
cover $|T|$. Smooth localization describes these embeddings algebraically.
They are preserved by pullback.

\begin{definition}[Prestack]
\label[definition]{def:Prestack_Derived_Cinfty}
A derived smooth prestack is a functor
$\mathcal F\colon\mathrm{DMfd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\An$.
\end{definition}

The anima $\mathcal F(T)$ contains the $T$-families, their isomorphisms,
and higher identifications between those isomorphisms.
Replacing it by its set of components loses automorphisms as well as
higher coherence. Pullback along a map of parameter objects is part
of the functor.

For an open cover, write $T_{i_0\cdots i_n}$ for the iterated intersection.
Restriction gives a cosimplicial diagram with degree-$n$ term
\[
 C^n=\prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}\mathcal F(T_{i_0\cdots i_n}).
\]
Its face maps restrict to smaller intersections, and its degeneracy maps
repeat indices. The following formula for its limit avoids any
need for an infinite disjoint union to be a test object.

\begin{definition}[Stack]
\label[definition]{def:Stack_Derived_Cinfty}
A prestack is a stack if, for every open covering family, the natural map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech}
 \mathcal F(T)\longrightarrow
 \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}
 \prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}\mathcal F(T_{i_0\cdots i_n})
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism of animae.
\end{definition}

For a set-valued functor this reduces to the usual sheaf condition:
local elements must agree on double overlaps.
For groupoid-valued functors one instead chooses isomorphisms on double
overlaps and imposes the cocycle condition on triple overlaps.
For general animae the cocycle condition itself is supplied by a homotopy,
followed by higher compatibility data. The limit in
\Cref{eq:Stack_Descent_Parameter_Cech} packages all of it.

Descent applies to maps between families too. It says that the anima of
global families is the anima of coherent local data, rather than merely
that some global family exists.

The resulting $\infty$-category of stacks is denoted
$\mathrm{dSt}_{\Cinfty}^{\mathrm{fp}}$ here.
The superscript records our site of finitely presented test objects.
Steffens embeds this category fully faithfully into his category using
all affine derived smooth schemes, and calls its objects stacks locally
of finite presentation
\cite[Proposition~2.1.40 and Definition~2.1.41]
{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
Our constructions take place in this subcategory.

\section{Represented stacks and the zero-locus example}

Representability asks whether all families arise as maps to a geometric
object. The universal property of a derived pullback answers this
question for the zero-locus functor and gives a model for the later
elliptic moduli problem.

For a derived scheme $X$ locally of finite presentation, its functor
of points is
\[
 h_X(T)=\Hom(T,X).
\]
It satisfies descent because morphisms of locally ringed objects can
be glued over an open cover of their source.
More explicitly, the continuous maps on the underlying spaces glue
uniquely. For the resulting map $f\colon|T|\to|X|$, the maps
$f^{-1}\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_T$ glue as morphisms of sheaves,
including all homotopies between them; this is the mapping assertion of
\Cref{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves} below. The condition that
the maps on stalks are local can be checked on the same cover.
This proves descent for the entire mapping anima and hence the
subcanonical property of the open topology.
Moreover, the functor $X\mapsto h_X$ is fully faithful.

\begin{definition}[Representability]
\label[definition]{def:Represented_Stack}
A stack $\mathcal F$ is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme $X$
if there is a natural isomorphism
$\mathcal F(T)\cong\Hom(T,X)$ for every test object $T$.
\end{definition}

The representing object is unique. If $X$ is not affine, the notation
$\mathcal F(X)$ means the value obtained by descent from an affine
open cover. Under representability, the identity of $X$ corresponds
to a universal family in $\mathcal F(X)$. Every other family is its
pullback, by the Yoneda lemma.

Let $f\colon U\to\R^r$ be smooth. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Derived_Zero_Locus_Prestack}
 \mathcal Z_f(T)=\Hom(T,U)\times_{\Hom(T,\R^r)}\Hom(T,*),
\end{equation}
with pullback in animae.
A point is a map $u\colon T\to U$ together with a homotopy from $f\circ u$
to zero. Identifications in this pullback give the isomorphisms between
such data and their higher compatibilities. Limits of stacks are computed on each test object, so
$\mathcal Z_f$ is a stack.

By the universal property of a pullback,
\[
 \mathcal Z_f(T)\cong\Hom(T,U\times_{\R^r}^{\mathrm{der}}*).
\]
Thus it is represented by the derived zero locus computed in \Cref{chap:Derived_Zero_Loci_And_Tangent_Complexes}.
This is a simple instance of the representability question that will
later arise for differential equations.

For a section $s\colon U\to E$ of a vector bundle, the analogous formula
is most conveniently written in the category over $U$:
\[
 \mathcal Z_s=U\times_E^{\mathrm{der}}U,
\]
using $s$ and the zero section. Restricting to a trivializing open
$U_i$ gives the Koszul presentation for its list of local components.
These local representatives will form an open atlas of $\mathcal Z_s$.

\section{Smooth stacks as derived stacks}

A smooth stack is a sheaf of animae on ordinary manifolds with their
open-cover topology. The inclusion of manifolds into derived manifolds
induces a fully faithful functor
\[
 j_!\colon\mathrm{Shv}(\mathrm{Mfd})
 \longrightarrow\mathrm{dSt}_{\Cinfty}^{\mathrm{fp}}.
\]
It is obtained by left Kan extension and sheafification, and sends
an ordinary manifold to its derived functor of points.
We can therefore regard smooth stacks as particular derived stacks.

The derived values of $j_!\mathcal F$ are not obtained just by
evaluating $\mathcal F$ on an underlying point set.
They are determined by this extension construction.
Conversely, restriction to ordinary test manifolds does not recover
every derived stack. For example, a multiple point and an ordinary
point have the same families over ordinary manifolds, while maps
from infinitesimal derived tests distinguish them.

There is also an essential distinction between the two categories of
pullbacks. In smooth stacks,
\[
 *\times_\R *=*
\]
for the two origins. After passing to derived stacks, the pullback
is the derived self-intersection, with algebra $\R[\varepsilon]$,
where $|\varepsilon|=1$ and $d\varepsilon=0$.
Thus $j_!$ does not preserve arbitrary pullbacks.
The pullbacks needed in the analytic argument will be compared using
submersiveness in \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}.

\section{Open atlases and local representability}

We now turn from gluing families over a parameter cover to presenting
the moduli stack itself by charts. Here a \v{C}ech diagram reconstructs
the stack as a colimit. Effectiveness expresses that the charts cover
the functor; openness makes it possible to glue their representing
objects into a derived scheme.
We use two sheaf-theoretic inputs below, with references to Steffens
and heuristic explanations, before giving the geometric proof of
open local representability.

Let $p\colon\mathcal U\to\mathcal F$ be a morphism of stacks.
Its \v{C}ech nerve is the simplicial stack
\[
 \mathcal U_n=
 \underbrace{\mathcal U\times_{\mathcal F}\cdots
 \times_{\mathcal F}\mathcal U}_{n+1\text{ factors}}.
\]
The augmentation maps its geometric realization to $\mathcal F$.
Here geometric realization means a colimit in stacks, computed by
taking the presheaf colimit and then sheafifying.

\begin{definition}[Effective epimorphism]
\label[definition]{def:Effective_Epimorphism}
The map $p$ is an effective epimorphism if
$|\mathcal U_\bullet|\to\mathcal F$ is an isomorphism.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Local lifting criterion]
\label[lemma]{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}
A map $p\colon\mathcal U\to\mathcal F$ is an effective epimorphism
if and only if, for every test object $T$ and every $x\in\mathcal F(T)$,
there is an open cover $\{T_a\to T\}$ on which $x$ lifts to
$\mathcal U$. A lift includes an identification of its image with
$x|_{T_a}$.
Effective epimorphisms are preserved by pullback.
\end{lemma}

We take this standard sheaf-theoretic criterion as an input.
For the reduction to sheaves on underlying topological spaces, see
\citet[Lemma~2.1.38]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli};
the local lifting criterion is used explicitly in the proof of
Corollary~2.1.49 there.

\begin{proof}[Heuristic explanation]
For a map of animae $A\to B$, the realization of its \v{C}ech nerve
is the union of the components of $B$ met by $A$. Indeed, its fibre
over $b\in B$ is the realization of the \v{C}ech nerve of
$A_b\to *$. This is empty if $A_b$ is empty. Otherwise, choosing a
point of $A_b$ contracts the augmented nerve by inserting that point,
so its realization is a point.

Apply this observation on each test object. The presheaf realization
of $\mathcal U_\bullet$ is the subpresheaf $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal F$
whose value on $T$ consists of the components containing an image
of $\mathcal U(T)$. Its sheafification is the substack of families
which locally belong to $\mathcal I$: this is a local condition,
and all identifications between such families are inherited from
$\mathcal F$. A map from $\mathcal I$ to any stack extends uniquely
to this substack by descent on the covers witnessing local membership,
which verifies the universal property of sheafification.
This substack is all of $\mathcal F$ precisely when
the stated local lifts exist. Since realization in stacks is
presheaf realization followed by sheafification, this proves the criterion.

If $\mathcal G\to\mathcal F$ is any map, a family in $\mathcal G(T)$
can locally be lifted to $\mathcal U$ together with an identification
in $\mathcal F(T)$. These are exactly the data of a local lift to
$\mathcal U\times_{\mathcal F}\mathcal G$. Thus the criterion also
proves stability under pullback.
\end{proof}

For a family $\{\mathcal U_i\to\mathcal F\}$, the criterion says
that each family in $\mathcal F(T)$ locally lifts to one of the
$\mathcal U_i$. A map into a coproduct of stacks locally lands in
one summand, since that coproduct is the sheafification of the
pointwise coproduct. This accounts for the possible refinement of
the parameter cover when applying the criterion to a family.

Starting with a globally defined stack supplies the overlap data.
If $U_i\to\mathcal F$ and $U_j\to\mathcal F$ are two charts, their
overlap is $U_i\times_{\mathcal F}U_j$. Its associativity and higher
compatibilities come from pullbacks in the ambient $\infty$-category.
The following condition makes these overlaps geometric open subsets.

\begin{definition}[Open atlas]
\label[definition]{def:Open_Atlas}
A morphism $\mathcal V\to\mathcal F$ is an open inclusion if every
pullback along $T\to\mathcal F$ is represented by an open subobject of $T$.
An open atlas is a small family of open inclusions $h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$
from derived schemes such that
$\coprod_i h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$ is an effective epimorphism.
\end{definition}

The coproduct here is in stacks. Each map is a condition on families:
after choosing a family over $T$, belonging to the indicated chart
cuts out an open subset of $T$.

The word \emph{open} is crucial for representing a stack by a scheme.
For example, a nontrivial finite group $G$ has a classifying stack $BG$.
The map $*\to BG$ is an effective epimorphism, expressing local
triviality of principal $G$-bundles. It is not an open inclusion:
its pullback along itself is $G\to*$.
The automorphisms of the trivial bundle also show that $BG$ cannot be
represented by a derived scheme, whose real points form a set.
More general notions of atlas lead to orbifolds and other geometric
stacks, but an arbitrary effective epimorphism is insufficient for
scheme representability.

To reconstruct a derived scheme, we must glue both its topology and
its animated structure sheaf. Our second input concerns this sheaf
construction. In the following lemma, coherent identifications mean
isomorphisms on double intersections, homotopies between their
composites on triple intersections, and all higher compatibilities.

\begin{lemma}[Gluing structure sheaves]
\label[lemma]{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves}
Let $Y$ be a topological space with a small open cover $\{V_i\}$.
Suppose that each $V_i$ carries a sheaf $\mathcal A_i$ of animated
$\Cinfty$-rings, with coherent identifications of their restrictions
to the intersections. There is a sheaf $\mathcal A$ on $Y$ whose
restrictions, with their identifications, recover this datum.
It is unique, and maps between such sheaves are likewise determined
by their coherent restrictions to the cover.
\end{lemma}

We also take this descent statement as an input. It is the sheaf
gluing mechanism in the proof of
\citet[Proposition~2.1.18]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli};
Remark~2.1.19(c) there describes the structure sheaf by the limit
formula explained below.

\begin{proof}[Heuristic explanation]
Write $V_{i_0\cdots i_n}=V_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap V_{i_n}$.
For an open subset $W\subseteq Y$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Glued_Animated_Structure_Sheaf}
 \mathcal A(W)=
 \lim_{[n]\in\Delta}
 \prod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
 \mathcal A_{i_0}(W\cap V_{i_0\cdots i_n}).
\end{equation}
The transition identifications supply the maps which change the first
index, and their coherences make this a cosimplicial diagram.
All limits are taken in the $\infty$-category of animated smooth rings.
Each functor of $W$ occurring on the right is a sheaf on $Y$:
an open cover of $W$ restricts to an open cover of its intersection
with $V_{i_0\cdots i_n}$. Limits of sheaves are computed on opens,
because limits commute with the limits expressing descent.
Thus the formula defines a sheaf.

If $W\subseteq V_k$, the transition identifications identify the
diagram with the descent diagram of $\mathcal A_k$ for the cover
$\{W\cap V_i\}$ of $W$. Hence the sheaf condition gives
$\mathcal A(W)\cong\mathcal A_k(W)$. These isomorphisms recover
the specified transition data, including its higher coherences.

Conversely, a sheaf on $Y$ is recovered from its restrictions by
the same formula, using descent for $\{W\cap V_i\}$. The construction
is functorial in the coherent restriction data, so gluing and
restriction are inverse functors. This proves both uniqueness and
the assertion about maps, including homotopies between them.
\end{proof}

Using these two inputs, we can now prove that an open atlas contains
all the data required for representability.

\begin{theorem}[Open local representability]
\label[theorem]{thm:Open_Local_Representability}
A derived smooth stack is represented by a derived $\Cinfty$-scheme
locally of finite presentation if and only if it has an open atlas
by affine derived schemes of finite presentation.
One may equivalently allow derived schemes locally of finite presentation
as the atlas objects.
\end{theorem}

This is \citet[Proposition~2.1.48]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\mathcal F=h_X$, and choose an affine open cover
$\{U_i\to X\}$. Each $h_{U_i}\to h_X$ is an open inclusion:
its pullback along $T\to X$ is the open subscheme $T\times_X U_i$.
These pullbacks cover $T$. Refining them by affine opens gives local
lifts of every $T\to X$ to the charts. By
\Cref{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}, the chart family is
an effective epimorphism and hence an open atlas.

Conversely, let $p_i\colon h_{U_i}\to\mathcal F$ be an affine open
atlas. An open inclusion is a monomorphism: on each test object its
homotopy fibres are either empty or a point, since a factorization
through an open subscheme, when it exists, is unique. In particular,
the diagonal gives $h_{U_i}\cong h_{U_i}\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_i}$.
For every pair of indices, write
\[
 h_{U_{ij}}=h_{U_i}\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_j}.
\]
By openness, this is represented by an open subscheme of $U_i$,
and its other projection identifies it with an open subscheme of
$U_j$. Write $W_{ij}\subseteq|U_i|$ for the first image and
$\varphi_{ij}\colon W_{ij}\to W_{ji}$ for the resulting homeomorphism.

The diagonal, symmetry, and composition in the \v{C}ech nerve give
$\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$,
$\varphi_{ji}=\varphi_{ij}^{-1}$, and
$\varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{ik}$ wherever the composite is
defined. For the last assertion, a pair of composable overlap points
comes from
$U_{ij}\times_{U_j}U_{jk}$, which represents
$h_{U_i}\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_j}\times_{\mathcal F}h_{U_k}$;
the remaining projection gives the corresponding point of $U_{ik}$.
Passing to underlying topological spaces turns the specified
homotopies into equalities.

Define a topological space
\[
 |X|=\left(\coprod_i|U_i|\right)\big/\sim,
 \qquad
 x\sim\varphi_{ij}(x)\quad(x\in W_{ij}),
\]
with the quotient topology. The preceding identities show that the
displayed relation is already an equivalence relation. It identifies
no two distinct points of a single $|U_i|$, because $W_{ii}=|U_i|$
and $\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id}$. Each induced map
$q_i\colon|U_i|\to|X|$ is therefore injective.
It is also open: for $O\subseteq|U_i|$ open, the inverse image
of $q_i(O)$ in $|U_j|$ is
$\varphi_{ij}(O\cap W_{ij})$, which is open. The definition of
the quotient topology then makes $q_i(O)$ open in $|X|$.
Thus $q_i$ is a homeomorphism onto an open subset $V_i$, the
$V_i$ cover $|X|$, and $q_i^{-1}(V_i\cap V_j)=W_{ij}$.

Transport $\mathcal O_{U_i}$ to $V_i$ along $q_i$.
The two open embeddings from $U_{ij}$ identify the restrictions
of $\mathcal O_{U_i}$ and $\mathcal O_{U_j}$ on $V_i\cap V_j$.
All the required coherences are supplied by the higher terms of
the original \v{C}ech nerve. Apply
\Cref{lem:Gluing_Animated_Structure_Sheaves} to obtain
$\mathcal O_X$ on $|X|$.
At a point in $V_i$, its stalk is the corresponding stalk of
$\mathcal O_{U_i}$, so its degree-zero ring is local with the
same residue map to $\R$. Consequently
$X=(|X|,\mathcal O_X)$ is a locally animated smooth-ringed space,
and $(V_i,\mathcal O_X|_{V_i})\cong U_i$.
Since the $U_i$ are affine of finite presentation, this is a derived
$\Cinfty$-scheme locally of finite presentation by
\Cref{def:Derived_Cinfty_Scheme}.
No global Hausdorff or second-countability condition is imposed on
a derived scheme; imposing either would require an additional
hypothesis on this gluing datum.

It remains to identify its functor of points with $\mathcal F$.
The open subscheme $U_i\times_X U_j$ has underlying open subset
$W_{ij}\subseteq|U_i|$ and structure sheaf
$\mathcal O_{U_i}|_{W_{ij}}$. Hence its two maps to the charts
identify it with $U_{ij}$. The same argument on iterated intersections
identifies all higher overlaps, with the compatibilities used in
constructing $\mathcal O_X$.

Set $\mathcal U=\coprod_i h_{U_i}$, with coproduct in stacks.
The chart embeddings give a map $q\colon\mathcal U\to h_X$.
For every test map $T\to X$, the open subschemes $T\times_X U_i$
cover $T$, so the local lifting criterion shows that $q$ is an
effective epimorphism. Its \v{C}ech nerve in degree $n$ is
\[
 \coprod_{i_0,\ldots,i_n}
 h_{U_{i_0}\times_X\cdots\times_X U_{i_n}}.
\]
Here pullbacks distribute over coproducts of stacks: locally on a
test object a map into a coproduct chooses a summand, so the two
sides have the same local families and identifications.
Also, functors of points preserve the displayed pullbacks by their
defining universal property. The overlap identifications above
therefore identify this simplicial stack with the \v{C}ech nerve
of $p\colon\mathcal U\to\mathcal F$.
Since both augmentations are effective epimorphisms, realization gives
\[
 h_X\cong|\check C(q)_\bullet|
 \cong|\check C(p)_\bullet|\cong\mathcal F.
\]
This is an isomorphism of stacks, and thus identifies the animae
of families on every derived test object.

Finally, suppose the atlas objects are only locally of finite
presentation. Choose an affine open cover of each $U_i$.
The composites of these open inclusions with $p_i$ are open.
A family in $\mathcal F(T)$ first locally lifts to some $U_i$,
and then, after pulling back its affine cover, locally lifts to
one of these affine charts. The local lifting criterion shows that
the refined family is again an effective epimorphism. The affine
case just proved applies.
\end{proof}

In the bundle-section example, the charts are the zero loci over a
trivializing cover of $U$. Their overlaps are the zero loci over the
corresponding intersections. The transition maps of the bundle identify
the Koszul models, and the theorem recovers the global derived zero locus.

The same argument preserves a local property such as quasi-smoothness.
If the charts are quasi-smooth, the cotangent amplitude condition
holds on the resulting scheme.

\section{Relative representability and base change}

Moduli problems often have a parameter stack $S$.
For example, the metric, the domain manifold, or a coefficient in an
equation may vary. The correct representability statement is then
relative to $S$.

\begin{definition}
\label[definition]{def:Relative_Representability}
A map $\mathcal F\to S$ is representable by derived $\Cinfty$-schemes
locally of finite presentation if, for each affine derived test
$T\to S$, the pullback $\mathcal F\times_S T$ is represented by such
a scheme. It is relatively quasi-smooth if these representing
morphisms to $T$ are quasi-smooth.
\end{definition}

This asserts representability of every pulled-back family.
It does not assert that the total stack $\mathcal F$ is itself a scheme
when $S$ has automorphisms.

\begin{corollary}[Locality on the base]
\label[corollary]{cor:Local_Representability_Relative}
Representability by derived schemes locally of finite presentation
can be checked after an effective epimorphic covering family of the base.
The same holds with the additional requirement of relative
quasi-smoothness.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $\{S_i\to S\}$ be a family whose coproduct is an effective
epimorphism, and suppose each
$\mathcal F_i=\mathcal F\times_S S_i\to S_i$ is representable.
Fix an affine derived test $T\to S$. By
\Cref{lem:Effective_Epimorphism_Local_Lifting}, there is an open
cover $\{T_a\to T\}$ and, for each $a$, a lift
$T_a\to S_{i(a)}$ with a specified identification over $S$.
Refine the cover so that each $T_a$ is affine of finite presentation.
The pullback identity
\[
 \mathcal F\times_S T_a
 \cong\mathcal F_{i(a)}\times_{S_{i(a)}}T_a
\]
and the assumed relative representability give a representing
scheme $X_a$ locally of finite presentation.

Put $\mathcal G=\mathcal F\times_S T$.
Each $h_{X_a}\to\mathcal G$ is the pullback of the open inclusion
$T_a\to T$, so is open. Their coproduct is an effective epimorphism:
for any test map $R\to\mathcal G$, the opens $R\times_T T_a$
cover $R$ and give local lifts to the $h_{X_a}$.
\Cref{thm:Open_Local_Representability}, including its non-affine
atlas formulation, therefore represents $\mathcal G$ by a scheme
$X$ locally of finite presentation. This works for every $T\to S$
and proves the required relative representability.

If every $\mathcal F_i\to S_i$ is relatively quasi-smooth, then
$X_a\to T_a$ is quasi-smooth by its definition on affine tests.
Moreover $X_a=X\times_T T_a$. Base change for relative cotangent
complexes identifies $\mathbb L_{X/T}|_{X_a}$ with
$\mathbb L_{X_a/T_a}$. Perfection and Tor-amplitude in $[0,1]$
can be checked on this open cover of $X$, so $X\to T$ is
quasi-smooth. Conversely, representability and relative
quasi-smoothness are preserved by base change, so the asserted
conditions are necessary as well.
\end{proof}

The source formulation is
\cite[Definition~2.1.42 and Corollary~2.1.49]
{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
This permits a proof over a smooth stack to be carried out on manifold
charts of the base, provided the moduli construction is compatible
with pullback.

\section{Further reading}

The local representability criterion does not make the construction of
a chart automatic.
One must identify the entire derived functor on an open neighbourhood,
including its values on derived tests. An identification of real
solution sets, or even of their tangent complexes, proves less.
The later Kuranishi argument is designed to establish precisely
this functorial identification.

\begin{exercise}
Let $f\colon U\to\R^r$ and let $\{U_i\}$ cover $U$.
Use the functor-of-points formula to verify that the corresponding
zero loci form an effective open atlas of $\mathcal Z_f$.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
For a nontrivial finite group $G$, calculate $*\times_{BG}*$ and
explain both the effectiveness of $*\to BG$ and its failure to be
an open inclusion.
\end{exercise}

All the descent and representability results used in this chapter are
contained in \cite[Section~2.1]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
