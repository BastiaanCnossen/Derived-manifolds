\chapter{Section stacks and derived solution stacks}
\label[chapter]{chap:Section_Stacks_Jets_Solution_Stacks}

Talk 9 introduced intrinsic moduli stacks and the local representability
theorem. We now construct the stack associated to a differential equation.
The construction must work for families over arbitrary test objects before
any representing derived scheme has been found.

Three ingredients are needed. A \emph{section stack} records families of
fields. A \emph{relative jet stack} makes finite-order differential operators
act functorially on such families. The \emph{solution stack} imposes the
equation by a derived pullback.

Our running example is
\[
  P(u)=\Delta u+u^2
\]
on a closed connected Riemannian manifold. Its ordinary solution set is one
point, but its linearization has both a kernel and a cokernel. The main payoff
of the chapter is that these appear as the cohomology of the intrinsic tangent
complex of the solution stack.

The chapter defines this stack and calculates its linearization. It does not
prove finite-dimensionality or representability. Talk 11 will construct one
finite-dimensional analytic model near a fixed solution.

\section{From one equation to families of sections}
\label[section]{sec:One_Point_Hidden_Deformation_Theory}

\subsection{A nonlinear elliptic equation}
\label[subsection]{sec:Nonlinear_Laplacian_Square_Equation}

Let $M$ be a closed connected Riemannian manifold. We use the nonnegative
Laplacian
\[
  \Delta=d^*d
\]
on smooth functions and consider the nonlinear operator
\[
  P\colon C^\infty(M)\longrightarrow C^\infty(M),
  \qquad
  P(u)=\Delta u+u^2.
\]

\begin{proposition}[The ordinary solution set]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Laplacian_Square_Ordinary_Solutions}
  The only smooth solution of
  \[
    \Delta u+u^2=0
  \]
  is $u=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Since $M$ is closed, Stokes' theorem gives
  \[
    \int_M\Delta u=0.
  \]
  If $P(u)=0$, it follows that
  \[
    0
    =\int_M(\Delta u+u^2)
    =\int_Mu^2.
  \]
  Hence $u$ vanishes identically.
\end{proof}

As a set, the solution space therefore contains no visible singularity. It is
just $\{0\}$.

\subsection{The linearization puzzle}
\label[subsection]{sec:Linearization_Puzzle_Laplacian_Square}

The derivative of $P$ at a function $u$ is
\[
  D_uP(v)=\Delta v+2uv.
\]
At the unique solution, this becomes
\[
  D_0P=\Delta.
\]
Compact Hodge theory gives
\[
  \ker(\Delta)\cong\R,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{coker}(\Delta)\cong\R.
\]
Indeed, the kernel consists of the constant functions, and the image consists
of the functions with integral zero. Thus the equation has one infinitesimal
deformation direction and one obstruction direction at its only solution.

This produces the guiding question of the chapter.

\begin{question}
  \label[question]{question:One_Point_Remembers_Laplacian_Kernel_Cokernel}
  What geometric object has ordinary solution set $\{0\}$ but tangent complex
  \[
    [C^\infty(M)\xrightarrow{\Delta}C^\infty(M)]
  \]
  at that point?
\end{question}

We will not attach this complex to the point as auxiliary data. Instead, we
will define the equation as a pullback in derived $\Cinfty$-stacks. The
complex will then follow by linearizing that pullback, exactly as in the
finite-dimensional calculation of Chapter~5.

\subsection{From one section to a family}
\label[subsection]{sec:From_One_Section_To_Family}

Before imposing a differential equation, we must say what a family of fields
is. If $T$ is an ordinary parameter manifold, a $T$-family of functions on
$M$ is one smooth map
\[
  u\colon T\times M\longrightarrow\R.
\]
Equivalently, it is a section of the pulled-back trivial line bundle
\[
  T\times M\times\R\longrightarrow T\times M.
\]
This formulation still makes sense when $T$ is derived. It is not a map from
the set of points of $T$ to the set $C^\infty(M)$.

We now pass to a relative geometric situation. Let
\[
  Y\longrightarrow M\xrightarrow{q}S
\]
be a diagram of derived $\Cinfty$-stacks. In the main geometric application,
$M\to S$ is a family of manifolds and $Y\to M$ is a family of submersions.
For a test object $a\colon T\to S$, write
\[
  M_T=T\times_SM,
  \qquad
  Y_T=T\times_SY.
\]
The map $Y_T\to M_T$ is the family obtained after changing the parameter
object from $S$ to $T$.

\begin{definition}[Section stack]
  \label[definition]{def:Section_Stack}
  The \emph{section stack} of $Y\to M$ relative to $M\to S$ is the object
  \[
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)\longrightarrow S
  \]
  characterized by natural equivalences
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Section_Stack_Universal_Property}
    \Hom_S\bigl(T,\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)\bigr)
    \simeq
    \Hom_{M_T}(M_T,Y_T)
  \end{equation}
  for all $T\to S$.
\end{definition}

The right side of \Cref{eq:Section_Stack_Universal_Property} is the anima of
sections of $Y_T\to M_T$. Thus the definition has a short geometric reading:

\begin{center}
  A $T$-point of the section stack is a section after base change to $T$.
\end{center}

Steffens denotes this object by
\[
  \operatorname{Res}_{M/S}(Y)
\]
and calls it the \emph{Weil restriction} of $Y$ along $M\to S$. The name
records its categorical construction. Pullback along $q$ defines a functor
from the slice $\infty$-category of derived $\Cinfty$-stacks over $S$ to the
corresponding slice over $M$, sending $T$ to $T\times_SM$.
Because the $\infty$-category of derived $\Cinfty$-stacks is locally
Cartesian closed, this functor admits a right adjoint $q_*$. One has
\[
  \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)
  =\operatorname{Res}_{M/S}(Y)
  =q_*Y.
\]
The adjunction formula for $q^*\dashv q_*$ is precisely
\Cref{eq:Section_Stack_Universal_Property}. We quote the existence of this
right adjoint. The universal property, rather than its construction, will do
all of the work below.

\begin{example}[Families of functions]
  \label[example]{ex:Section_Stack_Families_Of_Functions}
  Take $S=*$ and $Y=M\times\R$. Then
  \[
    \Hom\bigl(T,\operatorname{Sect}_{M}(M\times\R)\bigr)
    \simeq
    \Hom_M(T\times M,T\times M\times\R).
  \]
  A point of the right side is a map $T\times M\to\R$. Consequently,
  $\operatorname{Sect}_{M}(M\times\R)$ is the stack of families of smooth
  functions on $M$. Its ordinary points form $C^\infty(M)$, but the stack
  remembers its behavior on all parameter objects.
\end{example}

\subsection{Base change and descent}
\label[subsection]{sec:Section_Stacks_Base_Change_Descent}

The main formal advantage of the section stack is its compatibility with
changes of parameters.

\begin{proposition}[Base change for section stacks]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Section_Stack_Base_Change}
  Let $S'\to S$ be a morphism and put
  \[
    M_{S'}=S'\times_SM,
    \qquad
    Y_{S'}=S'\times_SY.
  \]
  There is a natural equivalence over $S'$
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Section_Stack_Base_Change}
    S'\times_S\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)
    \simeq
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M_{S'}/S'}(Y_{S'}).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Let $T\to S'$ be a test object. The universal properties of pullback and
  the section stack give
  \begin{align*}
    \Hom_{S'}\bigl(
      T,S'\times_S\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)
    \bigr)
    &\simeq
    \Hom_S\bigl(T,\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)\bigr)\\
    &\simeq
    \Hom_{M_T}(M_T,Y_T)\\
    &\simeq
    \Hom_{S'}\bigl(
      T,\operatorname{Sect}_{M_{S'}/S'}(Y_{S'})
    \bigr).
  \end{align*}
  These equivalences are natural in $T$, so the claim follows from Yoneda.
\end{proof}

In words, taking sections after pulling back the geometric family is the same
as pulling back the section stack. This is the precise sense in which a
single construction organizes sections for all parameter objects.

There is also no separate descent problem to solve. The right adjoint $q_*$
is formed inside the $\infty$-category of stacks. Hence
$\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)$ is already a stack, and its values on an open
cover of a parameter object satisfy the coherent descent condition from
Chapter~9. Concretely, local families of sections which agree coherently on
overlaps glue to a global family.

\subsection{The local model near a section}
\label[subsection]{sec:Local_Model_Near_Section}

The existence of the section stack is formal. Its local smooth geometry is
not. We record only the part needed for the tangent calculation at the end of
the chapter.

Suppose that $S$ is a manifold, that $M\to S$ is locally a product with
compact fiber, and that $Y\to M$ is a family of submersions. Let $s\in S$ and
let
\[
  \sigma\colon M_s\longrightarrow Y_s
\]
be a section. A fiberwise local addition identifies a neighborhood of the
image of $\sigma$ with a neighborhood of the zero section in the vertical
tangent bundle $\sigma^*T_{Y_s/M_s}$. After restricting to a neighborhood
$S'\subseteq S$ of $s$, this produces an open section-stack chart modeled on
\[
  S'\times
  \operatorname{Sect}_{M_s}
  \bigl(\sigma^*T_{Y_s/M_s}\bigr)
\]
whose image contains $\sigma$. This is the focused consequence of Steffens's
Proposition~3.2.22 that we will use
\cite[Proposition~3.2.22]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

At the zero section, the relative tangent space of this chart is the vector
space of smooth sections. Thus
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Relative_Tangent_Section_Stack}
  T_\sigma\bigl(\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)/S\bigr)
  \cong
  \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma^*T_{Y_s/M_s}\bigr).
\end{equation}
An infinitesimal variation of a section is a vertical vector field along it.
The word \emph{relative} matters: the parameter $s$ is held fixed. An
absolute tangent object would also see variations of the base.

Compactness and the submersion hypotheses enter this local smoothness
analysis. They are not needed merely to define
$\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)$.

\section{Relative jets and differential operators}
\label[section]{sec:Relative_Jets_Differential_Operators}

\subsection{Why finite order matters}
\label[subsection]{sec:Why_Finite_Order_Matters}

An arbitrary map between spaces of sections need not be local. Its value at a
point might depend on the section everywhere on $M$. A differential operator
of order at most $k$ has a much stronger property: its value at $x$ depends
only on the value and derivatives of the section at $x$ through order $k$.

Let $Y\to M$ be a submersion of manifolds. The fiber of the classical jet
bundle
\[
  J^k_M(Y)\longrightarrow M
\]
at $x$ consists of equivalence classes of local sections near $x$, where two
sections are equivalent if their derivatives through order $k$ agree at
$x$. For a function $u$, a connection gives the schematic description
\[
  j_x^2u=(u(x),d_xu,\nabla_x^2u).
\]
The connection supplies a convenient splitting, but the second jet itself is
intrinsic.

If $M\to S$ varies in a family, the operator should differentiate only along
the fibers. A family parametrized by $T\to S$ is a section over
$M_T\to T$, and its derivatives in the $T$-directions are irrelevant to the
fiberwise equation. This leads to the following slogan.

\begin{center}
  A relative jet remembers derivatives along $M\to S$, not in the parameter
  direction.
\end{center}

For a family of submersions $Y\to M\to S$, the fiber of
$J^k_{M/S}(Y)\to M$ over a point $x\in M_s$ is therefore the space of
$k$-jets at $x$ of local sections of $Y_s\to M_s$.

\subsection{Relative jets and jet prolongation}
\label[subsection]{sec:Infinitesimal_Diagonal_Relative_Jets}

Let $(M/S)^{(k)}$ be the $k$th infinitesimal neighborhood of the relative
diagonal in $M\times_SM$, with projections
\[
  \pi_1,\pi_2\colon(M/S)^{(k)}\longrightarrow M.
\]
For ordinary manifolds it is locally cut out by
$I_\Delta^{k+1}$. Over a general smooth stack its construction and base-change
functoriality are obtained by descent.

\begin{definition}[Relative jet stack]
  \label[definition]{def:Relative_Jet_Stack}
  For $Y\to M$, define
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Relative_Jet_Stack_Definition}
    J^k_{M/S}(Y)
    =
    \operatorname{Res}_{(M/S)^{(k)}/M}
    \bigl(Y\times_{M,\pi_1}(M/S)^{(k)}\bigr),
  \end{equation}
  where the Weil restriction is taken along $\pi_2$.
\end{definition}

The variance has a geometric meaning. Pull $Y$ back along the first
projection, then take sections over the vertically thickened point described
by the second projection. The result classifies fiberwise $k$-jets.

We quote the properties needed below.

\begin{proposition}[Properties of relative jets]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Relative_Jet_Properties}
  Relative jets commute with base change,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Relative_Jets_Base_Change}
    J^k_{M_{S'}/S'}(Y_{S'})
    \simeq
    S'\times_SJ^k_{M/S}(Y),
  \end{equation}
  recover the classical relative jet bundle for ordinary families, and
  preserve the relevant open restrictions and smoothness properties.
\end{proposition}

These statements are part of Steffens's Proposition~3.3.9
\cite[Proposition~3.3.9]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}. Their
proof and the complete list of properties are supplementary.

The universal section over
$\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)\times_SM$ restricts to every relative
infinitesimal neighborhood. Adjunction gives the \emph{jet prolongation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Jet_Prolongation_Section_Stack}
  j^k\colon
  \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)
  \longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}\bigl(J^k_{M/S}(Y)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Finite-order differential operator]
  \label[definition]{def:Finite_Order_Differential_Operator_Families}
  A family of nonlinear differential operators of order at most $k$ is a
  morphism
  \[
    \widetilde P\colon J^k_{M/S}(Y)\longrightarrow X
  \]
  over $M$.
\end{definition}

It induces the morphism of section stacks
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Differential_Operator_On_Section_Stacks}
  \begin{aligned}
    P\colon
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)
    &\xrightarrow{j^k}
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}\bigl(J^k_{M/S}(Y)\bigr)\\
    &\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Sect}(\widetilde P)}
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both stages commute with base change.

\begin{example}[The running operator as a jet map]
  \label[example]{ex:Laplacian_Square_As_Jet_Map}
  For $S=*$ and $Y=X=M\times\R$, the metric defines
  \[
    \widetilde P\colon J^2_M(M\times\R)\longrightarrow M\times\R,
    \qquad
    j_x^2u\longmapsto d^*du(x)+u(x)^2.
  \]
  The induced morphism of section stacks has ordinary points
  $u\mapsto\Delta u+u^2$.
\end{example}

\section{The derived stack of solutions}
\label[section]{sec:Derived_Stack_Of_Solutions}

\subsection{The zero-fiber case}
\label[subsection]{sec:Solution_Stack_Zero_Fiber_Case}

Suppose first that $X\to M$ is a vector bundle. Its zero section determines a
family of zero sections after every base change, hence a map
\[
  0\colon S\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X).
\]
Let $P\colon\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)\to
\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)$ be induced by a finite-order differential
operator.

\begin{definition}[Solution stack of a zero equation]
  \label[definition]{def:Solution_Stack_Zero_Equation}
  The \emph{solution stack} of the equation $P=0$ is the pullback
  \[
  \begin{tikzcd}
    \operatorname{Sol}(P) \rar \dar \drar[pullback]
      & \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y) \dar["P"] \\
    S \rar["0"']
      & \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)
  \end{tikzcd}
  \]
  in derived $\Cinfty$-stacks over $S$.
\end{definition}

For $S=*$, this is the derived fiber of $P$ over zero. It is the direct
infinite-dimensional analogue of the finite-dimensional derived zero locus
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{der}}(f)=U\times_{\R^r}\{0\}.
\]
At this point, the adjective \emph{infinite-dimensional} describes the
section stacks entering the presentation. It does not assert that the
solution stack is represented by an infinite-dimensional geometric object.
Representability has not yet been addressed.

Let $T\to S$ be a test object. Mapping $T$ into the defining pullback shows
that a $T$-point of $\operatorname{Sol}(P)$ consists of

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A section $u\colon M_T\to Y_T$.
  \item The zero section of $X_T\to M_T$.
  \item A path
    \[
      \eta\colon P(u)\simeq0
    \]
    in the anima of sections of $X_T\to M_T$.
\end{enumerate}

The equation is imposed by a derived equality. We do not first take the
underlying set on which an ordinary equality happens. If $T$ and all the
geometric data are classical, the relevant mapping animae are discrete and
this reduces to the usual equation $P(u)=0$.

\begin{example}[The derived solution stack of the running equation]
  \label[example]{ex:Derived_Solution_Stack_Laplacian_Square}
  The operator from \Cref{ex:Laplacian_Square_As_Jet_Map} defines
  \[
    \operatorname{Sol}(P)
    =
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M}(M\times\R)
    \times_{\operatorname{Sect}_{M}(M\times\R)}*.
  \]
  The first map to the middle section stack is
  $u\mapsto\Delta u+u^2$, while the second selects the zero function.
  \Cref{prop:Laplacian_Square_Ordinary_Solutions} says that its ordinary
  solution set is $\{0\}$. The derived pullback retains the way in which the
  operator meets zero.
\end{example}

\subsection{Differential moduli problems}
\label[subsection]{sec:Differential_Moduli_Problems}

The zero-fiber format presupposes a vector-bundle target and a preferred zero
section. Many geometric equations instead compare two differential
constructions which take values in the same nonlinear family. Steffens uses a
symmetric formulation which includes both situations.

\begin{definition}[Differential moduli problem]
  \label[definition]{def:Differential_Moduli_Problem}
  Let $M\to S$ be a family of manifolds. A \emph{differential moduli
  problem} over $M$ is a quintuple
  \[
    \mathcal E=(X,Y_1,Y_2,\widetilde P_1,\widetilde P_2)
  \]
  in which $X,Y_1,Y_2\to M$ are families of submersions and
  \[
    \widetilde P_i\colon J^k_{M/S}(Y_i)\longrightarrow X,
    \qquad i=1,2,
  \]
  are families of differential operators. We write
  \[
    P_i\colon
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y_i)
    \longrightarrow
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)
  \]
  for the induced maps of section stacks.
\end{definition}

Allowing different finite orders would give no additional generality: replace
both orders by their maximum and use the truncation maps between jet stacks.

\begin{definition}[Solution stack]
  \label[definition]{def:Solution_Stack_Differential_Moduli_Problem}
  The \emph{solution stack} of $\mathcal E$ is the pullback
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:General_Solution_Stack_Pullback}
    \operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)
    =
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y_1)
    \times_{\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)}
    \operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y_2).
  \end{equation}
\end{definition}

Thus a $T$-point of $\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)$ is a triple
\[
  (\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\eta)
\]
where $\sigma_i$ is a section of $Y_{i,T}\to M_T$ and
\[
  \eta\colon P_1(\sigma_1)\simeq P_2(\sigma_2)
\]
is a path in the anima of sections of $X_T\to M_T$. The two fields are part
of the moduli problem, and the path is the derived equality between the
values of their equations.

The zero-fiber case is recovered by taking $Y_2=M$, whose section stack is
terminal over $S$, and taking $\widetilde P_2$ to be a chosen section of
$X\to M$. In the running example, that section is zero.

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality of the solution stack]
  \label[proposition]{prop:Solution_Stack_Base_Change_Descent}
  The solution construction has the following properties.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The functor of $T$-families described above is a derived
      $\Cinfty$-stack.
    \item For every map $S'\to S$, there is a natural equivalence
      \[
        S'\times_S\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)
        \simeq
        \operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E_{S'}).
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The three section objects in
  \Cref{eq:General_Solution_Stack_Pullback} are stacks, and stacks are closed
  under pullbacks. This proves the first assertion. For the second, pull the
  defining square back along $S'\to S$. Pullbacks commute with pullbacks, and
  \Cref{prop:Section_Stack_Base_Change} together with
  \Cref{eq:Relative_Jets_Base_Change} identifies the resulting diagram with
  the diagram defining $\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E_{S'})$.
\end{proof}

We have now completed the first two tasks from the opening of the chapter. The
families have been defined over every test object, and descent is built into
the resulting solution stack. No properness or ellipticity hypothesis was
needed. Those hypotheses concern the local geometry and representability of
the stack, not its existence.

\section{Linearization and ellipticity}
\label[section]{sec:Solution_Stack_Linearization_Ellipticity}

\subsection{The relative tangent complex}
\label[subsection]{sec:Relative_Tangent_Complex_Solution_Stack}

Let $s\in S$ and let
\[
  \sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\eta)
\]
be a solution over $s$. Write $\sigma_X$ for the common output section of
$X_s\to M_s$, where the path $\eta$ is used to identify the two outputs.
By \Cref{eq:Relative_Tangent_Section_Stack}, the relative tangent spaces of
the three section stacks are
\begin{align*}
  T_{\sigma_i}\bigl(\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y_i)/S\bigr)
  &\cong
  \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_i^*T_{Y_{i,s}/M_s}\bigr),\\
  T_{\sigma_X}\bigl(\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)/S\bigr)
  &\cong
  \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_X^*T_{X_s/M_s}\bigr).
\end{align*}

The maps $P_i$ linearize to finite-order linear differential operators
\[
  D_{\sigma_i}P_i\colon
  \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_i^*T_{Y_{i,s}/M_s}\bigr)
  \longrightarrow
  \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_X^*T_{X_s/M_s}\bigr).
\]
The chosen path $\eta$ supplies the identification needed to regard their
targets as the same vector space.

\begin{theorem}[Tangent complex of a solution stack]
  \label[theorem]{thm:Tangent_Complex_Solution_Stack}
  With notation as above, the relative tangent complex of the solution stack
  over $S$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:Tangent_Complex_Solution_Stack}
    \mathbb T_\sigma\bigl(\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)/S\bigr)
    \simeq
    \left[
      \begin{matrix}
        \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_1^*T_{Y_{1,s}/M_s}\bigr)\\
        {}\oplus
        \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_2^*T_{Y_{2,s}/M_s}\bigr)
      \end{matrix}
      \xrightarrow{D_{\sigma_1}P_1-D_{\sigma_2}P_2}
      \Gamma\bigl(M_s,\sigma_X^*T_{X_s/M_s}\bigr)
    \right]
  \end{equation}
  in cohomological degrees $0$ and $1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The solution stack is the pullback in
  \Cref{eq:General_Solution_Stack_Pullback}. The exactness of tangent objects
  for pullbacks is formal in derived geometry. Talk~6 calculates its
  finite-dimensional instance; the same relative pullback formula applies to
  the smooth section-stack charts used here. It gives the
  two-term complex
  \[
    \left[
      T_{\sigma_1}\bigl(\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y_1)/S\bigr)
      \oplus
      T_{\sigma_2}\bigl(\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y_2)/S\bigr)
      \longrightarrow
      T_{\sigma_X}\bigl(\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)/S\bigr)
    \right].
  \]
  Substituting \Cref{eq:Relative_Tangent_Section_Stack} gives
  \Cref{eq:Tangent_Complex_Solution_Stack}. The differential is the
  difference of the two derivatives because the pullback imposes equality of
  the two outputs.
\end{proof}

Consequently,
\begin{align*}
  H^0\mathbb T_\sigma\bigl(\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)/S\bigr)
  &=\ker(D_{\sigma_1}P_1-D_{\sigma_2}P_2),\\
  H^1\mathbb T_\sigma\bigl(\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)/S\bigr)
  &=\operatorname{coker}(D_{\sigma_1}P_1-D_{\sigma_2}P_2).
\end{align*}
These are the infinitesimal deformation and obstruction spaces. The chain
groups in \Cref{eq:Tangent_Complex_Solution_Stack} are spaces of smooth
sections and are generally infinite-dimensional. At this stage, there is no
claim that the complex is perfect.

\subsection{Returning to the one-point solution stack}
\label[subsection]{sec:Return_One_Point_Solution_Stack}

For a zero equation, the second section stack is terminal and contributes no
tangent directions. Hence the tangent complex reduces to
\[
  [T_u\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)\xrightarrow{D_uP}
    T_0\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(X)].
\]
Applying this to $P(u)=\Delta u+u^2$ at its unique solution gives
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Tangent_Complex_Laplacian_Square_Solution}
  \mathbb T_0\operatorname{Sol}(P)
  \simeq
  [C^\infty(M)\xrightarrow{\Delta}C^\infty(M)].
\end{equation}
Therefore
\[
  H^0\mathbb T_0\operatorname{Sol}(P)\cong\R,
  \qquad
  H^1\mathbb T_0\operatorname{Sol}(P)\cong\R.
\]

This answers \Cref{question:One_Point_Remembers_Laplacian_Kernel_Cokernel}.
The ordinary solution space sees one point. The derived solution stack sees
the kernel and cokernel of the linearized equation at that point. It is the
infinite-dimensional counterpart of the derived zero locus of
$x\mapsto x^2$, whose tangent complex at zero is
\[
  [\R\xrightarrow{0}\R].
\]
Talk 11 will make this comparison stronger: a finite-dimensional reduction of
the running equation is locally the derived zero locus of the one-variable map
$a\mapsto a^2$ itself.

\subsection{Ellipticity as the analytic handoff}
\label[subsection]{sec:Ellipticity_Analytic_Handoff}

The map in the tangent complex is the point at which analysis enters.

\begin{definition}[Elliptic differential moduli problem]
  \label[definition]{def:Elliptic_Differential_Moduli_Problem}
  A differential moduli problem $\mathcal E$ is \emph{elliptic} if, for every
  point $s\to S$ and every solution
  $\sigma=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\eta)$ over $s$, the linear differential
  operator
  \[
    D_{\sigma_1}P_1-D_{\sigma_2}P_2
  \]
  in \Cref{eq:Tangent_Complex_Solution_Stack} is elliptic on $M_s$.
\end{definition}

Equivalently, its principal symbol is invertible away from the zero section of
$T^*M_s$. The definition concerns the linearization at every solution. The
original operator may be nonlinear.

For the running example,
\[
  D_uP=\Delta+2u.
\]
Multiplication by $2u$ has order zero, so it does not affect the principal
symbol. Every linearization has the symbol $|\xi|^2$ and is elliptic.

The hypotheses which will appear in the representability theorem have
different roles.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Finite order makes the nonlinear operator factor through a relative
    jet stack and hence act functorially on families.
  \item Ellipticity makes each linearization Fredholm after suitable analytic
    completion.
  \item Properness of $M\to S$ supplies compact fibers. Together with
    ellipticity, it gives finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel.
  \item None of these hypotheses is needed merely to form the solution
    pullback.
\end{enumerate}

The chapter ends at the boundary between geometry and analysis:
\[
  \begin{gathered}
    \text{finite-order equation}
    \longrightarrow
    \text{map of section stacks}
    \longrightarrow
    \text{derived solution pullback}\\
    {}\longrightarrow
    \text{elliptic deformation-obstruction complex}.
  \end{gathered}
\]
Talk 11 will choose analytic completions, isolate a finite-dimensional
obstruction space, and apply the implicit function theorem. The result will
be a finite-dimensional Kuranishi model with the same tangent complex.

\section{Summary and supplementary materials}
\label[section]{sec:Section_Stacks_Summary_Supplementary_Materials}

\subsection{Takeaways}
\label[subsection]{sec:Section_Jet_Solution_Takeaways}

The live route can be compressed into eight statements.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A $T$-point of $\operatorname{Sect}_{M/S}(Y)$ is a section of
    $Y_T\to M_T$.
  \item Section stacks commute with base change and already satisfy descent.
  \item A relative jet remembers derivatives in the fibers of $M\to S$ and
    ignores parameter directions.
  \item A finite-order differential operator is a map from a relative jet
    stack and therefore induces a map of section stacks.
  \item A solution is imposed by a derived equality between the values of two
    differential operators.
  \item The solution stack exists before any representability theorem has
    been proved.
  \item Its relative tangent complex is the two-term complex obtained by
    linearizing the equation.
  \item Ellipticity and properness make the cohomology of this complex
    finite-dimensional and prepare the finite-dimensional reduction.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Supplement: the full relative-jet package}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Full_Relative_Jet_Package_Talk_Ten}

Steffens's Proposition~3.3.9 states five properties of
$J^k_{M/S}$. It preserves limits, commutes with arbitrary base change,
preserves open restrictions of the target, recovers classical relative jet
bundles for ordinary families, and carries families of submersions to smooth
objects with submersive truncation maps
\[
  J^k_{M/S}(Y)\longrightarrow J^{k-1}_{M/S}(Y).
\]
The construction over a stacky base is obtained by descent from the
infinitesimal neighborhood of the relative diagonal. These facts justify all
restrictions and changes of parameters used in the live route, but their
proof is independent of the solution-stack calculation.

\subsection{Supplement: the vector-bundle tower of jets}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Vector_Bundle_Tower_Jets}

The structural statement in
\Cref{prop:Relative_Jet_Properties} has a familiar concrete form for vector
bundles. Suppose that $M\to S$ is a submersion of manifolds with
$n$-dimensional fibers and that $E\to M$ is a vector bundle of rank $r$.
Then $J^k_{M/S}(E)\to M$ is a vector bundle of rank
\[
  r\binom{n+k}{k}.
\]
For $k\geq1$, it fits into a short exact sequence
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:Relative_Jet_Vector_Bundle_Exact_Sequence}
  0\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Sym}^k(T^*_{M/S})\otimes E
  \longrightarrow
  J^k_{M/S}(E)
  \longrightarrow
  J^{k-1}_{M/S}(E)
  \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The quotient map forgets the top derivative. Its kernel is the symmetric
$k$-linear part of the Taylor expansion. A connection splits
\Cref{eq:Relative_Jet_Vector_Bundle_Exact_Sequence}, but the sequence itself
is canonical.

For a general submersion $Y\to M$, the map
\[
  J^k_{M/S}(Y)\longrightarrow J^{k-1}_{M/S}(Y)
\]
is an affine analogue of this vector-bundle projection. Steffens proves the
submersiveness needed for local chart arguments. A more precise torsor
description is discussed in Remark~3.3.14 of
\cite{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}; the paper explicitly does
not supply all of the technology for a full proof of that refinement.

\subsection{Supplement: what the path in a solution controls}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Path_In_Solution_Tangent_Complex}

For a point
\[
  (\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\eta)
  \in\operatorname{Sol}(\mathcal E)(T),
\]
the two output sections need not be literally equal. They are joined by a
path in the mapping anima. Applying the tangent construction to this path
identifies the tangent objects at its endpoints. Only after making this
identification can one form
\[
  D_{\sigma_1}P_1-D_{\sigma_2}P_2.
\]
This is why the path is part of the point of the pullback rather than a
dispensable witness. In the classical zero-fiber example, the mapping anima
is discrete and the identification is invisible.

The same phenomenon already occurs in any pullback $X\times_ZY$ in an
$\infty$-category. A point consists of $x$, $y$, and a path between their
images in $Z$. The tangent complex is formed by transporting along that path
and then taking the difference of the two derivatives.

\subsection{Supplement: definition, smoothness, and representability}
\label[subsection]{sec:Supplement_Definition_Smoothness_Representability_Solution}

Three logically different assertions have appeared.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The section stack exists because pullback in the stack category has a
    right adjoint.
  \item Under compactness and submersion hypotheses, the section stack has
    smooth local charts modeled on spaces of smooth sections.
  \item Under properness and ellipticity hypotheses, the solution stack is
    represented by a quasi-smooth derived $\Cinfty$-scheme locally of finite
    presentation.
\end{enumerate}

Only the first assertion is formal. The second uses local additions and
infinite-dimensional smooth geometry. The third is Steffens's relative
elliptic representability theorem and additionally uses Sobolev completions,
elliptic regularity, finite-dimensional reduction, and the local
representability theorem from Chapter~9. This chapter has used a focused
local consequence of the second assertion, but it has not proved either the
second or third assertion.

The distinction prevents a common circularity. We did not define the solution
stack by first choosing Kuranishi charts. We defined it as a functorial
pullback. The Kuranishi charts constructed later must represent restrictions
of that already-defined stack.

\subsection{Related literature}
\label[subsection]{sec:Section_Jet_Solution_Related_Literature}

The primary source for this chapter is Steffens,
\emph{Representability of Elliptic Moduli Problems in Derived
$C^\infty$-Geometry}. Definition~3.2.1, Example~3.2.3, and Remark~3.2.4 treat
Weil restriction, section stacks, and base change. Proposition~3.2.22 gives
the local section-stack chart used in
\Cref{eq:Relative_Tangent_Section_Stack}
\cite[Definition~3.2.1, Example~3.2.3, Remark~3.2.4, and
Proposition~3.2.22]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

Definition~3.3.7 and Remark~3.3.8 give the infinitesimal-diagonal definition
and universal property of relative jets. Proposition~3.3.9 proves the five
properties quoted in \Cref{prop:Relative_Jet_Properties}, and the construction
immediately before Section~3.4 defines jet prolongation
\cite[Definition~3.3.7, Remark~3.3.8, and
Proposition~3.3.9]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}. Definitions
3.4.1--3.4.2 introduce families of nonlinear PDEs, elliptic differential
moduli problems, and their solution stacks
\cite[Definitions~3.4.1--3.4.2]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

Pardon's \emph{Representability in non-linear elliptic Fredholm analysis}
uses the same central idea: the derived fiber of a nonlinear elliptic operator
has linearized deformation complex
\[
  [\Gamma(E)\longrightarrow\Gamma(F)],
\]
and the geometric work is to prove that this functorial solution object is
locally finite-dimensional. Section~4 applies the philosophy to moduli of
pseudo-holomorphic sections
\cite[Sections~2 and 4]{Pardon_Representability_Elliptic_Fredholm}. Pardon's
article is a research announcement and proof overview. Steffens supplies the
general relative section-stack and jet formalism used here.
