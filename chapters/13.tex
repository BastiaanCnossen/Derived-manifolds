\chapter{Elliptic representability III: the analytic input}
\label[chapter]{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Derived_Charts}

The local Kuranishi criterion reduces representability to an analytic
claim: after adding finitely many auxiliary variables, the equation
must be submersive as a map of smooth stacks. We now choose those
variables from the cokernel of the linearized operator and prove the
required condition for every smooth family of right-hand sides.

The analytic argument uses three results. Elliptic operators on a closed
manifold are Fredholm on suitable Sobolev spaces. The Banach implicit
function theorem turns a surjective Fredholm derivative into a local
finite-dimensional family of solutions. Elliptic regularity identifies
these Sobolev solutions, and their smooth family structure, with the
original smooth-section problem.

Once the analytic condition is established, the augmentation identity
gives a derived chart and identifies its tangent complex. We then
retain parameters and glue the charts to complete the relative
representability theorem. The final calculation returns to
$\Delta u+u^2=0$ and exhibits the multiple point that its solution
stack contains near zero.

\section{Sobolev spaces and the loss of derivatives}

Fix a closed manifold $N$ and a local equation
\[
 \widetilde P\colon Q\subseteq\Gamma(N;F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(N;E)
\]
of order $k$, with a chosen solution $t\in Q$.
The bundles $F,E$ and the open set $Q$ come from
\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models}.
We seek a finite-dimensional space $V$, a map
$\iota\colon V\to\Gamma(N;E)$, and a neighbourhood $U$ of $(t,0)$
on which $G(u,a)=\widetilde P(u)+\iota(a)$ is submersive.
Sobolev completions provide the Banach spaces on which we will first
study this augmented operator.

Choose metrics and a connection on a vector bundle $E$ over $N$.
For a nonnegative integer $\ell$, the Sobolev norm is equivalent to
\[
 \|u\|_{H^\ell}^2
 =\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\int_N|\nabla^ju|^2\,d\mathrm{vol}.
\]
Completing smooth sections gives the Hilbert space $H^\ell(E;N)$.
Its elements have weak derivatives through order $\ell$ in $L^2$;
they need not be smooth. Different geometric choices give equivalent
norms.

The Sobolev embedding theorem implies
\[
 H^{k+\ell}(F;N)\longrightarrow C^k(F;N)
 \quad\text{if }\ell>\tfrac12\dim N.
\]
Thus, at these levels, a section has a well-defined continuous $k$-jet.
If $Q$ was defined by requiring the jet to lie in an open
$W\subseteq J_N^k(F)$, the same requirement defines an open subset
$Q_{k+\ell}\subseteq H^{k+\ell}(F;N)$.

A differential operator of order $k$ uses $k$ derivatives.
Its Sobolev extension therefore has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sobolev_Extension_Nonlinear_Operator}
 \widetilde P_\ell\colon Q_{k+\ell}\longrightarrow H^\ell(E;N).
\end{equation}
For $\ell>\dim N/2$, multiplication and smooth substitution are
continuous operations on $H^\ell$. Applied to the finite jet formula,
these results make \Cref{eq:Sobolev_Extension_Nonlinear_Operator}
a smooth map of Banach manifolds.
We use the compatible construction in
\cite[Lemma~3.4.5]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.

The extensions commute with the inclusions between Sobolev levels.
Moreover,
\[
 \Gamma(N;E)\cong\lim_\ell H^\ell(E;N)
\]
as Fr\'echet spaces, with the smooth-section topology.
The corresponding statement holds for $Q$ and its Sobolev domains.
It is a limit statement in convenient manifolds and hence in smooth
stacks after applying $j_{\mathrm{Con}}$.
It is not asserted to be a limit statement after passage to derived
stacks.

\section{Choosing the auxiliary variables by elliptic theory}

Ellipticity makes the missing directions in the image of the derivative
finite-dimensional. Linear regularity supplies smooth representatives
of those directions, so a single augmentation works at every Sobolev level.

\begin{definition}
\label[definition]{def:Fredholm_Operator}
A bounded linear map between Banach spaces is Fredholm if its kernel
and cokernel are finite-dimensional and its image is closed.
Its index is
$\dim\ker D-\dim\operatorname{coker}D$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Linear elliptic theory]
\label[theorem]{thm:Elliptic_Fredholm_Regularity}
A smooth linear elliptic differential operator of order $k$ on a
closed manifold extends to Fredholm operators
\[
 D_\ell\colon H^{k+\ell}(F;N)\longrightarrow H^\ell(E;N).
\]
Its kernel consists of smooth sections and is independent of $\ell$.
Its cokernel is naturally identified with
$\Gamma(N;E)/D\Gamma(N;F)$, also independently of $\ell$.
If $v$ is a Sobolev section and $Dv$ is smooth, then $v$ is smooth.
\end{theorem}

We quote this standard analytic input, used in the proof of Steffens's
theorem. A basic estimate underlying it is
\[
 \|v\|_{H^{k+\ell}}
 \leq C_\ell\bigl(\|Dv\|_{H^\ell}+\|v\|_{L^2}\bigr).
\]
Ellipticity controls the highest derivatives through $Dv$.
The lower-order term accommodates the kernel.
A parametrix and compactness of the lower Sobolev inclusions give
Fredholmness; regularity gives smoothness of kernel elements.
Applying the same reasoning to the formal adjoint identifies the
cokernel by smooth representatives.

At the chosen solution, put $D=T_t\widetilde P$ and
\[
 V=\operatorname{coker}D
   =\Gamma(N;E)/D\Gamma(N;F).
\]
It is finite-dimensional. Choose a linear splitting
\[
 \iota\colon V\longrightarrow\Gamma(N;E)
\]
of the quotient map.
Concretely, choose a basis of the quotient and one smooth section
representing each basis vector, and extend linearly.
The phrase smooth representatives refers to these sections on $N$.
A map from finite-dimensional $V$ obtained in this way is also smooth
as a map to the Fr\'echet section space.

The augmented derivative is
\[
 D+\iota\colon\Gamma(N;F)\oplus V\longrightarrow\Gamma(N;E).
\]
It is surjective: the image of $\iota$ supplies every missing cokernel
class. The same is true at every Sobolev level by the linear regularity
theorem. Its kernel is finite-dimensional.

For nearby sections we restrict the jet neighbourhood so the principal
symbol remains elliptic. This is possible because invertibility is open
and the cosphere bundle of $N$ is compact. It also keeps the Fredholm
index fixed near the chosen solution.

\section{The augmented operator on Sobolev spaces}

The augmented derivative is surjective Fredholm at the chosen solution.
The Banach implicit function theorem describes its nearby fibres.
We must then choose the neighbourhoods compatibly, so that they return
to a single open set of smooth sections.

The version needed here follows from the Banach inverse function theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Implicit function theorem]
\label[theorem]{thm:Banach_Implicit_Function}
Let $g\colon B\supseteq O\to C$ be smooth between Banach spaces.
Suppose $d_bg$ is surjective and its kernel $K$ has a closed complement.
Then near $b$, there are smooth coordinates in which $g$ is the
projection from an open subset of $K\times C$ to $C$.
\end{theorem}

Choose $B=K\oplus B'$ so that $d_bg|_{B'}$ is an isomorphism.
The bounded inverse theorem makes its inverse bounded.
The map
\[
 x\longmapsto\bigl(\operatorname{pr}_K(x-b),g(x)\bigr)
\]
has an invertible derivative at $b$, so the inverse function theorem
gives the required coordinates.
For a surjective Fredholm operator, the finite-dimensional kernel has
a closed complement. In the Hilbert spaces used here one can take
its orthogonal complement.

Consequently, a smooth map with everywhere surjective Fredholm
derivative has local finite-dimensional fibres.
More generally, pull it back along a smooth map from a finite-dimensional
manifold $Z$. In the local coordinates above, the pullback is an open
subset of $K\times Z$, and its projection to $Z$ is a submersion.
This family statement is exactly the form required in \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}.

For each sufficiently large $\ell$, define
\[
 G_\ell(u,a)=\widetilde P_\ell(u)+\iota(a).
\]
Its derivative at $(t,0)$ is surjective Fredholm.
Surjectivity of a split surjection is open in the operator norm, and
Fredholmness is also open. We can therefore restrict to an open
neighbourhood $U_\ell\subseteq Q_{k+\ell}\times V$ where
$dG_\ell$ is everywhere surjective Fredholm.

The neighbourhoods must be chosen compatibly across Sobolev levels.
At a smooth section, linear elliptic regularity identifies the kernel
and cokernel of the derivative at every level.
Thus the condition that $dG_\ell$ be onto has the same restriction
to smooth sections for every $\ell$. Using these surjectivity loci,
and successively intersecting higher-level neighbourhoods with
the inverse images of lower-level ones, gives
\[
 U_{\ell+1}\longrightarrow U_\ell,\qquad
 U=U_\ell\cap(Q\times V)
\]
with the same smooth open neighbourhood $U$ at every level.
This is the neighbourhood construction in the proof of
\cite[Theorem~3.4.3, p.~69]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
The inverse limit of the resulting domains is $U$ in convenient
manifolds.

The derivative is independent of the auxiliary variable $a$, since
$\iota$ is linear. At the chosen section it has the form $D+\iota$.
The restriction is needed because the derivative in the section variable
changes with $u$.

\section{Testing submersiveness on smooth families}

We now verify the family condition from the preceding chapter. At each
Sobolev level, the implicit function theorem gives a finite-dimensional
pullback. Nonlinear regularity identifies its points with smooth
solutions, and linear regularity identifies its smooth structure.

Let $Z$ be an ordinary finite-dimensional manifold and let
$h\colon Z\to\Gamma(N;E)$ be smooth.
At Sobolev level $\ell$, consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Sobolev_Test_Family}
 W_\ell
 =\{(z,u,a)\in Z\times U_\ell:
       \widetilde P_\ell(u)+\iota(a)=h(z)\}.
\end{equation}
The Banach implicit function theorem makes $W_\ell$ an ordinary
finite-dimensional manifold with a submersion $W_\ell\to Z$.
The dimension of a fibre is the Fredholm index of $G_\ell$, namely
$\operatorname{ind}D+\dim V$.

It remains to identify these manifolds with the smooth-stack pullback.

We use the following nonlinear regularity statement from
\cite[Lemma~3.4.6]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}:
above a sufficiently high Sobolev threshold, a solution of the
nonlinear elliptic equation with smooth right-hand side is smooth.
The threshold includes $\ell>\dim N/2$ and the additional regularity
needed for the nonlinear elliptic bootstrap.
No optimal numerical threshold is required for the argument.

At a point of $W_\ell$ the equation reads
\[
 \widetilde P_\ell(u)=h(z)-\iota(a).
\]
The right side is a smooth section.
For $\ell$ above the regularity threshold, $u$ is therefore smooth.
The common smooth trace of the $U_\ell$ then shows that all
sufficiently high $W_\ell$ have the same underlying set.
Their transition maps are bijections.

This only compares points. A bijective smooth map between manifolds
need not be a diffeomorphism, so one further step is necessary.

At a smooth solution $(z,u,a)$, the tangent space along the fibre
of $W_\ell\to Z$ is
\[
 \ker\bigl(T_u\widetilde P_\ell+\iota\bigr).
\]
If $(v,b)$ lies in this kernel, then
$T_u\widetilde P_\ell(v)=-\iota(b)$ is smooth.
Linear elliptic regularity makes $v$ smooth.
It follows that the inclusions between Sobolev levels induce
isomorphisms on these vertical tangent spaces.

Each $W_\ell\to Z$ is a submersion, and the transition maps are over $Z$.
The short exact sequences of tangent spaces therefore show that
$W_{\ell+1}\to W_\ell$ induces an isomorphism on the full tangent space.
The finite-dimensional inverse function theorem makes it a local
diffeomorphism. Combined with bijectivity, this makes it a diffeomorphism.

Thus the tower is eventually constant as a tower of manifolds.
Write $W$ for its limiting manifold; $W\to Z$ is a submersion.

The Sobolev tower for smooth sections is a limit in convenient manifolds,
and $j_{\mathrm{Con}}$ preserves such limits in smooth stacks.
Since limits commute with pullbacks, the preceding identification gives
\[
 j_{\mathrm{Con}}W
 \cong Z\times_{j_{\mathrm{Con}}\Gamma(N;E)}^{\mathrm{Sm}}
                 j_{\mathrm{Con}}U.
\]
This identifies the entire smooth family functor, including its
smooth dependence on parameters.

The test manifold $Z$ and map $h$ were arbitrary.
By the criterion of \Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge}, the augmented map
$j_{\mathrm{Con}}G$ is submersive.
The comparison theorem then makes it submersive in derived stacks too.
This proves claim $(*)$ in Steffens's argument.

\section{The derived chart and its tangent complex}

The derived zero fibre of $G$ is now an ordinary finite-dimensional
manifold $Z_U$. Its projection onto $V$ gives
$\kappa\colon Z_U\to V$, and the augmentation identity identifies
\[
 Z_U\times_V^{\mathrm{der}}\{0\}
\]
with an open part of $\Sol(\widetilde P)$.

The tangent calculation also follows directly from the augmentation.
At a zero $(u,0)$, put
\[
 K=\ker(T_u\widetilde P+\iota)=T_{(u,0)}Z_U.
\]
The derivative of $\kappa$ is projection $K\to V$.
There is a chain map
\[
 [K\longrightarrow V]\longrightarrow
 [\Gamma(N;F)\xrightarrow{T_u\widetilde P}\Gamma(N;E)]
\]
given by projection onto $\Gamma(N;F)$ in degree zero and $-\iota$
in degree minus one. It is a chain map because
$T_u\widetilde P(v)=-\iota(a)$ for $(v,a)\in K$.
Surjectivity of the augmented derivative makes this a
quasi-isomorphism: its degree-zero homology is the original kernel,
and its degree-minus-one homology is the original cokernel.

Thus the finite-dimensional derived tangent complex is represented
by the linearized elliptic equation. Its virtual dimension is
\[
 \dim Z_U-\dim V=\operatorname{ind}(T_u\widetilde P).
\]
At the centre $t$, our choice $V=\operatorname{coker}D$ implies
$K\cong\ker D$ and $d\kappa=0$.

\section{Relative charts and completion of the proof}

To finish the theorem, we carry out the same analytic construction
with parameters and apply the local representability results. The
relative chart is the analytic output; gluing uses the solution stack
already defined before making any coordinate choices.

For a manifold base, the locally reduced operator is
\[
 (s,u)\longmapsto(s,\widetilde P_s(u)).
\]
At a chosen solution $t=(s_0,u_0)$, use the cokernel of the vertical
operator $T_{u_0}\widetilde P_{s_0}$.
It is also the cokernel of the total derivative: the total derivative
has the block form
\[
 (\dot s,v)\longmapsto
 \bigl(\dot s,\partial_s\widetilde P(\dot s)
                    +T_{u_0}\widetilde P_{s_0}(v)\bigr),
\]
and subtracting the parameter term in the target identifies its
kernel and cokernel with those of the vertical operator.

Choose smooth representatives $\iota$ at this solution and repeat the
Sobolev construction on $S\times Q\times V$.
A test family is now a map
$Z\to S\times\Gamma(N;E)$, written $(s(z),h(z))$.
Its equation is
\[
 \widetilde P_{s(z),\ell}(u)+\iota(a)=h(z).
\]
The Banach argument and both regularity arguments work with these
additional finite-dimensional parameters. They prove that the
augmented operator is submersive over $S$ in the required sense.

\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Representability_Smooth_Derived_Bridge} therefore produces relative Kuranishi charts.
\Cref{chap:Elliptic_Equations_Kuranishi_Models} showed that their neighbourhoods cover all solutions,
and \Cref{chap:Coherence_Descent_Local_Representability} glues this effective open atlas.
For a smooth stack base $S$, choose an effective covering by ordinary
manifolds and pull the differential moduli problem back to them.
The argument above proves relative representability on each such
family. Locality on the base, proved in
\Cref{cor:Local_Representability_Relative}, descends this conclusion
to $S$. The globally defined solution stack supplies the overlaps and
their coherence, so no independent choices of chart transitions are
needed. This completes the proof of
\Cref{thm:Relative_Elliptic_Representability}, subject to the
analytic and geometric input theorems explicitly quoted above.

\section{The running example as a multiple point}

Return to $P(u)=\Delta u+u^2$ on closed connected $N$.
At zero, the cokernel of $\Delta$ is represented by constants.
Take $V=\R$ and $\iota(a)=a$. The augmented equation is
\[
 \Delta u+u^2+a=0.
\]
Its derivative at $(0,0)$ is $(v,b)\mapsto\Delta v+b$, which is
surjective.

Write $u=c+w$, where $c$ is constant and $w$ has mean zero.
Let $\langle g\rangle$ denote the average of a function over $N$.
The mean-zero part of the augmented equation is
\[
 \Delta w+2cw+w^2-\langle w^2\rangle=0.
\]
On mean-zero functions, $\Delta$ is an isomorphism between the relevant
Sobolev spaces. The implicit function theorem, with parameter $c$,
gives a unique small solution $w=w(c)$.
Since $w=0$ solves the equation for every $c$, uniqueness implies
$w(c)=0$.

The remaining average equation is $a=-c^2$.
Hence near zero the augmented solution manifold is the curve
\[
 c\longmapsto(u=c,\ a=-c^2).
\]
Its Kuranishi map is $\kappa(c)=-c^2$.
Multiplying the target by $-1$ identifies its derived zero locus
with that of $c^2$.

The local function ring is therefore
\[
 \Cinfty(\R)/(c^2)\cong\R[\delta]/(\delta^2).
\]
The real solution set is one point, but it has a nontrivial infinitesimal
direction. Its tangent complex is $[\R\xrightarrow0\R]$ in degrees
$0,-1$, agreeing with the kernel and cokernel of $\Delta$.
This is the finite-dimensional singularity encoded by the original
infinite-dimensional equation.

\section{Further reading}

The construction used auxiliary sections and Sobolev completions.
We explain how these choices affect the presentations, and distinguish
the representability theorem from the further compactness questions
needed for enumerative applications.

The auxiliary space is not unique. Any finite-dimensional collection
of smooth sections surjecting onto the cokernel can be used.
Adding unnecessary representatives increases the dimension of the
augmented zero manifold and the number of auxiliary equations together.
The resulting presentations still describe open parts of the same
intrinsic solution stack.

A change of Sobolev exponent is also auxiliary. The regularity argument
identifies the resulting finite-dimensional manifolds at sufficiently
high levels and compares their universal smooth families.
This is stronger than observing that their real solution sets agree.
It explains why the theorem belongs to the original smooth equation,
rather than to a chosen Banach completion.

The analytic input proves representability and quasi-smoothness.
Compactness of the domain $N$ does not imply compactness of its
solution moduli object. Compactifying that object and constructing
enumerative invariants require further geometric arguments.

\begin{exercise}
For a Fredholm map $D$ and a cokernel splitting $\iota$, verify directly
that $\ker(D+\iota)\cong\ker D$.
Then replace $V$ by a larger auxiliary space and calculate the change
in the kernel dimension.
\end{exercise}

\begin{exercise}
In the running example, replace $u^2$ by $u^3$.
Use the mean-zero equation to compute the local Kuranishi map.
Compare the resulting classical function ring and tangent complex.
\end{exercise}

The detailed analytic construction is
\cite[Lemmas~3.4.5--3.4.6 and the proof of Theorem~3.4.3,
pp.~67--70]{Steffens_Representability_Elliptic_Moduli}.
The distinction between limits in smooth stacks and derived stacks
is discussed in its Remark 3.4.7.
